\paragraph{Budget-matched view.} recall@15 is not a budget-fair comparison: SyGMa reaches 0.554 by emitting $\sim$74 predictions per substrate, whereas GRAIL reaches 0.365 at $\sim$8.7. Normalising by output --- precision, recall per prediction --- reorders the board: SyGMa's precision is \textbf{0.029}, roughly 4$\times$ below the low-output methods (GRAIL \textbf{0.109}, BioTransformer 0.127, MetaTrans 0.122, MetaPredictor 0.116). Precision is a pessimistic lower bound under incomplete annotation (as noted in the Methods section), but the $\sim$7$\times$ output-volume gap it reflects is real. At a matched small budget (recall@5) the recall spread also compresses (SyGMa 0.465 $\approx$ MetaPredictor 0.470; GRAIL 0.321). This corroborates the independent SyGMa-overproduction finding of \citet{Boyce_2022} (as discussed in the related-work section): SyGMa's coverage is bought with a large prediction volume, which the budget-matched view controls for.

\paragraph{Budget-matched frontier and cross-method decomposition (we measure our own instrument first).} Table~\ref{tab:budget-matched-leaderboard}'s cut-offs already place GRAIL below SyGMa at every $k$; a fine-grained frontier confirms this is not an artifact of the $k=15$ operating point. Ranking each method's own candidate pool and reading recall@$k$ for $k = 1, \ldots, 64$ on the shared substrates (\texttt{results/budget\_matched\_frontier.json}, tautomer), \textbf{SyGMa dominates GRAIL at every budget with no crossover} --- from $k=1$ (\textbf{0.233 vs 0.127}) through $k=64$ (\textbf{0.520 vs 0.410}). GRAIL does \emph{not} win at a matched output budget; its learned selector/ranker is the binding weakness (Proposition 1), which the budget-matched view isolates from the output-volume confound. The mechanism is visible when recall is decomposed \textbf{per method}, not only for GRAIL (\texttt{results/cross\_method\_decomposition.json}, shared $n=150$): SyGMa applies its whole applicable rule set and emits it, so its \textbf{selection retention is 1.0 by construction} --- it has no selection stage (precision 0.076 at $\sim$64 outputs) and its recall is simply its bank's realised coverage, \textbf{0.520}. GRAIL's rule bank in fact covers \textbf{more} of these transformations --- bank ceiling \textbf{0.701} on this subset (cf. the 0.735 full-test ceiling, the coverage-ceiling section) $>$ SyGMa's realised 0.520 --- yet GRAIL realises only recall@15 0.318 here because its selection + ranking retains just \textbf{0.454} of that reachable coverage. The gap to SyGMa is therefore not a worse rule bank; it is a selection tax that recall charges only to a method that \emph{has} a selection stage --- a tax GRAIL pays and loses. We report this because a benchmark whose authors run their own pipeline through their own instrument, and find it loses at every budget, is more trustworthy than one whose method wins.
