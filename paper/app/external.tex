\subsection{Internal and external coverage ceilings}

The internal coverage ceiling is $\ceiling$, with a cluster-bootstrap 95\% confidence interval of
$[0.709,0.762]$ over 10{,}000 resamples of substrates. It is a micro estimate, the pooled ratio
of summed hits to summed references rather than a mean over per-substrate ratios: pairs within a
substrate are dependent, so a mean of ratios would misrepresent the uncertainty.

Recomputing the same quantity on an external set requires the same procedure throughout: the 37
GLORYx parent substrates, one uncapped depth-1 pass over the full bank, no cap on the candidate
pool, and the same tautomer-aware match. Under those conditions the external ceiling is 0.633,
with a 95\% interval of $[0.531,0.733]$, wide by design at this sample size.

An earlier figure of 0.3715 has circulated for the external ceiling. It was measured with a
capped candidate pool and reflects the generator budget of that evaluation rather than the
reach of the rule bank, so it is not comparable to the internal ceiling and should not be read
as an external ceiling.

\subsection{The criterion is implementation-dependent}

Tautomer canonicalisation is not a mathematical operation on a molecule but a search with a
budget. On the external references the enumerator reaches a canonical form for 191 of 196 unique
metabolites and exhausts its transform limit on the remaining 5, or 2.6\%, which are left at
whatever form the search stopped on. The canonical tautomer is also not stable across releases of
the toolkit. Neither fact undermines the comparisons here, since every method is mapped through the
same implementation at the same version, but both are reasons to report the implementation and its
version alongside the criterion, which is the practice this protocol argues for.

\subsection{Composition and the internal--external gap}

Both ceilings above are micro estimates. A separate question is whether the gap between them
reflects the composition of the two populations: GLORYx parents are on average larger and more
conjugated than the internal test substrates, and the spread of mass differences across the
uncovered set reported in the main text suggests coverage should fall with molecular complexity.

Regressing per-substrate coverage on molecular descriptors (molecular weight, ring count,
aromatic-atom count, heteroatom count, a count of conjugation sites, and the number of reference
metabolites) across both populations predicts macro coverages of about 0.79 internally and
0.74 externally in sample. These are macro quantities, per-substrate means, and are not directly
comparable to the micro ceilings above. The in-sample external prediction of 0.74 sits above the
measured external macro coverage of 0.697, so the in-sample fit overstates transferability.

Holding the external substrates out of the fit entirely, and predicting them from an
internal-only model, gives 0.738 out of sample and recovers 56\% of the gap in macro
coverage between the two populations. That out-of-sample figure, not the in-sample one, is the
quantity we report. It is a point estimate from six predictors fit on 37 external points and
carries no interval; it indicates a direction, not a magnitude that would transfer.

\subsection{Interpretation}

At $n=37$ this is a suggestive partial composition effect. It is consistent with molecular
composition accounting for part of the gap between internal and external coverage, and it is not
a fitted law that transfers to other populations. It does not indicate a defect in the rule bank
or in the matching protocol.
