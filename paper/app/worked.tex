A single pair of predictions makes the dependence on the matching convention concrete.

Suppose a method predicts D-alanine and the enol tautomer of acetone, and the references are
L-alanine and the keto tautomer of acetone. Under strict \textsc{inchikey} matching both
predictions miss: the alanine pair differs at a stereocentre, and the acetone pair differs by a
keto--enol tautomerisation, which lies outside what plain InChI normalises, so it misses under
stereo-blind matching as well.

Tautomer-aware matching canonicalises tautomers on both sides and generates its canonical SMILES
without stereochemistry, so it closes both gaps at once. The acetone pair becomes a hit because
the tautomers collapse, and the alanine pair becomes a hit because the stereocentre is no longer
distinguished. The same predictions score full recall.

Nothing about the chemistry changed between the two readings. The same fixed predictions score as
a near-total miss or as a perfect result depending only on which convention is applied --- the
effect the main text quantifies across five methods and an external set.
