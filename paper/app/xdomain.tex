\subsection{GRAIL does not win on recall}

GRAIL loses on recall everywhere in this paper. Against SyGMa the margin is significant and separated: a paired difference of $-0.242$ in mean per-substrate recall, 95\%
confidence interval $[-0.271,-0.212]$. A McNemar test on a different endpoint, whether each
substrate yields any hit at all within the top 15, rejects at $p\approx1.7\times10^{-44}$; it
corroborates the direction, not testing the margin. It also loses to the learned transformer baselines
on the shared subset of $\nshared$ substrates, where MetaPredictor reaches 0.585 and MetaTrans
0.561 against GRAIL's 0.365 under the same tautomer-aware protocol. Nothing here should be read
as a claim to the state of the art. What the paper contributes is the coverage ceiling, the
decomposition, and the matching protocol; GRAIL is one diagnosed row within them.

\subsection{Precision understates performance}

Every precision number in this paper is a lower bound. Negatives are rule-applicable products
that carry no annotation rather than confirmed non-metabolites, so an unannotated true metabolite
counts as a false positive, and the size of that understatement is unknown. We lead with recall and
with the mean size of the output set for that reason. Precision and F1 nonetheless appear in
\S\ref{sec:metric}, because under a uniform annotation propensity the understatement is a common
factor that leaves the precision ordering identified, and the F1 ordering survives correction down
to an annotation rate the references make implausible.

\subsection{The recommended criterion cannot penalise a limitation of our own system}

The default criterion disregards stereochemistry, and GRAIL's own standardisation writes SMILES
without stereochemistry, so it never emits a stereocentre. Under a stereo-sensitive criterion GRAIL
would be scored on a representation it cannot produce; under the default it is not. On the external
set this is the whole of the difference, and it is large: on the thirteen drugs GRAIL has not
trained on, it rises from $0.059$ to $0.185$ when stereochemistry is disregarded, against a further
$0.038$ from tautomer canonicalisation. (The corresponding figures on all $37$, $0.155$ to $0.310$,
are inflated by training overlap and are superseded below.) GRAIL's external figure is therefore conditional on a tolerance that happens to suit its output
convention. The comparisons in this paper remain valid, because every method is scored
under the same criterion and all three rungs are reported, but a system that does emit
stereochemistry is not rewarded for doing so anywhere in this evaluation.

\subsection{The population is undeclared, and does not survive being tested}
\label{app:popaxis}

Two of the four choices this paper names move an ordering and a third moves a reported quantity.
The fourth is the one we could not show does either, and separating those statements is the whole
point of testing it rather than asserting it from the others.

That it is undeclared is not in doubt. Eleven released files sit on three test sets sharing about a
tenth of their reactions (\S\ref{app:xdomainsets}), our own copy of a familiar name is one of the
deviations, and fifteen products that appear in two of those files carry a \emph{different} recorded
reaction in each: a disagreement about the answer and not merely about the sample. A number
quoted against one of those names cannot be read without knowing which set produced it.

Whether it \emph{reorders} is a separate question with an estimand, and the estimand needs care. The
smaller population is nested inside the larger, so a gap measured on the subset and a gap measured
on the whole are not independent and their difference carries no honest interval. The complement, what the larger population has and the subset does not, is disjoint from the subset, so the
contrast between subset and complement does. Reorderings below are reported against the whole population, which is the quantity a published table states; interactions are tested against the complement, which is the contrast that admits an interval.

Both instances are run this way. On our split the same three methods are scored under the same five
criteria by the same estimator on the $\nshared$ substrates all five methods share and on the full
$\ntest$: of $60$ comparisons $10$ reorder, and they are not scattered: MetaPredictor against
SyGMa on recall reverses under every one of the five criteria, ahead by about $0.02$ on the shared
subset and behind by about $0.02$ on the full split, with precision reordering four and F1 one. On
the released retrosynthesis files the two clusters share $505$ products, $490$ of which agree on the
recorded reaction; those $490$ are nested inside each cluster's own five thousand, giving $380$
comparisons of which $44$ reorder.

\textbf{Not one of the $440$ interactions survives Holm at $0.05$, and on our split not one has an
interval excluding zero even before correction.}

\paragraph{What the null could have detected.} A non-detection is a statement about the design as
much as about the data, so the size it would have caught is computed rather than asserted. For each
interaction the paired bootstrap already yields a sampling distribution; its standard deviation
$\hat\sigma$ is the standard error of that interaction, estimated from the same resamples that give
the interval and requiring no variance model beyond them. The two populations enter the contrast
through disjoint sets of items, the shared subset and the complement, so the resampling is
independent across them and no inter-arm correlation has to be assumed; where the two overlap, as
the subset does with the whole, the contrast is not used. A two-sided test at the family's own Holm
threshold rejects when the estimate exceeds $z_{1-\alpha/2m}\,\hat\sigma$ with $\alpha=0.05$ and $m$
the family size, and reaches power $1-\beta$ against an effect of
\[
\mathrm{MDE} \;=\; \bigl(z_{1-\alpha/2m} + z_{1-\beta}\bigr)\,\hat\sigma ,
\]
evaluated at $\beta=0.2$. With $m=380$ on the released files the critical quantile is $z=3.823$, and
with $m=60$ on our split $z=3.341$. The median test in each family then detects $0.100$ and $0.066$,
and the least sensitive $0.116$ and $0.202$. The median describes a typical comparison and the
maximum is what the family-wide statement needs, since a claim that no interaction of a given size is
present anywhere must hold at the blindest test in the family, so the bound this appendix asserts is
the second pair. Against the criterion differentials that survive
correction, $0.024$ internally and $0.120$ on the shared subset, the first bound is nearly five
times the smaller and the second is of the same order as the larger. So these designs exclude an
effect several times the size of the criterion's smaller interaction and are silent about one the
size of its largest. That is the sense in which the null is bounded, and it is the only sense
claimed. On the released files $18$ of $380$ have intervals
excluding zero, which is about what $380$ tests produce by chance. The reorderings are what a subset
of four hundred and ninety reactions, or a hundred and fifty substrates, does on its own.

So the axis is reported as a hazard to declaration and not as a lever on ranking. Two consequences follow. Reorderings obtained by re-scoring on a subset are not evidence that the population matters, although they resemble such evidence; and the criterion result is not
explained away by the same mechanism, since it was measured with a certified interaction on the same
estimator and $109$ of its $448$ tests survive the same correction.

The shared reactions do settle one thing no released table can. The three clusters have no system in
common, so no published number compares a system in one against a system in another. On the $490$
they share, all ten are comparable at once: \texttt{gln} leads at $0.574$ top-1 under canonical
matching, ahead of \texttt{chemformer} at $0.567$ and \texttt{gta} at $0.551$. On its own cluster's
five thousand reactions \texttt{gln} is fifth of seven at $0.525$. We state this ordering and
decline to defend it, for exactly the reason above: it is measured on four hundred and ninety
reactions, and nothing here separates it from the same noise.

\subsection{The multiplicity correction, and the family it is taken over}
\label{app:holm}

\S\ref{sec:rankflip} corrects for multiplicity post hoc, and both inputs to that correction belong
here, not in a sentence. The p-values are normal approximations to the stored paired-bootstrap
intervals, $\mathrm{se}=(\mathrm{hi}-\mathrm{lo})/2z$, and the external decisions do not depend on
that approximation. Recomputed from the per-substrate vectors by flipping the sign of each
substrate's difference-of-differences, which assumes nothing about the distribution, the same four
interactions are rejected, and they are rejected in a family of twelve rather than six, since the
sign-flip run tests both criterion steps rather than the one the table reports. The approximation
does fail, and where it fails is instructive: three comparisons on the tautomer step move both
methods by exactly the same amount on every substrate, so their bootstrap interval has zero width,
and dividing by it turns the least informative comparison in the set into the most significant one.
The sign-flip test returns $p=1$ for all three, which is what a contrast carrying no evidence about
its own sign deserves. None of the three enters any family this paper declares. The intervals
themselves remain the primary
evidence and the approximation is adequate only because most decisions sit far from their threshold: the one that does not is stated as such below.

We declare the family conservatively as the grid the two axes admit rather than the comparisons we
report; no family was pre-specified. The two axes are method pairs and criterion steps, and two steps are reported on each set:
canonical$\rightarrow$tautomer and canonical$\rightarrow$InChIKey internally,
InChIKey$\rightarrow$no-stereo and no-stereo$\rightarrow$tautomer externally. Six pairs times two
steps is a family of twelve externally; ten pairs times two steps is twenty internally. Taking the
family as the pairs actually tested, or as the pairs one table admits, counts only one of the two
axes.

\subsection{The rank-flip result, and the run that decides whether it leaves metabolism}
\label{app:xdomainflip}

The test was specified before it could be run, and specified so that it could fail: re-score the
frozen predictions of at least two comparable published models in another domain under the same
conventions, and if the ordering does not move, with an interaction interval covering zero, the
broader claim fails.

That run needs at least two models of comparable strength whose match sensitivity differs and
whose raw ranked predictions are public. Such predictions are public: the EvalRetro benchmark
releases the ranked outputs of twelve single-step retrosynthesis systems on what it presents as the
USPTO-50k test set \citep{Hastedt_2024}, eleven of them as CSV, under a licence that permits
re-scoring. Nothing needs training, and no checkpoint needs to run.

Two qualifications travel with it. The effect is concentrated at top-1: at $k=3$, $5$ and $10$ the

leaderboard; but the reordering is a top-1 phenomenon on this benchmark, not a property of the whole
accuracy curve.

\paragraph{The second group does not reorder, and says why the first one does.} The same run on the
three-system group is a replication we did not pre-register, and it comes back negative for
reordering: one ordering under all four criteria, no pair exchanged. It is not negative about the
criterion. Fifteen of its $66$ paired interactions survive Holm, so the criterion moves those systems
by certifiably different amounts too, and by almost exactly the same amounts as in the seven, a
largest differential of $0.013$ against $0.015$.

What differs is how tightly the leaderboard is packed, and that, not the criterion, is
what decides the outcome.

\paragraph{The inequality is arithmetic; how often it is met is not.} A criterion reverses a pair
exactly when it moves the two apart by more than the gap between them, since a difference changes
sign only if the change exceeds it. That needs no evidence. What needs evidence is whether real
leaderboards are packed tightly enough for it to happen, which is a fact about how a field's methods
converge. Both quantities are already computed for every leaderboard in this paper, so we compare
them on all of them ($562$ method-pair by criterion-pair comparisons across four domains, with
nothing re-scored:

survives the movement, on any of the $562$, which is the arithmetic holding and is checked rather
than assumed.

The distribution across leaderboards is the finding. Docking and the seven-system retrosynthesis
group each supply $28$ of the $76$ for opposite reasons, one because the criterion moves programs
by a fifth of their range and the other because its systems sit a hundredth apart; the three-system group, whose members sit three and a half times further
apart while the criterion moves them just as much, supplies none. Generation contributes $16$ from
$277$ comparisons because its median movement is $0.0001$, the criterion has almost no purchase
there, and the external metabolite set contributes $4$ from $18$ for the opposite reason, a
movement of $0.036$ large enough to cross even gaps of $0.13$. The reading is uncomfortable rather
than reassuring: a leaderboard becomes vulnerable to an undeclared convention exactly as it matures
and its entries converge.

Two earlier measurements outside metabolism come back negative, and they are negative about
different things. The first carries a leaderboard: it scores eight generative models and can
therefore exhibit an exchange or fail to. The second carries one model, and one model has no
ordering to disturb, so it cannot test reordering at all: what it measures is how far the
convention moves a score in that domain, which is an upper bound on what any pair separated by more
than that could suffer, and a bound is not an observation. We keep it for the bound and label it as
one.

The first targets molecular generation, where the matching criterion is not a way of checking an
answer but the metric itself: MOSES reports Unique@$k$ as the count of distinct canonical SMILES
among a model's generations \citep{Polykovskiy_2020}. A model that emits a keto and an enol form of
one compound is credited with two molecules. We re-scored the published generations of all eight
models MOSES ships samples for, 240{,}208 molecules, under the same five criteria.

The criterion's effect here is certified across a family of $112$ interactions and it is entirely stereochemical. Of $112$ paired
interactions, every pair of models against every criterion step, $22$ have intervals excluding
zero and $19$ survive Holm at $0.05$ across the family, and \textbf{all nineteen are the step from
canonical to stereo-blind}. Not one interaction survives under InChIKey, Tanimoto or tautomer
matching. Tautomer canonicalisation is inert to four decimals on every one of the eight, the single
non-zero difference being JT-VAE's $0.9992$ against $0.9991$.

What stereo-blindness does is visible per model and explains itself. The hidden Markov baseline
falls $0.9226 \to 0.8884$ and the $n$-gram baseline $0.9856 \to 0.9565$; LatentGAN falls
$0.9975 \to 0.9880$; and the combinatorial baseline moves by exactly $0.00000$
$[0.00000,0.00000]$, because it enumerates without stereocentres and a stereo-blind rule has nothing
of its to collapse. A criterion that is inert on one model by construction and costs another a
point is a differential effect in the strict sense, and it produces the one rank exchange this set
admits: LatentGAN past the combinatorial baseline, an interaction of $-0.0093$
$[-0.0109,-0.0076]$ that survives the same correction, swapping positions 5 and 6 under stereo-blind
matching and under no other criterion.

One exchange from a corrected effect is the packing condition rather than a weak result. Seven of
the eight sit at $0.986$ or above, so almost every pair is separated by less than the effect can
close, and the pair that is not, exchanges. This is the same asymmetry the external metabolite
set shows, stereochemistry doing the work and tautomerism none; what differs between the domains is
not whether the criterion acts but how much of the leaderboard is close enough to feel it.

The second targets retrosynthesis. Scoring ReactionT5v2 on 200
substrates from the USPTO-50k test set this repository holds, which is the three-system
group's, not the seven-system one scored above, the same predictions move only about 0.02 in top-1
accuracy across canonical, stereo-stripped, InChIKey and tautomer matching, between 0.695 and
0.715, roughly an order of magnitude less than the interaction we measure in metabolism. A
plausible reason is that retrosynthesis references are canonical, clean small molecules, whereas a
metabolism rule engine emits tautomer and stereochemical variants of its references. What this
licenses is narrow, and the seven-system result above says how narrow. A leaderboard reorders when
the convention moves two methods by \emph{different} amounts, and one model cannot show a
differential. One model's small movement might be read as a bound on what a pair could
suffer, and that reading is arithmetically true and practically empty: the movement here is about
$0.02$, the seven published systems sit within $0.005$ of one another, and five pairs exchange. A
bound larger than the gaps it is supposed to protect protects nothing.

\paragraph{What the three measurements say together.} Matching conventions do not reorder every
leaderboard, and the condition is a property of the references rather than of the task, but it is
narrower than the bare statement suggests. The effect needs references carrying representational variety
\emph{of a kind the compared systems differ on}. Generative models trained on canonical ZINC SMILES
reproduce that canonical form, so there is nothing to diverge from and the ordering barely moves.
Retrosynthesis references are canonical, which is why the earlier single-model probe found so
little, but they carry stereochemistry and the systems differ in reproducing it, and that alone
reorders the top-1 table. Metabolite references carry tautomeric \emph{and} stereochemical variety,
written with whatever the reporting assay produced, and there the effect is largest. Canonical is
not the same as variety-free, which is the distinction our own earlier reading missed.

\subsection{The external set is not external to GRAIL}

The criterion ladder of \S\ref{sec:rankflip} is reported on the $\nglory$ GLORYx drugs as an
external replication. Checking two independent curations against each other turned up something
that check was not looking for: $19$ of those $37$ parents are substrates in GRAIL's training split
and $5$ more are in validation. Thirteen are unseen. The overlap costs a minute to compute and had
never been computed.

The contamination is one-sided, which decides how much has to be repaired. SyGMa and BioTransformer
do not train on this corpus, and MetaPredictor was trained by its authors on their own data, so
their figures are unaffected \emph{by this overlap} and stay at $n=\nglory$. Only GRAIL's are
inflated, and inflated in our
own favour. Restricting the other three to the thirteen would have been an error in the opposite
direction, discarding two thirds of the sample to correct a bias they do not have.

The audit is scoped to GRAIL's splits because that is the only overlap that can inflate a number in
this paper, and it remains open whether a learned comparator had seen these well-known drugs.
The reported effect is the wrong shape for that: a method that had memorised stereo-bearing
reference structures would match them under strict \textsc{inchikey} and have \emph{less} left for a
stereo-blind criterion to recover, so memorisation attenuates a stereo-step gain, not producing one. MetaPredictor enters that step at $0.362$, below the rule engine's $0.492$, which is
the opposite signature. Nor is the comparator arm a system that trained on nothing: SyGMa's rule
probabilities are themselves fitted to an observed-metabolite database \citep{Ridder_2008}.

Recomputing on the thirteen with the same loaders, matcher and frozen predictions, so that only the
substrate list changes, GRAIL's external recall falls by up to a factor of $2.6$: $0.155 \to 0.059$
under strict \textsc{inchikey}, $0.310 \to 0.185$ once stereochemistry is disregarded, $0.333 \to
0.223$ under the tautomer-aware default. The other three methods move by less than $0.07$, which is
the sample change rather than a correction.

Two claims survive and one does not. GRAIL's stereo step remains significant at $+0.126$
$[+0.028,+0.239]$ against $+0.155$ on the full set, so the mechanism is real, not memorised:
GRAIL emits no assigned stereocentre on this corpus, and a stereo-blind criterion rewards that
whether or not it has seen the drug. Its interaction with SyGMa survives too, but carries no information beyond that step
and should not be counted as a second result: SyGMa's own stereo step on these thirteen is exactly
zero, with an interval of $[0,0]$, so the interaction is numerically the same measurement,
$+0.126$ $[+0.028,+0.239]$. The
interaction between BioTransformer and GRAIL does not: $+0.150$ $[+0.069,+0.242]$ on all $\nglory$,
it is $-0.104$ $[-0.223,+0.000]$ on the unseen drugs, reversing sign and losing significance. It is
among the two largest on the full set and is not claimed on either. Of six pairs, four were significant on the full set
and two are on the clean one, both against SyGMa.

The headline external result is unaffected because it involves neither contaminated arm. Under
strict \textsc{inchikey} SyGMa leads MetaPredictor by $-0.130$ $[-0.196,-0.065]$ and one rung later
the advantage is gone; both methods are external to this corpus in the ordinary sense.

We report this rather than quietly recomputing because the failure is instructive about the protocol
the paper argues for. An audit of train-test overlap is cheap, is not implied by any of the other
checks we run, and surfaced here only as a by-product of measuring something else.

\subsection{What two independent curations of the same drug agree on}

Twenty-five of the external drugs are also curated by this paper's corpus, independently, which
makes their two metabolite lists comparable to each other in exactly the way a prediction is
compared to a reference. Nothing in this subsection involves a model.

The criterion moves that agreement by a factor of nearly three. Under strict \textsc{inchikey} the

which this harness keys stereo-blind, $0.241$; under Tanimoto$=$1, $0.239$; stereo-blind
\textsc{inchikey}, $0.384$; and under the tautomer-aware default $0.406$. The five are not
nested for this measurement (canonical sits above strict \textsc{inchikey}, because dropping
stereochemistry from a SMILES string merges more than dropping it from a hash) so what the figure
shows is the span, not a ladder. One-sided,
the corpus recovers $0.647$ $[0.535,0.756]$ of the GLORYx list and GLORYx $0.539$ $[0.439,0.643]$ of
the corpus list, both under the default. All figures are on the same twenty-five drugs, so none of
this is a population effect. Two experts looking at the same chemistry appear to agree about
a seventh of it under the strictest criterion and about two-fifths under the most tolerant; the
difference is notation, not chemistry, and it is the cleanest argument in this paper for declaring
the criterion.

Those two numbers also bound what the benchmark can register, and the bound needs no model. Neither
curation is a predictor (a source's list is what its inclusion criteria admitted, not an attempt
to reconstruct the other's) so what the pair fixes is the level at which two independent readings
of the same chemistry coincide: $0.539$ one way, $0.647$ the other. References that no method
retrieves cannot all be counted as model error.

Where models sit against that level is a statement about budget, and the budget is the parameter
\S\ref{sec:metric} declares. Uncapped, scored against the corpus annotation with the same matcher,
MetaPredictor recovers $0.562$ $[0.443,0.676]$, SyGMa $0.524$, BioTransformer $0.480$, GRAIL
$0.338$; MetaPredictor's paired gap to the curation is $-0.023$ $[-0.153,+0.123]$, a tie inside
noise, not a lead, and it is bought with $10.6$ metabolites per drug against the curation's
$6.0$, SyGMa's near-tie with $95.9$. Capped instead at each drug's own curated set size (the
budget the curation actually spent, with the curation itself not truncated) its $0.539$ stands
against MetaPredictor's $0.301$, SyGMa's $0.271$, GRAIL's $0.284$ and BioTransformer's $0.193$, and
every paired gap excludes zero (MetaPredictor $+0.238$ $[+0.123,+0.354]$, the smallest of the four).
A fixed $k{=}6$, the curation's mean output, truncates the curation as well and points the same way
without reaching significance: $0.472$ against $0.394$, gap $+0.078$ $[-0.050,+0.210]$. It is the
more conservative of the two matches, since a curation asserts no ranking and the order its list is
cut in is not one it claims.

Models therefore reach curator-level recall only by spending more output than a curator does. At the
curator's own budget, matched at each drug's own curated set size, they are behind by a quarter to a
third of the reference set; the symmetric $k{=}6$ match in the sentence before this one points the
same way and does not reach significance, and this verdict does not cover it.

That per-substrate cap is not an arbitrary handicap, which is the objection it invites. The size
supplied is the curator's own, so it is the per-substrate cardinality an oracle would choose, and
Appendix~\ref{app:props} measures what that oracle is worth and how little of it is obtainable. Both
figures are taken out of sample, from the same cross-fit, so they share a baseline: for
MetaPredictor on the full split the oracle cutoff lifts macro-F1 from $0.254$ at the best constant
$k$ to $0.435$, and a head trained to predict that cutoff recovers $0.008$ of the $0.181$ available,
$4.3\%$ ($3.2\%$ for GRAIL, $1.1\%$ for SyGMa). No method here can set this budget for itself. What
the cap does \emph{not} do is deny the model outputs it could have justified, under recall a cap
can only cost, so the comparison reduces to the plain statement: spending what the curator spent,
the curator retrieves $0.539$ of the other source's list and the best model $0.301$.

One reading is not available, and it is the one the framing invites. The curators had the literature
on each of these drugs in front of them; they were recording what had been observed, not predicting
it from structure. So this measures the references, not the contestants: an independent competent
reading of the same chemistry omits $46\%$ of what the corpus records, which is why recall past that
level is increasingly a measurement of one source's inclusion criteria. It is not evidence about
whether models have caught up with expert judgement, and should not be read as any.

Two limits bear on the interpretation. Twenty-five drugs give intervals
around $\pm0.1$, and these are drugs two sources both chose to curate, so they carry $5.7$ to $6.5$
references each against $2.2$ across the corpus. Better-studied compounds are exactly where two
curations have the most opportunity to agree, so $0.539$ is more likely an upper bound on
inter-source agreement than a typical value. The direction of that bias is not identified: on
less-studied chemistry both the curations and the models have less to go on, and we have not
measured which of the two degrades faster.
