\begin{abstract}
A position on a molecular machine-learning leaderboard is decided in part by choices that no paper
declares: the rule that decides when two structures denote the same molecule, the number of
candidates a method may emit, the convention by which a rule engine applies reaction templates, and
the population that a familiar dataset name refers to. We measure the effect of each on frozen
predictions, retraining nothing and varying only the choice under study.

Each of the first three changes a published ordering, and each changes a different one. Re-scoring
the released predictions of eleven retrosynthesis systems under four matching criteria exchanges
positions inside the largest group that shares a test set, and the exchanges are certified as
interactions between method and criterion, not read off a change in rank. Varying the output budget
alone reorders a metabolite leaderboard, the system that emits most moving from last to first as the
budget widens. The engine convention reverses which of two published rule banks reaches further:
under one convention the larger bank recovers more references, under the other the smaller recovers
four times as many, so a coverage figure quoted without the procedure that produced it is not
comparable across papers. The fourth choice is undeclared without being shown to decide anything.
Eleven released files sit on three test sets under a single name, and no interaction that varies
only the population survives correction for multiplicity.

One condition governs when a choice reorders rather than shifts: it must move two systems apart by
more than the margin between them. A benchmark therefore becomes vulnerable to an undeclared choice
as it matures and its leading systems converge, which is what a table of eight published molecular
generators shows from the other side, exchanging a single adjacent pair because almost no pair on it
is close enough to cross.

Where the candidate set can be enumerated, recall decomposes exactly into coverage, selection and
ranking, and enumerability turns the first term into an exhaustive measurement rather than a sample
estimate or a search bound. Applied to metabolite prediction, the decomposition assigns each
system's shortfall to a stage of its pipeline. We release the evaluation protocol, the audited
split, the frozen predictions of every method compared here, and the analysis code.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Machine learning is used to predict the chemistry that a molecule will undergo. Given a drug, a
model predicts the metabolites into which enzymes will convert it; given a target compound, a model
predicts the reactants from which it can be synthesised; given a design objective, a model proposes
molecules that meet it. All three tasks are evaluated in the same way. A method proposes a ranked
list of structures, a curated reference records the structures that are correct, and the method is
scored by how often the two agree.

Whether two structures agree is not self-evident. A single compound can be drawn in several ways
that a chemist reads as one substance: as either of two tautomers, which differ in the position of a
mobile hydrogen; with or without the three-dimensional arrangement at a stereocentre; as any of
several equivalent strings. An evaluation must therefore fix a rule that decides when a predicted
structure counts as the reference structure. It must also fix how many candidates a method may
propose, because a method that proposes more will match more. Where predictions are generated by
applying reaction templates, it must fix the convention by which a template is matched against a
molecule, because a template that names a hydrogen explicitly will not match a structure whose
hydrogens are implicit. And it must fix which molecules the test set contains. These four choices
are made in every published evaluation and stated in almost none.

That evaluation choices can change conclusions is established in several fields. Standardising the
comparison procedure has reversed a published ordering of retrosynthesis models
\citep{Maziarz_2025}; re-scoring frozen submissions under a different notion of equivalence has
changed which system leads in text-to-SQL \citep{Zhong_2020} and in question answering
\citep{Kamalloo_2023}; the choice of aggregate inverts the winner in drug-response prediction
\citep{agarwal2026metric}.
What is not established is how far any single choice moves a ranking when it is the only thing that
changes, and in particular whether a choice can \emph{reorder} a leaderboard rather than shifting
every system on it by a similar amount. The distinction matters, because a shift leaves conclusions
intact and a reordering does not.

We answer that question by re-scoring predictions that other groups have already released, so that
nothing is retrained and no method is reimplemented. Three of the four choices reorder a published
leaderboard, and the reorderings are certified as interactions between method and choice, not asserted from a change in rank. The fourth choice is undeclared but produces no interaction that
survives correction, and separating those two statements is possible only because the same test is
applied to all four. A single condition governs when a reordering occurs: the choice must move two
systems apart by more than the margin between them, which makes a benchmark more vulnerable as its
leading methods converge.

The deepest case study is metabolite prediction, where the effect of a choice can be measured
against a quantity that is itself exact. In that setting the set of candidates a rule-based method
could ever propose is finite and can be enumerated, so the best score the method could achieve with
perfect selection and ranking is not estimated but computed. Recall then decomposes exactly into
what the rule base reaches, what selection retains of that, and what ranking returns of what
remains, which turns a single score that admits no diagnosis into an attribution of the shortfall to
a stage.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt \parskip0pt
\item The matching criterion exchanges positions in a published leaderboard of retrosynthesis
  systems, on predictions their authors released. The exchanges are certified as method-by-criterion
  interactions and corrected for multiplicity across the whole family of tests.
\item The output budget reorders a metabolite leaderboard on its own, with every method and its
  ranking held fixed.
\item The convention by which an engine applies reaction templates reverses which of two published
  rule banks reaches further. A coverage figure is therefore a property of a rule set together with
  a procedure, and is not comparable across papers that do not state the procedure.
\item A choice reorders a leaderboard only when it moves two systems apart by more than the margin
  between them. Across four leaderboards in three domains, no reordering occurs where the margin
  exceeds the movement, so a benchmark becomes more vulnerable as its leading systems converge.
\item Which population a dataset name refers to is ambiguous in released work, and the ambiguity is
  not shown to reorder anything: eleven released prediction files sit on three test sets, and no
  interaction that varies only the population survives correction.
\item An evaluation protocol, an audited split, the frozen predictions of every method compared, a
  checker that reports these choices from files alone, and the analysis code are released. Where the
  candidate set is enumerable, the coverage term of the decomposition is measured exhaustively,
  which no sampled or search-bounded setting supplies.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of this article is organised as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews prior work
on the four choices and on staged evaluation. Section~\ref{sec:tame} defines the protocol, the
matching criteria and the split. Section~\ref{sec:xdomainmain} re-scores released retrosynthesis
predictions and establishes the condition under which a criterion reorders a leaderboard.
Section~\ref{sec:decomp} develops the decomposition and the coverage ceiling, and shows that the
ceiling depends on the engine. Section~\ref{sec:results} applies the four choices inside one
benchmark. Section~\ref{sec:limitations} states the scope of the results and
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{The domain, and what has been compared in it.} Rule-based metabolite prediction was
established by SyGMa \citep{Ridder_2008}, which encodes phase I and phase II biotransformations as
reaction templates and scores products by the empirical frequency with which each template yields an
observed metabolite. GLORYx \citep{de_Bruyn_Kops_2020} couples a comparable library to a learned
model of sites of metabolism; BioTransformer \citep{Djoumbou_Feunang_2019} extends templates to
microbial and environmental transformations; MetaTrans \citep{Litsa_2020} and MetaPredictor
\citep{Zhu_2024} dispense with templates for sequence-to-sequence translation and a transformer with
a filtering stage. Comparative studies recover the same coverage-precision trade-off: five
predictors on 85 agrochemical parents \citep{Scholz_2023}, six tools on 37 chemicals where the
broadest-coverage system also produced the most incorrect predictions \citep{Boyce_2022}, human
radiolabelled ADME data \citep{Gao_2026}. Each fixes one criterion for a correct prediction, and none reports how sensitive its conclusion is to that choice. That one structure can be written
more than one way is documented independently of this domain: \citet{Dhaked_2020} catalogue
tautomeric transformations that the standard InChI identifier normalises only a subset of,
\citet{Hahnke_2018} report roughly $60\%$ of PubChem records differing from their canonical InChI
representation, and \citet{Mansouri_2024} describe a standardisation pipeline for
structure-activity modelling. That literature treats standardisation as preprocessing applied
before an analysis, not as a parameter of the analysis.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Evaluation choices in adjacent fields.} None of the four choices is in doubt in general.
On the matching rule, \citet{Maziarz_2025} re-evaluated retrosynthesis models under a standardised
procedure and reversed a published ordering, in part because one model had been scored under a
relaxed stereochemistry criterion that its baselines were not given; \citet{Hastedt_2024} release
the ranked predictions of twelve such systems, which makes comparisons possible without
reimplementation; and \citet{liu2026massspecgymwild} identify divergent metric implementations and
inconsistent stereochemistry handling in published mass-spectrometry work. Outside chemistry,
re-scoring frozen submissions under a different equivalence rule misrepresents the state of the art
in text-to-SQL \citep{Zhong_2020} and changes which question-answering system leads
\citep{Kamalloo_2023}. On the aggregate, the choice of metric inverts the winner in drug-response
prediction \citep{agarwal2026metric}, the standard score in structure-based drug design rises with
the atom count of the output instead of its quality \citep{Gao_2025_reframing}, which motivated
rebuilding that benchmark \citep{Lin_2025_cbgbench}, and weighting examples by difficulty reorders
natural-language leaderboards \citep{Mishra_2021, Rodriguez_2021}. On the output budget the relevant
literature is older, having long evaluated ranked output at a declared cut-off
\citep{Jarvelin_2002}, under incomplete relevance judgements \citep{Buckley_2004}, corrected for
recording propensity \citep{Joachims_2017}, and with the winner of a cut-off metric changing with
how the candidates entering it are counted \citep{Krichene_2020}.

\paragraph{Staged evaluation.} Separating what a candidate generator reaches from what a later
stage retains is standard practice: cascade ranking prunes stage by stage \citep{Wang_2011_cascade},
recommendation is built and evaluated as candidate generation followed by ranking
\citep{Covington_2016}, open-domain question answering reports retriever recall at a budget beside
end-task accuracy \citep{Karpukhin_2020}, and \citet{Maziarz_2025} separate single-step accuracy
from search. In each of these the first term is estimated from a sample or bounded by a search
budget.

\paragraph{What is left unestablished.} Two questions remain open in this literature. The first is
how far a single evaluation choice moves a ranking when it is the only quantity that varies, and
whether it reorders a leaderboard rather than shifting every system on it; prior demonstrations
compare studies that differed in several respects at once, so the effect of any one choice is not
identified. The second is what the first term of a staged decomposition can be when the candidate
set is finite and enumerable, not sampled, and whether a coverage figure obtained that way is
comparable across papers. The present work addresses both.

\section{The evaluation protocol: what is declared and what is held fixed}
\label{sec:tame}

The protocol used throughout is a matching specification, an audited split and a re-scoring
harness. It is referred to below as \textsc{tame}, for tautomer-aware matched evaluation, and it
fixes what every number in \S\ref{sec:results} is measured under.

\paragraph{The matching criterion.} The field has never agreed what counts as a match. GLORYx uses a stereo-blind InChIKey skeleton,
MetaTrans compares Morgan fingerprints, and rule-based systems that canonicalise their own output,
SyGMa among them, effectively compare canonical SMILES. Each is a different equivalence relation,
and each is applied silently. \textsc{tame} states all five as named equivalence relations: predicted and reference
structures are mapped through one of
\[
q \in \{\textsc{canonical},\ \textsc{inchikey},\ \textsc{inchi-no-stereo},\ \textsc{tanimoto1},\
\textsc{inchikey-tautomer}\}
\]
and a prediction counts as a hit iff $q(\hat{y}) = q(y)$ for some reference $y$. Each map is
computed with RDKit 2022.09.5, the version that reproduces the columns reported here. A ceiling
reported under this protocol declares the \emph{application} procedure too, namely the engine and its version,
sanitisation, the enumeration and filtering policy and the depth, since at fixed rules that
procedure moves reach by as much as the reach gap reported here (Appendix~\ref{app:enginereach}).
\textsc{canonical} is canonical SMILES equality, \textsc{inchikey} the full InChIKey and
\textsc{inchi-no-stereo} its first block, the connectivity skeleton blind to stereochemistry, charge
and isotopes; \textsc{tanimoto1} is Tanimoto similarity of one over Morgan fingerprints at radius 2
over 2048 bits; \textsc{inchikey-tautomer} standardises both sides before hashing, taking the
fragment parent, neutralising charges, canonicalising tautomers and dropping stereochemistry.

All five criteria are reported for every comparison. The default for this corpus is
\textsc{inchikey-tautomer}, chosen because the references neither carry stereochemistry nor
distinguish tautomers, and because standard InChI normalises only a subset of tautomers
\citep{Dhaked_2020}: an engine that emits the keto form of an enol reference fails to match under
plain \textsc{inchikey} while producing the same substance. This default is the most permissive of
the five and is blind to stereochemistry as well as to tautomerism, which also suits methods that
standardise their output without stereocentres and therefore cannot emit one. Standardisation of
this kind is established preprocessing; adopting it as the \emph{matching} rule is not.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Split and leakage audit.} All splits are substrate-disjoint: no substrate in test or
validation appears in train, and no test substrate appears in validation. Clean triples are then
audited read-only by a separate script that canonicalises SMILES and verifies zero substrate and
zero positive-pair overlap across every split pair, emitting a machine-checkable report.
Leakage-aware splitting is itself not novel, since DataSAIL \citep{Joeres_2025} minimises
cross-split similarity on MoleculeNet-style tasks; the leak that matters here is
substrate--metabolite \emph{identity} overlap, which is domain-specific. As a secondary check, the deployed configuration
gives macro recall@15 $=0.344$ on validation against $0.334$ on test (293 and 291 substrates). The
$+0.010$ gap matches the seed-to-seed standard deviation, so it corroborates the zero-overlap audit
rather than standing as independent evidence.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Metrics and inference.} Results are reported as recall@$k$ together with the mean output
size. Precision alone is unsuitable under incomplete annotation, where an unannotated but real
metabolite is counted as a false positive; recall alone is unsuitable because a method can raise it
by emitting more (Appendix~\ref{app:budget}). Three comparisons are confirmatory and each is
corrected for multiplicity: the differential criterion sensitivity of \S\ref{sec:rankflip}, the
learned-against-prior gap of \S\ref{sec:xmc}, and the cross-domain interaction, whose $448$ paired
tests form one family. All remaining intervals are exploratory. Model and configuration selection
is performed on validation data, with two exceptions stated in Appendix~\ref{app:arch}.

\section{The criterion reorders a published leaderboard in another field}
\label{sec:xdomainmain}

\looseness=-1
\citet{Hastedt_2024} released the ranked predictions of twelve retrosynthesis systems, of which
eleven parse. Reading those files, not the accompanying table shows that they were produced
on three different test sets (Appendix~\ref{app:xdomainsets}). Within the largest group sharing
one, seven systems scored on their $4{,}999$ common reactions change places at top-1, on predictions that never move and under a rule applied identically to all seven. Under canonical matching
the leaders run GraphRetro $0.5377$, LocalRetro $0.5339$, Retroformer $0.5325$, Graph2SMILES
$0.5293$; disregard stereochemistry and they run Graph2SMILES $0.5571$, Retroformer $0.5541$,
GraphRetro $0.5501$, LocalRetro $0.5469$. Fourth becomes first (Figure~\ref{fig:xdomain}).
Five of twenty-one pairs exchange in all.

An exchange of positions is a description of the data; the claim is the interaction, corrected
across the largest family declared in this work. Of $448$ paired tests, one for every pair of systems, every pair of conventions and every $k$, $233$ have intervals excluding zero, $109$ of $448$
paired interactions surviving Holm at $0.05$ across the whole family. The effect is concentrated at top-1, the number this
field leads with but not the whole accuracy curve.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{The condition is packing, and it is arithmetic before it is empirical.} An exchange needs
the criterion to move two systems by more than the gap between them, so a leaderboard becomes
vulnerable exactly as it matures. These seven sit a median $0.013$ apart against a differential of
$0.015$ and reorder; three $0.047$ apart are moved as much and do not. Over $439$
method-pair by criterion-pair comparisons across four leaderboards in three domains, with nothing
re-scored, the movement exceeds the gap once in nine and reverses the pair once in seventeen, and no
reversal occurs where the gap survives the movement. The third domain shows the condition from its
other end: on eight published molecular generators the same criterion certifies $19$ of $112$
interactions and yields one exchange, a metric scoring seven of eight above $0.986$ leaving almost
nothing close enough to cross (Appendix~\ref{app:xdomain}).

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Which population a familiar name refers to.} The same eleven files answer the fourth
question, and the answer is different in kind. Two of the three groups share $490$ reactions on
which they agree about the recorded reaction, nested inside each group's own five thousand, so two
systems can be compared on the subset and on the whole with method, criterion and budget all fixed.
Of the $380$ comparisons this gives, $44$ change the ordering; on the metabolite split of
\S\ref{sec:results} the same test gives $10$ of $60$. Neither instance survives correction. None of
the $440$ interactions passes Holm at $0.05$, and on the metabolite split none has an interval
excluding zero before correction. The population is therefore ambiguous in released work without
being shown to decide a ranking, and the two statements are separable only because the same test is
applied to all four choices (Appendix~\ref{app:popaxis}).

\section{The decomposition and the coverage ceiling}
\label{sec:decomp}

\looseness=-1
The deep case study runs on GRAIL, a metabolite predictor built for this work. It has three stages:
a learned selector chooses which of its \nbank\ reaction rules to apply to a substrate, an engine
applies the selected rules to enumerate candidate products, and a learned filter scores and ranks
those candidates. It is used here because a rule-grounded system of this form is the setting in
which a coverage figure can be measured exhaustively, and it is compared throughout against
published systems on frozen predictions.

A rule-grounded predictor reaches a reference metabolite only by passing three gates in sequence:
the transformation must be \emph{expressible} by some rule in the bank, that rule must be
\emph{selected} into the substrate's candidate pool under a finite budget, and the resulting
product must be \emph{ranked} into the top $k$. Write, for a substrate set $S$ with reference sets
$Y_s$:
\[
\underbrace{\frac{|H|}{|U|}}_{\text{recall@}k}
\;=\;
\underbrace{\frac{|C_{\text{full}}|}{|U|}}_{\text{coverage}}
\;\cdot\;
\underbrace{\frac{|C_{\text{bud}}|}{|C_{\text{full}}|}}_{\text{selection}}
\;\cdot\;
\underbrace{\frac{|H|}{|C_{\text{bud}}|}}_{\text{ranking}},
\qquad
|U|=\textstyle\sum_{s\in S}|Y_s| ,
\]
where $C_{\text{full}}$ are references recovered by applying the \emph{entire} bank without a
budget, $C_{\text{bud}}$ those surviving the selector's budgeted pool, and $H$ those actually
returned in the top $k$. The first factor requires an enumerable candidate set: the bank for rule-grounded methods, a wide
decode for a sequence-to-sequence one, where the same three gates apply: MetaPredictor factorises
$0.612\times0.804\times1.000$, exactly its realised recall, though a beam is a budget rather than a
knowledge base and bounds that decode instead of the model (Appendix~\ref{app:seq2seq}). The identity is exact by construction (each set contains the next), so
the three factors partition the loss with no residual: it is an accounting device, not a model. Two
aggregations are in use and every figure states which. The identity is written in \emph{micro}, the
pooled ratio of sums, because only that form multiplies out exactly; head-to-head comparisons use
\emph{macro}, the comparator tools' convention. The macro ceiling is the higher of the two,
$\ceilingmacro$ against $\ceiling$, so writing the identity in micro understates the coverage
factor (Appendix~\ref{app:formal}).

\paragraph{The ceiling as a primitive.} The first factor is measurable without any model: apply all
\nbank\ rules to every test substrate and ask what fraction of references appears anywhere in the
union. That \textbf{coverage ceiling} is the best recall achievable if selection and ranking were
both perfect, and on the clean test split it is \ceiling\ (cluster-bootstrap 95\% CI
$[0.794,0.839]$). It should be reported alongside recall for any rule-grounded method, and reported as system reach: a rule set together with a declared application procedure, not what a knowledge base can express. Swapping only the engine moves it by as much as the reach gap below, so a ceiling quoted without its engine is not comparable across papers. Inside one system it
is exactly what it claims, and no ranking improvement can exceed it.

\paragraph{What the decomposition says.}\looseness=-1 Figure~\ref{fig:decomp} localises the loss. The deployed pipeline converts only $32.5\%$ of its own ceiling into realised recall, and the three factors separate with no pair of intervals overlapping. Selection is where the loss sits at this operating point: barely a third of what the bank can reach survives the selector's rule budget. Ranking is close to lossless, at $0.960$ $[0.941,0.976]$. That third factor is truncation at GRAIL's own output
policy, itself a cap at $k=15$: it emits at the cap on $308$ of \ntest\ substrates ($26.3\%$), and all $29$
references lost between the budgeted pool and the emitted output fall on those. Selection binds
jointly with the cut-off, not alone.
Widening the selector moves the loss downstream, since what selection recovers the cut-off gives
back: the deployed pair caps the pipeline near $0.41$, macro on $245$ substrates
(Appendix~\ref{app:props}).

\looseness=-1
Removing selection entirely tests that reading. With no selector and no threshold, ranking the whole
pool, the bank passes SyGMa by $0.167$ $[0.112,0.223]$ at $k=82$ and by $0.063$ $[0.001,0.125]$ at
$k=32$, while remaining behind at $k=8$ and indistinguishable at the field's $k=15$
(Appendix~\ref{app:noselect}).

\looseness=-1
Applied to a second method it stops being self-diagnosis: SyGMa decomposes on the same split in the
same frame with the inverse profile, converting nearly all of a narrower reach where we convert a
third of a wider one, so what separates us is the cost of \emph{this} selection stage, not a
worse rule base, and the bank's own bound is the corpus rather than tuning
(Appendix~\ref{app:decompsygma}).

\paragraph{The coverage factor belongs to the system, not the rule base.}
\label{sec:enginereach}
\looseness=-3
Reporting a ceiling presumes it is a property of the bank, and it is not. $152$ of SyGMa's $175$
rules sit verbatim in ours, so those can be held fixed while only the procedure applying them
changes: a depth-1 enumeration that expands the substrate with explicit hydrogens against SyGMa's
own, which does not, same substrates, same matcher, one step each. They reach $0.189$
$[0.151,0.230]$ under the first and $0.381$ $[0.333,0.433]$ under the second,
an engine term of $+0.188$ $[+0.170,+0.205]$ paired, against an entire bank-to-bank gap of
$0.201$ on those substrates, with the $23$ rules absent from our bank worth $+0.143$ $[+0.107,+0.180]$ at
fixed engine and depth no confound. Almost all of what reads as a difference between knowledge
bases is a difference between the programs that query them, and that difference is a single preprocessing step, the expansion itself. Our own generator does not expand, which is why \ceiling\ is quoted without it;
measured with it the same bank reaches $0.735$. Toggling that one call, bank and substrates
untouched, carries reach from $0.200$ to $0.390$, the whole of the engine term, leaving $+0.002$
$[0.000,+0.005]$ micro between the two programs, though a macro estimator separates them at $+0.006$
$[+0.002,+0.010]$. The loop's other two choices move reach by nothing
(Appendix~\ref{app:enginereach}).
The term is thus not a fact about two particular programs but about one decision neither reports: what a bank covers is uninterpretable without the engine
that realised the figure (Appendix~\ref{app:props}). The dependence is not specific to metabolism. Every one of the $42{,}554$ patterns in the standard retrosynthesis template library constrains atoms by degree and none names a hydrogen, so the same step costs that library $82\%$ of its applicability. That is the second of the two mechanisms: a match lost before a product is built (Appendix~\ref{app:retroconvention}).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The same axis inside one benchmark, where the ceiling is measured}
\label{sec:rankflip}

\looseness=-2
Every factor counts matched references, so the criterion is settled first: published methods have
their \emph{frozen} predictions re-scored under all five criteria, so only the match rule changes
across columns, never the chemistry. GRAIL, SyGMa \citep{Ridder_2008} and MetaPredictor
\citep{Zhu_2024} are compared on the whole clean test split, paired on identical substrates
(Table~\ref{tab:matchsens}); BioTransformer \citep{Djoumbou_Feunang_2019} and MetaTrans
\citep{Litsa_2020} on the $\nshared$-substrate shared subset (Appendix~\ref{app:xdomain}).

\looseness=-1
Methods are not equally sensitive to the convention. Over the full split GRAIL gains $0.016$
$[0.010,0.023]$ from canonical to tautomer matching against MetaPredictor's $0.040$
$[0.030,0.050]$, a differential of $+0.024$ $[0.014,0.034]$ surviving Holm--Bonferroni over the
three pairs. Unequal headroom runs the wrong way here: GRAIL enters from the
lower baseline, so has more left to recover, and gains the less (Table~\ref{tab:matchsens}). The same differential against BioTransformer on the $\nshared$-substrate subset is $+0.120$, five
times larger; the full-split figure is the estimate, the subset having been chosen for data
availability. Output size is a second, orthogonal confound: SyGMa emits $81$
candidates per substrate against GRAIL's $8.4$ (Appendix~\ref{app:budget}).

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Primary endpoint.} The endpoint is the differential sensitivity, the interaction between
criterion and method, estimated by a paired bootstrap over substrates with $10{,}000$ resamples.
Every method gains under a more tolerant criterion, so only the differential can change an ordering.
On the $\nshared$-substrate subset two pairs separate along two criterion steps, BioTransformer
gaining \interA\ \interAci\ more than GRAIL from canonical to tautomer matching and SyGMa
\interB\ \interBci\ more than MetaTrans from canonical to InChIKey; the first survives correction
at that family size and the second does not. The criterion is therefore a \textbf{method-dependent
confounder}, not a neutral scoring choice. The declared families, every raw and adjusted
$p$-value, and the pre-specification of the design are given in Appendix~\ref{app:holm}.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{External replication.} The results above live on a $\nshared$-substrate subset of our own
split, so we repeated the analysis on the field's de-facto external hold-out, the \nglory\ GLORYx
drugs \citep{de_Bruyn_Kops_2020}, whose own tool is not scored here for want of runnable weights. It is
external for three of the four methods and not for ours: $24$ of the $37$ are in GRAIL's train or
validation split, so its row uses the $13$ unseen (Table~\ref{tab:matchsens}).

\looseness=-1
The result this section rests on involves neither contaminated arm. Under strict InChIKey SyGMa
leads MetaPredictor by $-0.130$ $[-0.196,-0.065]$; one rung later the advantage is gone ($+0.005$
$[-0.071,+0.086]$). The endpoint is not that transition, which contrasts a significant
result with a null one, but the interaction: MetaPredictor gains $+0.135$ $[+0.072,+0.209]$ more
than SyGMa across the stereo step, rejected at either family size (Appendix~\ref{app:holm}). That interval is what is certified: the relaxed gap covers zero
and still admits SyGMa ahead by $0.071$, so a criterion erases a significant advantage here rather
than reversing it.
Normalised by the headroom left it does not vanish but grows, to $+0.209$ $[+0.111,+0.322]$
(Appendix~\ref{app:holm}). On the uncontaminated drugs GRAIL's stereo sensitivity survives at $+0.126$
$[+0.028,+0.239]$, as does its interaction with SyGMa, whose stereo step here is zero; the
\emph{external} interaction with BioTransformer does not survive there and is not claimed.
The set's coverage ceiling stays on all $\nglory$: it is $0.719$ on the twenty-four seen drugs
against $0.684$ on the thirteen unseen, a difference whose interval spans zero
(Appendix~\ref{app:external}).



\paragraph{The criterion moves human agreement, and that agreement bounds what a recall figure
means.} The
strongest evidence for declaring the criterion involves no model. Twenty-five of the external drugs are independently curated by our corpus as well, so the two metabolite lists can be compared as a prediction is compared to a reference. On the same drugs, the two expert curations agree at a Jaccard of $0.145$ under strict \textsc{inchikey} matching and at $0.406$ under the tautomer-aware default. Most of what
looks like disagreement about observed chemistry is disagreement about notation.

\looseness=-1
They also bound what a recall figure means rather than what it can reach: a competent independent
curation records only $0.539$ of the corpus annotation, so past that level recall measures one
source's inclusion choices. That $0.539$ is measured on the $25$ drugs both sources chose to
curate, which carry $5.7$ to $6.5$ references each against $2.2$ across the clean test split. Where models sit against it depends on
the budget, and both matched-budget analyses belong here, not only the significant one. MetaPredictor's uncapped $0.562$ is a tie inside noise, bought with $10.6$ metabolites per drug against the curation's $6.0$. Capped at each drug's own curated set size it recovers $0.301$, behind by $0.238$ $[0.123,0.354]$. Under a symmetric match, a fixed $k{=}6$ that truncates the curation as well, it reaches $0.394$ against $0.472$, a gap of $+0.078$ $[-0.050,+0.210]$ that does not reach significance (Appendix~\ref{app:xdomain}).

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Mechanism.} The spread tracks how far a method's outputs diverge
\emph{representationally} from the references, and the intermediate column says which divergence is
responsible: here stereochemistry alone, every separating gain realised by the stereo step. GLORYx
references carry stereocentres that several of these systems cannot emit; internally, where
references are already stereo-stripped, tautomerism fails instead. Which relaxation dominates is therefore a property of the reference set. The effect requires
references that carry representational variety, and a leaderboard whose margins are narrower than
the differential it produces.


\subsection{The aggregate and the budget are one variable, and the decomposition predicts it}
\label{sec:metric}

\looseness=-1
Every interval in this subsection is exploratory. A method that emits eighty candidates to catch a
few more references is rewarded by recall and punished by F1, and on each method's deployed output
set the two orderings disagree. Against GRAIL the disagreement is certified in both directions:
SyGMa leads on recall by $0.253$ $[0.222,0.283]$ and sits $0.088$ $[0.076,0.099]$ below it on F1.
Against MetaPredictor only the F1 direction is certified, at $0.113$ $[0.107,0.120]$, while SyGMa's
recall lead of $0.023$ rests on an interval bounded at zero and is bought by emitting $80.7$
candidates against $10.8$.

\looseness=-1
The budget does the same work, and it is what identifies the variable. Truncating a ranked list
moves the emitted count with the method and its ranking held fixed, so a reordering along the sweep
cannot be a property of any one system. MetaPredictor's count has been moved the other way with the
model fixed too, $10.9$ distinct candidates to $34.3$ at a wider beam (Appendix~\ref{app:seq2seq}). It
reorders without any budget having to be chosen: swept over $k$ the three methods take three
distinct orderings, SyGMa above GRAIL at every budget up to $13$, certified from $k=1$ to $k=10$ and by $0.071$ $[0.057,0.086]$ at $k=5$, and below only from $14$ on, where the field's $15$ sits. A
fourth method exhausts the field: on $291$ substrates carrying all four, MetaTox at $33.6$
candidates is last at $k\le5$ and first from $k=15$, the budget this field reports at
(Appendix~\ref{app:fourmethod}). A budget binds a method only when it emits more, so at $k=15$ SyGMa is cut on $92.7\%$ of
substrates and GRAIL on none (Appendix~\ref{app:props}).

\looseness=-1
Every certified reversal along the sweep involves the one method that overproduces, and none
certifies among methods emitting alike; that second half is a failure to detect, those pairs leaving
intervals up to $0.158$ open. The matching rule acts independently of the budget: matched at about
eight emitted candidates, GRAIL's sensitivity sits below both comparators by $-0.020$
$[-0.029,-0.010]$ and $-0.025$ $[-0.035,-0.015]$, so the criterion separates methods at fixed output
size (Appendix~\ref{app:xdomain}).

\looseness=-1
The decomposition says why. SyGMa's selection factor is $1$ (\S\ref{sec:decomp}), which is what
recall rewards and precision punishes, so a pipeline with no selection stage is scored relatively higher by the first than by the second, although reach remains free and it need not lead on recall.

\looseness=-1
The matching criterion acts on these metrics as well, and it acts where it barely touched recall.
The F1 gap between GRAIL and MetaPredictor is certified under four of the five criteria, at $-0.026$
$[-0.038,-0.013]$ under the tautomer-aware default, and is indistinguishable from zero under plain
\textsc{inchikey}. That is an erased gap and not a reversal, and it shows that the matching rule and
the output size do not act on the same comparisons (Appendix~\ref{app:props}).





\subsection{Selection as extreme multi-label classification}
\label{sec:xmc}
\label{sec:diagnosis}

\looseness=-1
GRAIL trails both published comparators at the field's budget and leads at none: SyGMa reaches
$0.572$ on the $1{,}168$ substrates they share, a paired $-0.242$ $[-0.271,\,-0.212]$. The second
confirmatory comparison locates that shortfall in selection. Through the same enumeration and
filter, a learned selector reaches $0.266$ where rule-firing frequency alone reaches $0.410$, a
paired $-0.144$ $[-0.196,\,-0.095]$, and inverse-propensity weighting moves it to $0.249$.
Choosing which of \nbank\ rules apply is extreme multi-label classification with a near-empty
positive set, where frequency baselines are hard to beat \citep{Jain_2016}.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\looseness=-2
\paragraph{What the metabolite numbers bound.} The five-method table rests on $\nshared$ shared
substrates of frozen predictions, because MetaTrans's pipeline no longer reproduces and
BioTransformer's configuration is unscripted; the other three methods are scored on the full
$\ntest$. An unannotated but real product counts as a false positive, so every precision and F1
figure is pessimistic. Some references are multi-generation products that no single rule reaches,
and depth-2 application lifts the ceiling by ${\sim}0.012$ at $8.5$ times the cost, so $\ceiling$
bounds this formulation and not the task.

\looseness=-2
\paragraph{What the newer results rest on.} The cross-domain numbers come from eleven files
produced elsewhere, read under an assumption proven where an independent ground truth exists, and
the reordering there is a top-1 phenomenon. Packing is measured across four leaderboards, which is
few. The population negative bounds how large an interaction would have had to be to show, not
whether one exists. The figure reported for this system is a single evaluation of the deployed
configuration.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\looseness=-2
Four choices are settled before any model is compared: when two structures count as the same
molecule, how many candidates a method may emit, how an engine applies a reaction template, and
which population a dataset name refers to. None is routinely declared and three of them move a
published ordering. All four can be read from files that are already public, and the checker
released with this work reports them from a template bank and a set of prediction files, so the
evidence supports a requirement to declare them and not a standard that fixes them.

\subsubsection*{Reproducibility statement}

Every quantity reported here derives from a committed artifact and regenerates from the released
material, and the checks that establish this are released as executable scripts. One of them is
intended for prediction files produced elsewhere: given a template bank it reports which templates
depend on the hydrogen convention and whether the bank mixes conventions, and given two or more
prediction files it reports whether they were produced on the same population and how far apart
their output sizes are. It requires no reference data and no trained model, and applied to the
eleven released retrosynthesis files it recovers the three test sets in seconds.

The cost of regeneration differs by analysis. Every split-level result regenerates from the
committed artifacts alone, which comprise the frozen predictions, the matching-key tables and the
per-substrate records. The ablations that rank or re-select candidates additionally require the
trained checkpoints, which the anonymised archive contains together with the frozen predictions of
every method compared, the audits, the evaluation harness, the split construction and the analysis
code.

Three constraints bear on exact, not approximate reproduction. One result depends on a
third-party distribution that is not redistributed here: the reach of BioTransformer's template set
is computed from the $994$ SMIRKS shipped with that tool, which an installed copy supplies; its
weights and those of MetaPredictor are likewise third-party and are cited rather than shipped.
Results measured on a subsample of a split record the cap and the seed that drew it, without which
the substrate set is not recoverable, since sampling proceeds without replacement and a different
cap yields a different set of the same size, not a subset. Confidence intervals are
regenerable as well: per-substrate rows are sorted before resampling, so a repeated run reproduces
an artifact exactly.

Two standing reconciliations guard the analyses against the code they analyse. Where an analysis
path re-derives a deployed output, the two are verified equal before use, and the released
per-candidate score dump reproduces the deployed ranking on all $\ntest$ substrates. Where a
reimplemented loop stands in for the deployed one, it is gated against a figure the deployed loop
produced, at the scale at which the claim is made.

Every set treated here as external or third-party has been audited for overlap with the training and
validation substrates. The GLORYx set overlaps on $24$ of its $37$ drugs, and \S\ref{sec:rankflip}
reports GRAIL on the $13$ that do not overlap; the $\nshared$-substrate shared subset overlaps on
none of its $150$. The audit is released as a script.
