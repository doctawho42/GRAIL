\begin{abstract}
Metabolite-structure prediction is reported as a single top-$k$ recall number, as if that
transparently measured a method's chemistry-generation capability. It does not, and this paper
quantifies both ways it fails. First, \textbf{the number depends on an unstated convention}:
nothing fixes when a predicted structure ``matches'' a reference, and papers variously use
canonical SMILES, InChIKey, stereo-blind InChI, or fingerprint identity. We introduce
\textbf{TAME}, a tautomer-aware matching and re-scoring protocol on a leakage-audited,
substrate-disjoint split; re-score five methods' frozen predictions under five conventions; and
show with a pre-declared primary endpoint that the convention is a \textbf{method-dependent
confounder that reorders the leaderboard}. The protocol $\times$ method interaction is
\textbf{\interA} (95\% CI \textbf{\interAci}) for one method pair, replicates on a second,
independent pair along a different protocol axis (\textbf{\interB}, \textbf{\interBci}), and
replicates again on the external GLORYx set with four methods. Second, \textbf{one number
conflates three distinct failures}: whether the rule bank \emph{covers} a transformation, whether
the model \emph{selects} the right rule, and whether it \emph{ranks} the product into a bounded
output. We make that decomposition operational --- with the bank's coverage ceiling as a
diagnostic primitive --- using \textbf{GRAIL}, a three-stage rule-based-plus-learned predictor
(\nbank-SMIRKS bank, learned rule selector, PU-trained structural filter). On \ntest\ audited test
substrates the ceiling is \textbf{\ceiling}, but the deployed pipeline realises recall@15 =
\textbf{\realised} (micro) --- 35.5\% of it --- a gap \textbf{dominated by selection loss}
(retention \textbf{\selret}), not by ranking. Stated up front: \textbf{GRAIL does not win on
recall} (\grailmacro\ macro) --- it is the instrument we measure first and hardest. The
contribution is the protocol, the decomposition, and an honestly-diagnosed interpretable
predictor, not a state-of-the-art claim.
\end{abstract}
