\begin{abstract}
A position on a molecular machine-learning leaderboard is decided in part by choices that no paper
declares: the rule that decides when two structures denote the same molecule, the number of
candidates a method may emit, the convention by which a rule engine applies reaction templates, and
the population that a familiar dataset name refers to. We measure the effect of each on frozen
predictions, retraining nothing and varying only the choice under study.

Each of them is a cell of a grid that leaderboards are computed in and papers do not report, so a
published ordering can be asked a question it is never asked: does it also hold in every other cell?
The pairs for which it does are the part of a ranking that can be quoted without naming the cell,
and they are a minority. On three leaderboards in two fields, roughly a third of the pairwise
orderings survive every cell of a grid drawn from choices those fields already vary between papers;
on one of the three, none do. The share is computable from released prediction files alone.
Two of the four choices change a published ordering and they differ in how they break it. The
output budget reverses margins that a paired
interval separates from zero: one method is last at tight budgets and first at the budget the field
reports, with its ranking held fixed. The matching criterion does something else. Re-scoring the released predictions of eleven
retrosynthesis systems exchanges positions inside the largest group that shares a test set, at the
top-1 accuracy those tables lead with, and every pair it exchanges is one the benchmark never
separated; where a separation is certified, the criterion erases it instead of reversing it. The engine convention decides no ordering but decides what a bank reaches: one
published rule base loses three quarters of its references when the hydrogens it was written for are
withdrawn, another gains slightly, so a coverage figure is the value of a bank together with a
convention and is not comparable across papers that state only the bank. The fourth choice is
undeclared without being shown to decide anything: eleven released files sit on
three test sets under a single name, and no interaction that varies only the population survives
correction, although that null excludes only effects much larger than the ones the criterion
produces.

A benchmark is most exposed where its leading systems are closest, which a table of eight published
molecular generators shows from the other side: almost no pair on it is close enough to cross, and
one adjacent pair exchanges.

Where the candidate set can be enumerated, recall decomposes exactly into coverage, selection and
ranking, and enumerability makes the first term an exhaustive measurement rather than a sample
estimate. We release the protocol, the audited split, the frozen predictions of every method
compared here, and the analysis code.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Machine learning is used to predict the chemistry that a molecule will undergo. Given a drug, a
model predicts the metabolites into which enzymes will convert it; given a target compound, a model
predicts the reactants from which it can be synthesised; given a design objective, a model proposes
molecules that meet it. All three tasks are evaluated in the same way. A method proposes a ranked
list of structures, a curated reference records the structures that are correct, and the method is
scored by how often the two agree.

Whether two structures agree is not self-evident. A single compound can be drawn in several ways
that a chemist reads as one substance: as either of two tautomers, which differ in the position of a
mobile hydrogen; with or without the three-dimensional arrangement at a stereocentre; as any of
several equivalent strings. An evaluation must therefore fix a rule that decides when a predicted
structure counts as the reference structure. It must also fix how many candidates a method may
propose, because a method that proposes more will match more. Where predictions are generated by
applying reaction templates, it must fix the convention by which a template is matched against a
molecule, because a template that names a hydrogen explicitly will not match a structure whose
hydrogens are implicit. And it must fix which molecules the test set contains. These four choices
are made in every published evaluation and stated in almost none.

That evaluation choices can change conclusions is established in several fields. Standardising the
comparison procedure has reversed a published ordering of retrosynthesis models
\citep{Maziarz_2025}; re-scoring frozen submissions under a different notion of equivalence has
changed which system leads in text-to-SQL \citep{Zhong_2020} and in question answering
\citep{Kamalloo_2023}; the choice of aggregate inverts the winner in drug-response prediction
\citep{agarwal2026metric}.
What is not established is how far any single choice moves a ranking when it is the only thing that
changes, and in particular whether a choice can \emph{reorder} a leaderboard rather than shifting
every system on it by a similar amount. The distinction matters, because a shift leaves conclusions
intact and a reordering does not.

We answer that question by re-scoring predictions that other groups have already released, so that
nothing is retrained and no method is reimplemented. Two of the four choices reorder a published leaderboard
and the reorderings are certified as interactions between method and choice, not asserted from a
change in rank; a third changes a reported quantity, what a rule base reaches, without changing any
ordering. The fourth is undeclared but produces no interaction that survives correction, and separating those two statements is possible only because the same test is
applied to all four. A single condition governs when a reordering occurs: the choice must move two
systems apart by more than the margin between them, which makes a benchmark more vulnerable as its
leading methods converge.

The deepest case study is metabolite prediction, where the effect of a choice can be measured
against a quantity that is itself exact. In that setting the set of candidates a rule-based method
could ever propose is finite and can be enumerated, so the best score the method could achieve with
perfect selection and ranking is not estimated but computed. Recall then decomposes exactly into
what the rule base reaches, what selection retains of that, and what ranking returns of what
remains, which turns a single score that admits no diagnosis into an attribution of the shortfall to
a stage.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt \parskip0pt
\item A coverage figure is a property of a rule set together with the procedure that applies it,
  and is not comparable across papers that leave the procedure unstated. How far a published rule
  base reaches moves by up to $0.353$ with a preprocessing step neither paper reports, and moves in
  opposite directions for banks written in opposite conventions; which of the two reaches further
  does not change. Three quarters of what that step first appeared to be worth was an application
  loop left unfinished, readable from the rule file without running anything.
\item The matching criterion exchanges positions in a published leaderboard of retrosynthesis
  systems, on predictions their authors released. The exchanges are certified as method-by-criterion
  interactions and corrected for multiplicity across the whole family of tests.
\item The output budget reorders a metabolite leaderboard on its own, with every method and its
  ranking held fixed.
\item The part of a published ranking that no declared choice can contradict is a partial order,
  computable from released prediction files alone. On three leaderboards in two fields roughly a
  third of the pairwise orderings belong to it, and on one of them none do.
\item Which population a dataset name refers to is ambiguous in released work, and the ambiguity is
  not shown to reorder anything: eleven released prediction files sit on three test sets, and no
  interaction that varies only the population survives correction.
\item An evaluation protocol, an audited split, the frozen predictions of every method compared, a
  checker that reports these choices from files alone, and the analysis code are released. Where the
  candidate set is enumerable, the coverage term of the decomposition is measured exhaustively,
  which no sampled or search-bounded setting supplies.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of this article is organised as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews prior work
on the four choices and on staged evaluation, and Section~\ref{sec:tame} defines the protocol, the
matching criteria and the split. Section~\ref{sec:enginereach} shows that what a published rule base
reaches is decided by the engine applying it, and surveys how far that dependence extends across
released template libraries. Section~\ref{sec:robust} asks what part of a published ranking survives
every choice in a declared grid, on released retrosynthesis predictions.
Section~\ref{sec:decomp} develops the decomposition that makes a coverage figure measurable, and
Section~\ref{sec:results} applies the remaining choices inside one benchmark.
Section~\ref{sec:limitations} states the scope of the results and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}
concludes.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{The domain, and what has been compared in it.} Rule-based metabolite prediction was
established by SyGMa \citep{Ridder_2008}, which encodes phase I and phase II biotransformations as
reaction templates and scores products by the empirical frequency with which each template yields an
observed metabolite. GLORYx \citep{de_Bruyn_Kops_2020} couples a comparable library to a learned
model of sites of metabolism; BioTransformer \citep{Djoumbou_Feunang_2019} extends templates to
microbial and environmental transformations; MetaTrans \citep{Litsa_2020} and MetaPredictor
\citep{Zhu_2024} dispense with templates for sequence-to-sequence translation and a transformer with
a filtering stage. Comparative studies recover the same coverage-precision trade-off: five
predictors on 85 agrochemical parents \citep{Scholz_2023}, six tools on 37 chemicals where the
broadest-coverage system also produced the most incorrect predictions \citep{Boyce_2022}, human
radiolabelled ADME data \citep{Gao_2026}. Each fixes one criterion for a correct prediction, and none reports how sensitive its conclusion is to that choice. That one structure can be written
more than one way is documented independently of this domain: \citet{Dhaked_2020} catalogue
tautomeric transformations that the standard InChI identifier normalises only a subset of,
\citet{Hahnke_2018} report roughly $60\%$ of PubChem records differing from their canonical InChI
representation, and \citet{Mansouri_2024} describe a standardisation pipeline for
structure-activity modelling. That literature treats standardisation as preprocessing applied
before an analysis, not as a parameter of the analysis.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Evaluation choices in adjacent fields.} None of the four choices is in doubt in general.
On the matching rule, \citet{Maziarz_2025} re-evaluated retrosynthesis models under a standardised
procedure and reversed a published ordering, in part because one model had been scored under a
relaxed stereochemistry criterion that its baselines were not given; \citet{Hastedt_2024} release
the ranked predictions of twelve such systems, which makes comparisons possible without
reimplementation; and \citet{liu2026massspecgymwild} identify divergent metric implementations and
inconsistent stereochemistry handling in published mass-spectrometry work. Outside chemistry,
re-scoring frozen submissions under a different equivalence rule misrepresents the state of the art
in text-to-SQL \citep{Zhong_2020} and changes which question-answering system leads
\citep{Kamalloo_2023}. On the aggregate, the choice of metric inverts the winner in drug-response
prediction \citep{agarwal2026metric}, the standard score in structure-based drug design rises with
the atom count of the output instead of its quality \citep{Gao_2025_reframing}, which motivated
rebuilding that benchmark \citep{Lin_2025_cbgbench}, and weighting examples by difficulty reorders
natural-language leaderboards \citep{Mishra_2021, Rodriguez_2021}. On the output budget the relevant
literature is older, having long evaluated ranked output at a declared cut-off
\citep{Jarvelin_2002}, under incomplete relevance judgements \citep{Buckley_2004}, corrected for
recording propensity \citep{Joachims_2017}, and with the winner of a cut-off metric changing with
how the candidates entering it are counted \citep{Krichene_2020}.

\paragraph{Staged evaluation.} Separating what a candidate generator reaches from what a later
stage retains is standard practice: cascade ranking prunes stage by stage \citep{Wang_2011_cascade},
recommendation is built and evaluated as candidate generation followed by ranking
\citep{Covington_2016}, open-domain question answering reports retriever recall at a budget beside
end-task accuracy \citep{Karpukhin_2020}, and \citet{Maziarz_2025} separate single-step accuracy
from search. In each of these the first term is estimated from a sample or bounded by a search
budget.

\paragraph{What is left unestablished.} The demonstrations above compare studies that differed in
several respects at once, so the effect of any one choice is not identified. Nor is it known what
the first term of a staged decomposition can be when the candidate set is finite and enumerable
rather than sampled, or whether a coverage figure obtained that way is comparable across papers.
The present work addresses both.

\section{The evaluation protocol: what is declared and what is held fixed}
\label{sec:tame}

The protocol used throughout is a matching specification, an audited split and a re-scoring
harness. It is referred to below as \textsc{tame}, for tautomer-aware matched evaluation, and it
fixes what every number in \S\ref{sec:results} is measured under.

\paragraph{The matching criterion.} The field has never agreed what counts as a match. GLORYx uses a stereo-blind InChIKey skeleton,
MetaTrans compares Morgan fingerprints, and rule-based systems that canonicalise their own output,
SyGMa among them, effectively compare canonical SMILES. Each is a different equivalence relation,
and each is applied silently. \textsc{tame} states all five as named equivalence relations: predicted and reference
structures are mapped through one of
\[
q \in \{\textsc{canonical},\ \textsc{inchikey},\ \textsc{inchi-no-stereo},\ \textsc{tanimoto1},\
\textsc{inchikey-tautomer}\}
\]
and a prediction counts as a hit iff $q(\hat{y}) = q(y)$ for some reference $y$. Each map is computed with RDKit 2022.09.5, the version that reproduces
the columns reported here. A ceiling
reported under this protocol declares the \emph{application} procedure too: the engine and its
version, sanitisation, the enumeration and filtering policy, and the depth (\S\ref{sec:enginereach}).
\textsc{canonical} is canonical SMILES equality, \textsc{inchikey} the full InChIKey and
\textsc{inchi-no-stereo} its first block, blind to stereochemistry, charge and isotopes. One caution attaches to the first. Canonical SMILES equality is
computed without stereochemistry on the metabolite corpus, whose references carry none, and with it
in the two cross-domain harnesses, whose references do: the same rule applied to corpora that differ
in what they record, so the heading means the stricter thing wherever stereochemistry is present.
That is why the cross-domain step to \textsc{inchi-no-stereo} moves those systems and the
metabolite step cannot.

 \textsc{tanimoto1} is Tanimoto similarity of one over Morgan fingerprints at radius 2
over 2048 bits; \textsc{inchikey-tautomer} standardises both sides before hashing, taking the
fragment parent, neutralising charges, canonicalising tautomers and dropping stereochemistry.

All five criteria are reported for every comparison. The default for this corpus is
\textsc{inchikey-tautomer}, chosen because the references neither carry stereochemistry nor
distinguish tautomers, and because standard InChI normalises only a subset of tautomers
\citep{Dhaked_2020}: an engine that emits the keto form of an enol reference fails to match under
plain \textsc{inchikey} while producing the same substance. This default is the most permissive of the five and blind to
stereochemistry as well as tautomerism, which also suits methods that standardise their output
without stereocentres and so cannot emit one. Standardisation of
this kind is established preprocessing; adopting it as the \emph{matching} rule is not.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Split and leakage audit.} All splits are substrate-disjoint, and a separate read-only
audit canonicalises every SMILES and verifies zero substrate and zero positive-pair overlap across
each split pair. Leakage-aware splitting is not novel, since DataSAIL \citep{Joeres_2025} minimises
cross-split similarity on comparable tasks; the leak that matters here is substrate-metabolite
\emph{identity} overlap, specific to this domain.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Metrics and inference.} Results are reported as recall@$k$ together with the mean output
size. Precision alone is unsuitable under incomplete annotation, where an unannotated but real
metabolite counts as a false positive; recall alone is unsuitable because a method can raise it by
emitting more (Appendix~\ref{app:budget}). Three comparisons are confirmatory and each is corrected for
multiplicity: the differential criterion sensitivity of \S\ref{sec:rankflip}, the learned-against-prior
gap of \S\ref{sec:xmc}, and the cross-domain interaction, whose $448$ tests form one family. All remaining intervals are exploratory. A difference is called
\emph{certified} below when its paired interval excludes zero and it survives Holm correction in its
declared family; others are called observed. Model and configuration selection is on validation data, with two
exceptions stated in Appendix~\ref{app:arch}.

\section{What a rule base reaches depends on the engine that applies it}
\label{sec:enginereach}

\looseness=-1
A paper that publishes a rule base usually publishes a figure for what it covers: the fraction of
reference products that applying every rule recovers, its \emph{reach}. It is quoted as a property
of the rules and it is not one, because applying a rule requires deciding how the substrate is
presented to the matcher, and that decision is made silently by every engine and reported by none.

\looseness=-1
$152$ of SyGMa's $175$ rules sit verbatim in ours, so those can be held fixed while only the
procedure changes: a depth-1 enumeration that expands the substrate with explicit hydrogens against
SyGMa's own, which does not. On the clean test split they reach $0.200$ $[0.182,0.218]$ under the
first and $0.387$ $[0.365,0.410]$ under the second, a paired $+0.190$ $[+0.173,+0.208]$. Most of
that is not the convention. Expanding a substrate and never contracting the product leaves the drawn
hydrogen on the reacting atom; one call restores it, lifting the expanded arm to $0.342$
$[0.320,0.365]$, worth $+0.142$ $[+0.125,+0.160]$, and what survives against the unexpanded arm is
$+0.047$ $[+0.038,+0.057]$. Three quarters of the apparent effect is a missing call and a quarter
the convention, both certified. The whole distance between the two
configurations is $+0.339$: the engine term as the two programs stand, $+0.188$ $[+0.170,+0.205]$,
$+0.145$ $[+0.128,+0.162]$ from the $23$ rules our bank lacks, and $+0.0065$ $[+0.0038,+0.0096]$
from composing steps.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{The two parts fail in opposite ways.} The larger is not a matching effect: the
templates fire equally often expanded, and the toolkit then refuses $63\%$ of the fragments those
products separate into, against $0.4\%$ without the expansion, because a template written against
implicit hydrogens does not name the hydrogen it replaces and the drawn one is never removed.
Contract the product and that number is zero. The smaller is a matching effect and nothing else: the
completed loop yields $20{,}601$ fragments against the unexpanded arm's $21{,}106$, so a substrate
carrying its hydrogens matches a little less. The convention decides what a bank reaches without deciding which reaches
further. With the loop complete, BioTransformer recovers $1{,}235$ references with hydrogens drawn
and $317$ with them implicit, a swing of $-0.353$; SyGMa recovers $1{,}260$ and $1{,}390$, a swing
of $+0.050$ the other way. A coverage figure is the value of a bank together with a convention, not
comparable across papers that state only the bank. It is not an exchange: SyGMa leads in both arms
once the loop is complete.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{What is convention-dependent is a construct, not a domain.} Which \textsc{smarts} constructs an
expansion touches is readable from a rule file: counts that already include the hydrogens about to
be drawn are inert, counts of explicit connections or of implicit hydrogens lose every match, and
the hydrogen \emph{atom} primitive and the wildcard fire more often rather than less. Across six
independent libraries and $51{,}368$ templates the construct almost everyone uses is the inert one,
and the two that move are used by disjoint sets of libraries. Every one of the $42{,}554$ patterns
in the standard retrosynthesis template library pins a connection count and none carries the atom
primitive, so the same step costs that library $82\%$ of the template--product pairs it can match at
all: a match lost before a product is built, applicability rather than coverage, and a hazard latent
in that field rather than an error in it. The convention travels with the transcription: of the
rules one release attributes to another tool, the $57$ copied verbatim carry the atom primitive not
once, while $88$ of the $121$ re-typed do, absent from all $175$ rules of the tool they came from.

\looseness=-1
A third turned up inside our own measurement rather than in another group's library. The three ways
this bites, with one measured instance each, are these; the second is a defect and the third is
repairable, so only the first has to be declared rather than fixed
(Appendix~\ref{app:retroconvention}).

\section{What part of a published ranking is safe to quote}
\label{sec:robust}\label{sec:xdomainmain}

\looseness=-1
A leaderboard reports a total order, computed in one cell of a grid of choices its paper does not
report because the cell is not thought of as a parameter. Fix the items and the predictions and ask
of each pair whether the ordering the table asserts holds in every other cell too. Say the higher system \emph{dominates} the lower when the margin is positive in every cell, and
that the domination is \emph{certified} when a paired interval excludes zero in every cell.
Domination is the intersection of the per-cell orders, so it is transitive and is the finest
relation no declared choice can contradict; certified domination is a sub-relation of it, reported
separately because an interval condition is not transitive.

\looseness=-1
The share that dominate is what a reader can quote without naming the cell, and it is computable
from released prediction files alone: nothing retrained, nothing the authors did not publish.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
leaderboard & grid & dominate & certified \\
\midrule
retrosynthesis, seven systems & $4$ criteria $\times$ $4$ budgets & $8$ of $21$ & $7$ \\
retrosynthesis, three systems & $4$ criteria $\times$ $4$ budgets & $0$ of $3$  & $0$ \\
metabolites, four methods     & $9$ budgets                       & $2$ of $6$  & $2$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\looseness=-1
About a third in each case. The remainder divides into two kinds the instrument separates: pairs
some cell reverses, and pairs no cell reverses but the benchmark cannot resolve at its own sample
size. Only one of the twenty-one on the seven-system leaderboard is of the second kind, so there the
loss is reversal rather than resolution.

\paragraph{How one choice breaks an ordering.}
\looseness=-1
\citet{Hastedt_2024} released the ranked predictions of twelve retrosynthesis systems, of which
eleven parse. Reading those files rather than the accompanying table shows that they were produced
on three different test sets (Appendix~\ref{app:xdomainsets}). Within the largest group sharing one,
seven systems scored on their $4{,}999$ common reactions change places at top-1, on predictions that
never move and under a rule applied identically to all seven. Under canonical matching the leaders
run GraphRetro $0.5377$, LocalRetro $0.5339$, Retroformer $0.5325$, Graph2SMILES $0.5293$; disregard
stereochemistry and they run Graph2SMILES $0.5571$, Retroformer $0.5541$, GraphRetro $0.5501$,
LocalRetro $0.5469$, so fourth becomes first. Five of twenty-one pairs exchange in all.

\looseness=-1
An exchange of positions describes the data; the claim is the interaction, corrected across the
largest family declared in this work. That family is the grid the two axes admit, twenty-one system pairs
by six pairs drawn from the four conventions by four budgets, which is $504$ cells less the $56$ in
which two conventions return identical results and no test exists. Of the $448$ that remain, $233$ have intervals excluding zero and $109$ survive
Holm at $0.05$. The effect is concentrated at top-1, the number this field leads with but not the
whole accuracy curve, and stereo-blind matching is a candidate convention here and not a straw one:
GLORYx scores on a stereo-blind skeleton, and GRAIL and SyGMa cannot emit a stereocentre under any
criterion (Figure~\ref{fig:xdomain}, Appendix~\ref{app:xdomain}).

\looseness=-1
\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Most of these margins were never resolvable.} Since the predictions are frozen, a paired
bootstrap gives an interval on each margin directly. \textbf{Eleven of the twenty-one margins do not
separate from zero, and five of the six adjacent margins in the published order do not.} The
smallest margin this benchmark certifies is $0.0146$ against a criterion movement of $0.015$, and
the three-system group's narrowest is $0.0213$, so
what the criterion exchanges are pairs the benchmark never separated, and where a separation is
certified it erases the gap instead of reversing it.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Which population a familiar name refers to.} Two of the three groups share $490$
reactions nested inside each group's own five thousand, so two systems can be compared on the subset
and on the whole with method, criterion and budget fixed. Of the $380$ comparisons this gives, $44$
change the ordering; on the metabolite split the same test gives $10$ of $60$. Neither instance
survives correction: none of the $440$ interactions passes Holm at $0.05$. The null is bounded rather
than absolute, since at the family's own threshold and $80\%$ power these designs would have detected
an interaction of about $0.10$ on the released files and $0.07$ on the metabolite split, an order of
magnitude larger than the criterion interactions that do survive (Appendix~\ref{app:popaxis}).

\section{The decomposition and the coverage ceiling}
\label{sec:decomp}

\looseness=-1
The deep case study runs on GRAIL, a metabolite predictor built for this work: a learned selector
chooses which of its \nbank\ rules to apply, an engine applies them, and a learned filter ranks the
result. A rule-grounded system of this form is the setting in which a coverage figure can be
measured exhaustively rather than sampled.

\looseness=-1
A rule-grounded predictor reaches a reference only by passing three gates in sequence: the
transformation must be \emph{expressible} by some rule, that rule must be \emph{selected} into the
substrate's pool under a budget, and the product must be \emph{ranked} into the top $k$. Writing
$C_{\text{full}}$ for the references the whole bank recovers without a budget, $C_{\text{bud}}$ for
those surviving the selector, and $H$ for those returned:
\[
\underbrace{\tfrac{|H|}{|U|}}_{\text{recall@}k}
=\underbrace{\tfrac{|C_{\text{full}}|}{|U|}}_{\text{coverage}}
\cdot\underbrace{\tfrac{|C_{\text{bud}}|}{|C_{\text{full}}|}}_{\text{selection}}
\cdot\underbrace{\tfrac{|H|}{|C_{\text{bud}}|}}_{\text{ranking}},
\qquad |U|=\textstyle\sum_{s\in S}|Y_s| .
\]
Each set contains the next, so the identity is exact: an accounting device, not a model. It is
written in \emph{micro} because only that form multiplies out exactly, head-to-head comparisons use
\emph{macro}, and every figure states which. The first factor needs an enumerable candidate set,
which a bank supplies and a wide decode approximates: MetaPredictor factorises
$0.612\times0.804\times1.000$, exactly its realised recall, though a beam bounds that decode rather
than the model (Appendix~\ref{app:seq2seq}).

\paragraph{The ceiling as a primitive.} Apply all \nbank\ rules to every test substrate and ask what
fraction of references appears anywhere in the union. That \textbf{coverage ceiling} is the best
recall achievable if selection and ranking were perfect, and on the clean test split it is \ceiling\
(cluster-bootstrap 95\% CI $[0.794,0.839]$); the macro counterpart is $\ceilingmacro$. It should be
reported alongside recall for any rule-grounded method, and reported as system reach in the sense of
\S\ref{sec:enginereach}, since it is a property of bank and engine together.

\paragraph{What the decomposition says.}\looseness=-1 The deployed pipeline converts only $32.5\%$ of its own ceiling into realised recall, drawn as a waterfall beside SyGMa's in Appendix~\ref{app:formal}. Selection is where the loss sits: barely a third of what the bank reaches survives the selector's rule budget, while ranking is close to lossless at $0.960$ $[0.941,0.976]$. It does not bind alone: the output cap binds on $308$ of \ntest\ substrates and all $29$ references lost between the budgeted pool and the emitted output fall on those (Appendix~\ref{app:props}). Removing selection entirely tests that reading: ranking the whole pool with no selector, the bank passes SyGMa by $0.167$ $[0.112,0.223]$ at $k=82$ and is indistinguishable at the field's $k=15$. Applied to a second method the reading stops being self-diagnosis: SyGMa decomposes on the same split with the inverse profile, converting nearly all of a narrower reach where we convert a third of a wider one (Appendices~\ref{app:noselect} and~\ref{app:decompsygma}).

\subsection{The same axis inside one benchmark, where the ceiling is measured}
\label{sec:rankflip}

\looseness=-2
Published methods have their \emph{frozen} predictions re-scored under all five criteria, so only
the match rule changes across columns and never the chemistry. GRAIL, SyGMa \citep{Ridder_2008} and
MetaPredictor \citep{Zhu_2024} are compared on the whole clean test split, paired on identical
substrates (Table~\ref{tab:matchsens}); BioTransformer \citep{Djoumbou_Feunang_2019} and MetaTrans
\citep{Litsa_2020} on the $\nshared$-substrate shared subset.

\looseness=-1
Methods are not equally sensitive to the convention. Over the full split GRAIL gains $0.016$
$[0.010,0.023]$ from canonical to tautomer matching against MetaPredictor's $0.040$
$[0.030,0.050]$, a differential of $+0.024$ $[0.014,0.034]$, which survives Holm--Bonferroni
over the three method pairs and over the thirty cells the rule declared for the other families,
every pair by every criterion step, admits here. Unequal headroom runs against the effect: GRAIL enters lower, has more left
to recover, and gains the less. Output size is a second and orthogonal confound, SyGMa emitting $81$
candidates per substrate against GRAIL's $8.4$ (Appendix~\ref{app:budget}).

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Primary endpoint.} The endpoint is the differential sensitivity, the interaction between
criterion and method, estimated by a paired bootstrap over substrates with $10{,}000$ resamples.
Every method gains under a more tolerant criterion, so only the differential can change an ordering.
On the $\nshared$-substrate subset two pairs separate along two criterion steps, BioTransformer
gaining \interA\ \interAci\ more than GRAIL from canonical to tautomer matching and SyGMa
\interB\ \interBci\ more than MetaTrans from canonical to InChIKey; the first survives correction
at that family size and the second does not. The criterion is therefore a \textbf{method-dependent
confounder}, not a neutral scoring choice. The declared families, every raw and adjusted
$p$-value, and what was fixed in advance against what was not, are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:holm}. The design was specified before the data; the families were declared after
it, and are declared conservatively as the grid the axes admit.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{External replication.} The analysis is repeated on the field's de-facto external
hold-out, the \nglory\ GLORYx drugs \citep{de_Bruyn_Kops_2020}. It is external for three of the four
methods and not for ours: $24$ of the $37$ are in GRAIL's train or validation split, so its row uses
the $13$ unseen.

\looseness=-1
The result this section rests on involves neither contaminated arm. Under strict InChIKey matching
MetaPredictor trails SyGMa by $0.130$, and one rung later the advantage is gone. The endpoint is the
interaction rather than that transition: MetaPredictor gains $+0.135$ $[+0.072,+0.209]$ more than
SyGMa across the stereo step, rejected at either family size, and rejected again by a sign-flip test
that assumes no distribution. A criterion therefore erases a significant advantage here instead of
reversing it, since the relaxed gap covers zero while still admitting SyGMa ahead by $0.071$
(Appendix~\ref{app:holm}).



\paragraph{The criterion moves human agreement.} The strongest evidence for declaring the criterion
involves no model. Twenty-five external drugs are curated independently by two sources, and on the
same drugs the two expert lists agree at a Jaccard of $0.145$ under strict \textsc{inchikey}
matching and at $0.406$ under the tautomer-aware default, so notation accounts for a third of what
reads as disagreement about observed chemistry and most of it survives the relaxation. The two curations also bound what a recall figure
means, since one records only $0.539$ of the other and beyond that level recall measures one
source's inclusion choices (Appendix~\ref{app:xdomain}).

\paragraph{Mechanism.} Which relaxation dominates is a property of the reference set: here stereochemistry alone, and internally, where references are already stereo-stripped, tautomerism instead. The effect requires references that carry representational variety and a leaderboard whose margins are narrower than the differential it produces.


\subsection{The aggregate and the budget are one variable, and the decomposition predicts it}
\label{sec:metric}

\looseness=-1
Intervals in this subsection are exploratory except the budget sweep, whose family is declared below. A method that emits eighty candidates to catch a
few more references is rewarded by recall and punished by F1, and on each method's deployed output
set the two orderings disagree. Against GRAIL the disagreement is certified in both directions:
SyGMa leads on recall by $0.253$ $[0.222,0.283]$ and sits $0.088$ $[0.076,0.099]$ below it on F1.
Against MetaPredictor only the F1 direction is certified, at $0.113$ $[0.107,0.120]$, while SyGMa's
recall lead of $0.023$ rests on an interval bounded at zero and is bought by emitting $80.7$
candidates against $10.8$.

\looseness=-1
The budget does the same work, and it is what identifies the variable. Truncating a ranked list
moves the emitted count with the method and its ranking held fixed, so a reordering along the sweep
cannot be a property of any one system. MetaPredictor's count has been moved the other way with the
model fixed too, $10.9$ distinct candidates to $34.3$ at a wider beam (Appendix~\ref{app:seq2seq}). It
reorders without any budget having to be chosen: swept over $k$ in macro F1 the three methods take
three distinct orderings, SyGMa above GRAIL at every budget up to $13$, certified from $k=1$ to
$k=10$ and by $0.071$ $[0.057,0.086]$ at $k=5$, and below from $14$ on, where the field's $15$ sits,
that second half observed rather than certified. A
fourth method exhausts the field: on $291$ substrates carrying all four, MetaTox at $33.6$
candidates is last at $k\le5$ and first from $k=15$, the budget this field reports at. Four of the
six pairs change sign along that sweep and three change it between margins that each separate from
zero and survive Holm over the grid that axis admits. Its lead is volume: on macro F1 at that same
budget MetaTox is second at $0.125$ behind MetaPredictor's $0.156$, and at $k=1$ it is last at
$0.072$ against SyGMa's $0.239$. The F1 ordering changes along the sweep too
(Appendix~\ref{app:fourmethod}). A budget binds a method only when it emits more, so at $k=15$ SyGMa is cut on $92.7\%$ of
substrates and GRAIL on none (Appendix~\ref{app:props}).

\looseness=-1
Every certified reversal along the sweep involves the one method that overproduces, and none
certifies among methods emitting alike, which is a failure to detect: those pairs leave intervals up
to $0.158$ open. The matching rule acts independently of the budget: matched at about
eight emitted candidates, GRAIL's sensitivity sits below both comparators by $-0.0195$
$[-0.029,-0.010]$ and $-0.025$ $[-0.035,-0.015]$, so the criterion separates methods at fixed
output size (Appendix~\ref{app:matchedemission}).

\looseness=-1
The decomposition says why: SyGMa's selection factor is $1$ (\S\ref{sec:decomp}), which is what
recall rewards and precision punishes, so a pipeline with no selection stage is scored relatively
higher by the first than by the second. The matching criterion acts on these metrics as well, and it
acts where it barely touched recall.
The F1 gap between GRAIL and MetaPredictor is certified under four of the five criteria, at $-0.026$
$[-0.038,-0.013]$ under the tautomer-aware default, and is indistinguishable from zero under plain
\textsc{inchikey}. That is an erased gap and not a reversal, and it shows that the matching rule and
the output size do not act on the same comparisons (Appendix~\ref{app:props}).





\looseness=-1
\paragraph{A global budget is the wrong object.} The budget is not only undeclared, it is the wrong shape.
Enzymes compete for one substrate, so its annotated products are alternatives from a shared pool:
the median substrate here has one and the mean $2.22$, while the deployed policy emits $8.4$ for
every substrate alike. Holding the ranking fixed and changing only how many are emitted,
the deployed $k=15$ scores $0.136$ macro F1, the best global constant $0.195$, and truncating each
substrate at its own annotated count $0.214$. A rule that reads the pool instead of a constant recovers
almost all of that: emitting while a candidate scores within half the leader's score gives $0.209$,
a paired $+0.074$ $[+0.059,+0.089]$ over the deployed policy and above every global constant. Forecasting the count from the substrate alone fails and is reported as such
(Appendix~\ref{app:negative}): what the pool supports is a relative rule, which is what a model
normalised over siblings would learn. The rule's advantage over a constant is not uniform. On the
$845$ substrates that are parent drugs it reaches $0.236$ against the best constant's $0.215$; on
the $325$ that are themselves metabolites of another substrate in the same split it reaches $0.140$
against $0.141$, so where the model is weaker its score distribution says less about how many to
emit.

\paragraph{Where this system's own shortfall sits.} GRAIL trails both published comparators at
the field's budget, by a paired $-0.242$ $[-0.271,\,-0.212]$ against SyGMa. The second confirmatory
comparison locates that shortfall in selection: a learned selector reaches $0.266$ where rule-firing
frequency alone reaches $0.410$ through the same enumeration and filter, a paired $-0.144$
$[-0.196,\,-0.095]$ (Appendix~\ref{app:props}).

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\looseness=-2
\paragraph{What the metabolite numbers bound.} The five-method table rests on $\nshared$ shared
substrates of frozen predictions, because MetaTrans's pipeline no longer reproduces and
BioTransformer's configuration is unscripted; the other three are scored on the full $\ntest$. An
unannotated but real product counts as a false positive, so every precision and F1 figure is
pessimistic, depth-2 application lifts the ceiling by ${\sim}0.012$ at $8.5$ times the cost, so
$\ceiling$ bounds this formulation rather than the task, and the figure reported for this system is
a single evaluation of the deployed configuration. Its rule labels are built with the substrate
expanded and its rules are fired without the expansion, so the two agree on about half the
rule--substrate positives, and the reading that locates the shortfall in selection is conditioned
on that (Appendix~\ref{app:enginereach}).

\looseness=-2
\paragraph{What the newer results rest on.} The cross-domain numbers come from files produced
elsewhere, the reordering there is a top-1 phenomenon, the robust order is measured on three
leaderboards, and the population result is a bounded null.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\looseness=-2
Four choices are settled before any model is compared: when two structures count as the same
molecule, how many candidates a method may emit, how an engine applies a reaction template, and
which population a dataset name refers to. None is routinely declared, all four can be read from
files that are already public, and the part of a published ranking that survives every one of them
is about a third. Two move a published ordering and they break it differently: the budget reverses
margins an interval separates from zero, the criterion exchanges systems a mature benchmark never
separated. Declaring them is what the evidence supports; the budget supports more, since a set sized
to the candidate pool beats every global budget on the same predictions.

\subsubsection*{Reproducibility statement}

Every quantity reported here derives from a committed artifact and regenerates from the released
material, and the checks that establish this are released as executable scripts. One of them is
intended for prediction files produced elsewhere: given a template bank it reports which templates
depend on the hydrogen convention and whether the bank mixes conventions, and given two or more
prediction files it reports whether they were produced on the same population and how far apart
their output sizes are. It requires no reference data and no trained model, and applied to the
eleven released retrosynthesis files it recovers the three test sets in seconds.

The cost of regeneration differs by analysis. Every split-level result regenerates from the
committed artifacts alone, which comprise the frozen predictions, the matching-key tables and the
per-substrate records. The ablations that rank or re-select candidates additionally require the
trained checkpoints, which the anonymised archive contains together with the frozen predictions of
every method compared, the audits, the evaluation harness, the split construction and the analysis
code.

Three constraints bear on exact, not approximate reproduction. One result depends on a
third-party distribution that is not redistributed here: the reach of BioTransformer's template set
is computed from the $994$ SMIRKS shipped with that tool, which an installed copy supplies; its
weights and those of MetaPredictor are likewise third-party and are cited rather than shipped.
Results measured on a subsample of a split record the cap and the seed that drew it, without which
the substrate set is not recoverable, since sampling proceeds without replacement and a different
cap yields a different set of the same size, not a subset. Confidence intervals are
regenerable as well: per-substrate rows are sorted before resampling, so a repeated run reproduces
an artifact exactly.

Two standing reconciliations guard the analyses against the code they analyse. Where an analysis
path re-derives a deployed output, the two are verified equal before use, and the released
per-candidate score dump reproduces the deployed ranking on all $\ntest$ substrates. Where a
reimplemented loop stands in for the deployed one, it is gated against a figure the deployed loop
produced, at the scale at which the claim is made.

Every set treated here as external or third-party has been audited for overlap with the training and
validation substrates. The GLORYx set overlaps on $24$ of its $37$ drugs, and \S\ref{sec:rankflip}
reports GRAIL on the $13$ that do not overlap; the $\nshared$-substrate shared subset overlaps on
none of its $150$. The audit is released as a script.
