\begin{abstract}
A position on a leaderboard is decided in part by choices no paper declares. Four of them are
measured here one at a time, in molecular prediction: the rule deciding when two structures denote
the same molecule, the number of candidates a method may emit, the convention by which a rule engine
applies reaction templates, and the population a familiar dataset name refers to. Every effect below
is measured on frozen predictions, retraining nothing and varying only the choice under study.

Each is a cell of a grid that leaderboards are computed in and papers do not report, so a published
ordering can be asked a question it is never asked: does it hold in every other cell? The pairs for
which it does are what can be quoted without naming the cell. Most of a leaderboard turns out never
to have been resolved at all: eleven of the twenty-one pairwise orderings on a seven-system
retrosynthesis table carry margins its own benchmark cannot separate. Of the ten it does separate,
seven tenths survive every cell. Across twenty-three leaderboards in four domains and $1{,}922$
pairs the share runs from none of three to nine tenths, so it is a property of a leaderboard and not
a constant; summed, those leaderboards publish $293$ places and what survives supports $176$. Which pairs are at risk needs no pairwise statistic: two systems
exchange under a change of criterion exactly when it moves them apart by more than the larger of the
two margins it connects, so scoring each system once in the alternative cell settles every pair by
arithmetic, and on the $11{,}794$ comparisons this paper spans in five domains the condition holds
on the $653$ that exchange and on nothing else. Exposure is a ratio and not a stage of maturity: the board
here with the widest margins is not the one least at risk.
Two of the four choices change a published ordering and they break it differently. The output budget
reverses margins a paired interval separates from zero: one method is last at tight budgets and
first at the budget the field reports, with its ranking held fixed. The matching criterion instead
exchanges positions the benchmark never separated, inside the largest group of the eleven released
retrosynthesis prediction files that shares a test set, seven systems at the top-1 accuracy those
tables lead with, and where a separation does
hold it erases the gap rather than reversing it. The engine convention decides no ordering but
decides what a rule bank reaches: one published base loses three quarters of its references when the
hydrogens it was written for are withdrawn, so a coverage figure is the value of a bank together
with a convention. The repair we registered for it, dispatching the convention per
template, fails on the only mixed published bank there is to test it on; what survives is a bound,
the least a bank recovers over the settings it might run under, a quarter of the figure one of them
reports. The fourth choice is two questions, one a bounded null and one not: which released test set a
familiar name refers to is undeclared and no interaction varying only that survives correction,
while a single split is part parent drugs and part their own metabolites, and how much a method
loses between the two differs by method by more than a paired interval admits.

Where the candidate set can be enumerated, recall decomposes exactly into coverage, selection and
ranking, and enumerability makes the first term exhaustive rather than an estimate. We release the
protocol, the audited split, the frozen predictions of every method compared, and the analysis
code.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Machine learning is used to predict the chemistry a molecule will undergo: the metabolites enzymes
will convert a drug into, the reactants a target compound can be made from, the molecules that meet
a design objective. All three are evaluated the same way. A method proposes a ranked list of
structures, a curated reference records which are correct, and the method is scored by how often the
two agree.

Whether two structures agree is not self-evident. A single compound can be drawn in several ways
that a chemist reads as one substance: as either of two tautomers, which differ in the position of a
mobile hydrogen; with or without the three-dimensional arrangement at a stereocentre; as any of
several equivalent strings. An evaluation must therefore fix a rule that decides when a predicted
structure counts as the reference structure. It must also fix how many candidates a method may
propose, because a method that proposes more will match more. Where predictions are generated by
applying reaction templates, it must fix the convention by which a template is matched against a
molecule, because a template that names a hydrogen explicitly will not match a structure whose
hydrogens are implicit. And it must fix which molecules the test set contains. These four choices
are made in every published evaluation and stated in almost none.

That evaluation choices can change conclusions is established in several fields
\citep{Maziarz_2025, Zhong_2020, Kamalloo_2023, agarwal2026metric}. What is not established is how
far any single choice moves a ranking when it is the only thing that changes, and in particular
whether a choice can \emph{reorder} a leaderboard rather than shifting every system on it by a
similar amount. The distinction matters, because a shift leaves conclusions intact and a reordering
does not.

We answer that question by re-scoring predictions that other groups have already released, so that
nothing is retrained and no method is reimplemented. Two of the four choices reorder a published leaderboard
and the reorderings are certified as interactions between method and choice, not asserted from a
change in rank; a third changes a reported quantity, what a rule base reaches, without changing any
ordering. The fourth is undeclared but produces no interaction that survives correction, and separating those two statements is possible only because the same test is
applied to all four. A single condition governs when a reordering occurs: the choice must move two
systems apart by more than the margin between them, which makes a benchmark more vulnerable as its
leading methods converge.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt \parskip0pt
\item A coverage figure is a property of a rule set together with the procedure that applies it, and
  is not comparable across papers that leave the procedure unstated: reach moves by up to $0.374$
  with a preprocessing step neither paper reports, and in opposite directions for banks written in
  opposite conventions. A registered repair, dispatching that step per template, is measured and
  fails; what is comparable is the least a bank recovers over the settings it might be run under,
  which for one published bank is a quarter of the figure it reports.
\item The matching criterion exchanges positions in a published leaderboard of retrosynthesis
  systems, on predictions their authors released. The exchanges are certified as method-by-criterion
  interactions and corrected for multiplicity across the whole family of tests.
\item The output budget reorders a metabolite leaderboard on its own, with every method and its
  ranking held fixed.
\item The part of a published ranking that no declared choice can contradict is a partial order,
  computable from released prediction files alone, together with its longest chain. Measured on
  $23$ leaderboards in four domains and $1{,}922$ pairs: they publish $293$ places and what survives
  supports $176$. Because it is an intersection over cells it can only shrink as a grid widens, so it
  is reported as a curve over the declared axes rather than as one number.
\item Which pairs a criterion exchanges is not estimated but decided: it exchanges a pair exactly
  when it moves the two apart by more than the larger of the margins it connects, so scoring each
  system once in the alternative cell settles every pair without a paired statistic. On the
  $11{,}794$ comparisons here the condition holds on the $653$ that exchange and on nothing else,
  and exposure is the ratio of movement to gap rather than the size of either.
\item Which population a dataset name refers to is ambiguous in released work and is not shown to
  reorder anything: eleven released prediction files sit on three test sets, and no interaction
  varying only the population survives correction. A protocol, an audited split, the frozen
  predictions of every method compared, a checker that reports these choices from files alone, and
  the analysis code are released.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{The domain, and what has been compared in it.} Rule-based metabolite prediction was
established by SyGMa \citep{Ridder_2008}, which encodes phase I and phase II biotransformations as
templates and scores products by how often each template yields an observed metabolite. GLORYx
\citep{de_Bruyn_Kops_2020} couples a comparable library to a learned model of sites of metabolism;
BioTransformer \citep{Djoumbou_Feunang_2019} extends templates to microbial and environmental
transformations; MetaTrans \citep{Litsa_2020} and MetaPredictor \citep{Zhu_2024} dispense with
templates altogether. Comparative studies recover the same coverage-precision trade-off on 85
agrochemical parents \citep{Scholz_2023}, on 37 chemicals where the broadest-coverage system also
produced the most incorrect predictions \citep{Boyce_2022}, and on radiolabelled ADME data
\citep{Gao_2026}; each fixes one criterion for a correct prediction and none reports how sensitive
its conclusion is to that choice. That one structure can be written more than one way is documented
independently of this domain: \citet{Dhaked_2020} catalogue tautomeric transformations the standard
identifier normalises only a subset of, \citet{Hahnke_2018} report roughly $60\%$ of PubChem records
differing from their canonical InChI, and \citet{Mansouri_2024} describe a standardisation pipeline.
That literature treats standardisation as preprocessing applied before an analysis, not as a
parameter of the analysis.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Evaluation choices in adjacent fields.} None of the four choices is in doubt in general.
On the matching rule, \citet{Maziarz_2025} reversed a published retrosynthesis ordering under a
standardised procedure, in part because one model had been scored under a relaxed stereochemistry
criterion its baselines were not given; \citet{Hastedt_2024} release the ranked predictions of
twelve such systems; \citet{liu2026massspecgymwild} find divergent metric implementations in
mass spectrometry; and outside chemistry a different equivalence rule misrepresents the state of the
art in text-to-SQL \citep{Zhong_2020} and changes which question-answering system leads
\citep{Kamalloo_2023}. On the aggregate, the metric inverts the winner in drug-response prediction
\citep{agarwal2026metric}, the standard score in structure-based drug design rises with output atom
count rather than quality \citep{Gao_2025_reframing}, which motivated rebuilding that benchmark
\citep{Lin_2025_cbgbench}, and weighting by difficulty reorders natural-language leaderboards
\citep{Mishra_2021, Rodriguez_2021}. On the output budget the literature is older: ranked output
evaluated at a declared cut-off \citep{Jarvelin_2002}, under incomplete judgements
\citep{Buckley_2004}, corrected for recording propensity \citep{Joachims_2017}, and a cut-off
metric whose winner changes with how the candidates entering it are counted \citep{Krichene_2020}.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Sweeping the choices rather than fixing them.} Computing a result in every cell of a
declared grid of defensible analytic choices and reporting what survives all of them is multiverse
analysis \citep{Steegen_2016} and specification-curve analysis \citep{Simonsohn_2020}; the
machine-learning counterparts account for benchmark variance \citep{Bouthillier_2021} and test a
claim jointly across datasets \citep{Dror_2017}. Those designs sweep an \emph{estimate}.
Section~\ref{sec:robust} sweeps an \emph{order}, whose intersection over cells is a partial order
rather than a distribution, so what survives has a share and a longest chain, both computable from
released prediction files. Closest in aim is work that perturbs a leaderboard and reports how far it
moves: \citet{Knowles_2021} resamples the human evaluation behind the WMT ranking, and
\citet{Jin_2026} corrects the annotations of two text-to-SQL benchmarks and reports shifts of up to
nine places. Both establish that a ranking moves. Neither returns an object a reader can quote in
its place, nor a rule that says in advance which pairs will move.

\paragraph{Staged evaluation, and what is left unestablished.} Separating what a candidate generator
reaches from what a later stage retains is standard \citep{Wang_2011_cascade, Covington_2016,
Karpukhin_2020, Maziarz_2025}, and in each case the first term is estimated from a sample or bounded
by a search budget. The demonstrations above compare studies differing in several respects at once,
so the effect of any one choice is not identified; nor is it known what the first term can be when
the candidate set is finite and enumerable, or whether a coverage figure obtained that way is
comparable across papers. The present work addresses both.

\section{The evaluation protocol: what is declared and what is held fixed}
\label{sec:tame}

The protocol used throughout is a matching specification, an audited split and a re-scoring
harness. It is referred to below as \textsc{tame}, for tautomer-aware matched evaluation, and it
fixes what every number in \S\ref{sec:results} is measured under.

\paragraph{The matching criterion.} The field has never agreed what counts as a match. GLORYx uses a
stereo-blind InChIKey skeleton, MetaTrans compares Morgan fingerprints, and rule-based systems that
canonicalise their own output compare canonical SMILES, each silently. \textsc{tame} names all five
and maps both sides through one of $q \in \{\textsc{canonical}, \textsc{inchikey},
\textsc{inchi-no-stereo}, \textsc{tanimoto1}, \textsc{inchikey-tautomer}\}$, a prediction counting as
a hit iff $q(\hat{y}) = q(y)$ for some reference $y$, under RDKit 2022.09.5. They are canonical SMILES
equality; the full InChIKey; its first block, blind to stereochemistry, charge and isotopes;
Tanimoto similarity of one over Morgan fingerprints at radius 2 over 2048 bits; and standardising
both sides before hashing. A ceiling reported here declares the \emph{application} procedure too:
engine and version, sanitisation, enumeration and filtering policy, and depth
(\S\ref{sec:enginereach}). One caution attaches to the first, which is computed without
stereochemistry on the metabolite corpus, whose references carry none, and with it in the
cross-domain harnesses, whose references do; that is why the cross-domain step to
\textsc{inchi-no-stereo} moves those systems and the metabolite step cannot. The fourth is a
similarity threshold rather than an identity relation and is reported but never treated as a
candidate convention (Appendix~\ref{app:robust}).

All five are reported for every comparison. The default here is \textsc{inchikey-tautomer}, because
the references neither carry stereochemistry nor distinguish tautomers and standard InChI normalises
only a subset of them \citep{Dhaked_2020}: an engine emitting the keto form of an enol reference
fails to match under plain \textsc{inchikey} while producing the same substance. Standardisation of
this kind is established preprocessing; adopting it as the \emph{matching} rule is not.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Split and leakage audit.} All splits are substrate-disjoint, and a read-only audit
canonicalises every SMILES and verifies zero substrate and zero positive-pair overlap across each
split pair. Leakage-aware splitting is not novel \citep{Joeres_2025}; the leak that matters here is
substrate-metabolite \emph{identity} overlap, specific to this domain.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Metrics and inference.} Results are reported as recall@$k$ together with the mean output
size. Precision alone is unsuitable under incomplete annotation, where an unannotated but real
metabolite counts as a false positive; recall alone is unsuitable because a method can raise it by
emitting more (Appendix~\ref{app:budget}).

\looseness=-1
Two words are used throughout and are not interchangeable. A difference is \emph{separated} when its
paired bootstrap interval excludes zero, which needs no family and is the only claim made about an
exploratory quantity. It is \emph{certified} when it is separated \emph{and} survives Holm
correction inside a family fixed by a rule stated before the correction was computed, the grid the
declared axes admit, and not by which comparisons proved worth reporting; no family here was
pre-registered and Appendix~\ref{app:holm} says so. Three comparisons are confirmatory: the
differential criterion sensitivity of \S\ref{sec:rankflip}, the learned-against-prior gap of
\S\ref{sec:xmc}, and the cross-domain interaction, whose $448$ tests form one family. The word is used of those three and of any sentence
naming its own correction and family size, which two appendix families do, and nowhere else; a
released check permits exactly those forms. Two further families are corrected without being
confirmatory, the $54$-cell budget sweep and the $440$ population interactions, and are called
corrected rather than certified. Every remaining interval is exploratory, and every use of either
word is listed against its family and adjusted $p$ in Appendix~\ref{app:holm}. Model and
configuration selection is on validation data, with two exceptions stated in
Appendix~\ref{app:arch}.

\section{What a rule base reaches depends on the engine that applies it}
\label{sec:enginereach}

\looseness=-1
A paper that publishes a rule base usually publishes a figure for what it covers, the fraction of
reference products applying every rule recovers: its \emph{reach}. It is quoted as a property of the
rules and is not one: applying a rule means deciding how the substrate is presented to the matcher,
and every engine decides that silently and none reports it.

\looseness=-1
$152$ of SyGMa's $175$ rules sit verbatim in ours, so those can be held fixed while only the
procedure changes: one engine, one bank, the substrate expanded with explicit hydrogens or not. On
the clean test split those two arms reach $0.200$ $[0.182,0.218]$ and $0.390$ $[0.367,0.413]$, a
paired $+0.190$ $[+0.173,+0.208]$, and almost none of that is the convention. SyGMa's own program on the same rules
reaches $0.387$, so the switch and not the reimplementation moves this. Expanding a substrate and never contracting the product leaves the
drawn hydrogen on the reacting atom; contracting it lifts the expanded arm to $0.377$
$[0.354,0.400]$, worth $+0.177$ $[+0.159,+0.195]$, and what survives against the unexpanded arm is
$+0.013$ $[+0.007,+0.020]$. Thirteen fourteenths of it is an unfinished loop and the
remainder the convention, both separated. The whole distance between the two configurations is $+0.339$: the
engine term as the two programs stand, $+0.188$ $[+0.170,+0.205]$, $+0.145$ $[+0.128,+0.162]$ from
the $23$ rules our bank lacks, and $+0.0065$ $[+0.0038,+0.0096]$ from composing steps.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{The two parts fail in opposite ways.} The larger is not a matching effect: the templates
fire equally often expanded, and the toolkit then refuses $63\%$ of the fragments those products
separate into against $0.4\%$ without the expansion, because a template written against implicit
hydrogens does not name the hydrogen it replaces and the drawn one is never removed. Contract the
product and that number is zero. The smaller is a matching effect and nothing else, the completed
loop yielding $21{,}302$ fragments against the unexpanded arm's $21{,}106$. The convention then
decides what a bank reaches without deciding which reaches further: with the loop complete
BioTransformer recovers $1{,}289$ references with hydrogens drawn against $317$ with them implicit,
a swing of $-0.374$, while SyGMa recovers $1{,}350$ against $1{,}390$, a swing of $+0.015$ the other
way, and SyGMa leads in both. A coverage figure is the value of a bank together with a convention
and is not comparable across papers that state only the bank.

\looseness=-1
The repair we registered for this works on one bank of three and on neither published one. Dispatch
the convention per template rather than per run: a one-line syntactic test on each rule, whether its
reactant side names a hydrogen \emph{atom}, decides which presentation that rule sees, consulting no
outcome. On SyGMa it is the identity, none of whose $175$ templates names one, which is the
structural null the design needs. On BioTransformer, whose $726$ of $994$ templates make it the
mixed bank by that syntax, it \emph{loses} to the better single setting by $-0.0038$
$[-0.0067,-0.0015]$. It clears that setting only on our own bank, by $+0.0035$ $[+0.0015,+0.0058]$,
whose $675$ dispatched templates were written by hand. A bank can be mixed in its syntax and
single-convention in what it reaches, and the one case where dispatch pays is the bank whose
provenance we control.

\looseness=-1
What is comparable without that is a bound, and it is what a reader of a coverage figure quoted
without its procedure may assume: the least a bank recovers over the settings it might run under.
For BioTransformer that is $0.1221$ against the $0.4963$ its best single setting reaches, a published
figure four times what any one convention guarantees.

\looseness=-1
What a bank has to lose is readable from its rule file without running anything, and the hazard is
not confined to this domain: every one of the $42{,}554$ patterns in the standard retrosynthesis
template library pins a connection count, so the same step costs that library $82\%$ of the
template--product pairs it can match at all (Appendix~\ref{app:retroconvention}).



\section{What part of a published ranking is safe to quote}
\label{sec:robust}\label{sec:xdomainmain}

\looseness=-1
A leaderboard reports a total order computed in one cell of a grid its paper does not report. Fix
the items and the predictions and ask of each pair whether the ordering the table asserts holds in
every other cell. The higher system \emph{dominates} the lower when the margin is positive in every
cell, and is \emph{separated in every cell} when a paired interval excludes zero in each. Domination
is the intersection of the per-cell orders, so it is transitive and is the finest relation no
declared choice can contradict; separation is a sub-relation reported apart, an interval condition
not being transitive.

\looseness=-2
A pair that does not dominate has failed in one of two ways that should not be added together. Some
cell may order the two the other way with an interval excluding zero, a choice deciding against the
published table; or none may, leaving an ordering that fails on a margin the benchmark cannot
resolve. Only the first is \emph{contested}, so noise on a tie is not a reversal. All twenty-three run a grid of four
criteria crossed with a second axis their own field leaves undeclared: the budget a table is read at,
whether a docked pose is minimised first, whether an error score is floored, whether a human score is
standardised within the annotator who gave it (Appendix~\ref{app:robust}).

\looseness=-2
The last three columns are the ones to take away, because most of what a leaderboard prints was
never resolved at all: eleven of the seven-system table's twenty-one pairs carry margins its own
benchmark cannot separate before any choice is varied. Across all twenty-three leaderboards the share runs
from none of three to nine tenths with a median of $0.85$, so it is not a constant of the field and
none is reported. Summed, they publish $293$ places and what survives supports $176$. The longest chain is the readable
form of the same measurement: seven published places support $3$ tiers, resampling to $[3,3]$;
nineteen support $11$; and the same seven systems take $4$ total orders across $16$ cells. The system that table places first is not in the top
tier of what survives; the one it places second is the only system that is
(Figure~\ref{fig:hasse}). Docking and translation are controls: docking's field published its
reversal already \citep{Buttenschoen_2024} and the grid recovers it, while the translation tables,
whose ranking is human and whose scoring convention ships as a function, contest $14$ pairs of which
none survives correction. What does not survive on the docking board includes its own top pair. Because domination is an
intersection, all of this can only shrink as cells are added, so each axis is also reported alone
(Appendix~\ref{app:robust}).

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Which pairs are at risk follows from an inequality, and it is an equivalence.} Two
systems exchange under a change of criterion exactly when the change moves them apart by more than
the larger of the two margins it connects: with $d$ their margin in the published cell, $d'$ their
margin in the other and $\delta_a,\delta_b$ how far each system moves, $d'=d+(\delta_a-\delta_b)$ and
the sign survives iff $|\delta_a-\delta_b|\le\max(|d|,|d'|)$. Against $|d|$ alone it is necessary and not
sufficient, and the difference is not a technicality: over the $11{,}794$ method-pair by criterion-pair
comparisons this paper spans in five domains the one-sided form flags $3{,}602$ where $653$ exchange,
and the two-sided form flags the $653$ and nothing else. The $n$ systems are therefore scored once in the other
cell and every one of the $n(n-1)/2$ pairs is settled exactly, with no paired statistic; a resample
is needed only for whether an exchange clears zero. A typical movement for the domain in place of the
per-system one over-predicts fragility by a third and is reported as a failure
(Appendix~\ref{app:negative}). Run on the twenty-three tables it flags
$295$ of their $1{,}922$ pairs, which are exactly the $295$ some cell reverses. Where the flags fall says which benchmarks are exposed, and neither
the gap nor the movement decides alone: the twenty-four tables run from $0\%$ flagged to $15.9\%$, and
the widest-gapped is not the safest. A benchmark is exposed when its entries converge faster than the
conventions separating them, a ratio evaluable before any convention is varied
(Appendix~\ref{app:xdomain}).

\paragraph{How one choice breaks an ordering.} \looseness=-1
\citet{Hastedt_2024} released the ranked predictions of twelve retrosynthesis systems, of which
eleven parse, produced on three different test sets (Appendix~\ref{app:xdomainsets}). Within the
largest group, seven systems on their $4{,}999$ common reactions change places at top-1 on
predictions that never move: five of twenty-one pairs exchange, and disregarding stereochemistry
takes the fourth-placed system to first. An exchange describes the data; the claim is the
interaction, corrected across the largest family declared in this work, the grid the two axes admit.
Of its $448$ testable cells, $233$ have intervals excluding zero and $109$ survive Holm at $0.05$,
concentrated at top-1, the number this field leads with (Appendix~\ref{app:xdomain}).

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Most of these margins were never resolvable.} Since the predictions are frozen, a paired
bootstrap gives an interval on each margin directly. \textbf{Eleven of the twenty-one margins do not
separate from zero, and five of the six adjacent margins in the published order do not.} The
smallest margin this benchmark separates is $0.0146$ against a criterion movement of $0.015$, and
the three-system group's narrowest is $0.0213$, so
what the criterion exchanges are pairs the benchmark never separated, and where a separation is
separated it erases the gap instead of reversing it.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{One split, two populations.} A benchmark can mix populations without changing its name.
Of the \ntest\ substrates here $845$ are parent drugs and $325$ are annotated products of another
substrate in the split, so one question is about a compound a patient swallows and the other about
what is left of it, averaged into one number. Methods do not treat them alike: GRAIL loses $0.152$
$[0.101,0.202]$ of recall between the two while MetaPredictor loses $0.001$ $[-0.053,0.057]$, an
interaction of $+0.150$ $[+0.084,+0.216]$ and against SyGMa $+0.116$ $[+0.051,+0.181]$. No paper
reporting on this split reports the mixture. The other population question is a null: which released
test set a familiar name refers to changes an ordering in $44$ of $380$ comparisons and $10$ of $60$
on the metabolite split, and none of the $440$ interactions survives Holm at $0.05$, in a design
whose blindest test would see $0.116$ and $0.202$ at $80\%$ power (Appendix~\ref{app:popaxis}).

\section{The decomposition and the coverage ceiling}
\label{sec:decomp}

\looseness=-1
The deep case study runs on GRAIL, a metabolite predictor built for this work: a learned selector
chooses which of its \nbank\ rules to apply, an engine applies them, and a learned filter ranks the
result. A system of this shape is where a coverage figure can be measured exhaustively rather than
sampled, because it reaches a reference only by passing three gates in sequence. With
$C_{\text{full}}$ the references the whole bank recovers without a budget, $C_{\text{bud}}$ those
surviving the selector and $H$ those returned,
\[
\underbrace{\tfrac{|H|}{|U|}}_{\text{recall@}k}
=\underbrace{\tfrac{|C_{\text{full}}|}{|U|}}_{\text{coverage}}
\cdot\underbrace{\tfrac{|C_{\text{bud}}|}{|C_{\text{full}}|}}_{\text{selection}}
\cdot\underbrace{\tfrac{|H|}{|C_{\text{bud}}|}}_{\text{ranking}},
\qquad |U|=\textstyle\sum_{s\in S}|Y_s| ,
\]
exact by construction because each set contains the next: an accounting device, not a model, in
\emph{micro} because only that form multiplies out, with head-to-head comparisons in \emph{macro}.
MetaPredictor factorises $0.612\times0.804\times1.000$, exactly its realised recall, though a beam
bounds that decode rather than the model (Appendix~\ref{app:seq2seq}).

\paragraph{The ceiling as a primitive.} Apply all \nbank\ rules to every test substrate and ask what
fraction of references appears anywhere in the union. That \textbf{coverage ceiling} is the best
recall perfect selection and ranking would reach, here \ceiling\ (cluster-bootstrap 95\% CI
$[0.794,0.839]$), macro $\ceilingmacro$, and should be reported alongside recall for any
rule-grounded method, as system reach in the sense of \S\ref{sec:enginereach}. The deployed pipeline
converts $32.5\%$ of its own ceiling, and selection is where the loss sits, barely a third of what
the bank reaches surviving the rule budget. Ranking is close to lossless at $0.960$ $[0.941,0.976]$
and close to free: re-ranking the same pool by Tanimoto similarity to the substrate, nothing
trained, reaches $0.342$ against the filter's $0.340$, $+0.002$ $[-0.004,+0.009]$, while reversing
that order costs $0.040$ and shuffling it $0.015$. The filter has found the scaffold and little past
it. Applied to a second method the decomposition stops being self-diagnosis, since SyGMa decomposes
with the inverse profile (Appendices~\ref{app:noselect} and~\ref{app:decompsygma}).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The same axis inside one benchmark, where the ceiling is measured}
\label{sec:rankflip}

\looseness=-2
Published methods have their \emph{frozen} predictions re-scored under all five criteria, so only
the match rule changes across columns and never the chemistry. GRAIL, SyGMa \citep{Ridder_2008} and
MetaPredictor \citep{Zhu_2024} are compared on the whole clean test split, paired on identical
substrates (Table~\ref{tab:matchsens}); BioTransformer \citep{Djoumbou_Feunang_2019} and MetaTrans
\citep{Litsa_2020} on the $\nshared$-substrate shared subset.

\looseness=-1
Methods are not equally sensitive to the convention. Over the full split GRAIL gains $0.016$
$[0.010,0.023]$ from canonical to tautomer matching against MetaPredictor's $0.040$
$[0.030,0.050]$, a differential of $+0.024$ $[0.014,0.034]$, which survives Holm--Bonferroni
over the three method pairs and over the thirty cells the rule declared for the other families,
every pair by every criterion step, admits here. Unequal headroom runs against the effect: GRAIL enters lower, has more left
to recover, and gains the less. Output size is a second and orthogonal confound, SyGMa emitting $81$
candidates per substrate against GRAIL's $8.4$ (Appendix~\ref{app:budget}).

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Primary endpoint.} The endpoint is the interaction between criterion and method, by a
paired bootstrap over substrates with $10{,}000$ resamples; every method gains under a more tolerant
criterion, so only the differential can change an ordering. On the $\nshared$-substrate subset two

curations of twenty-five external drugs agree at a Jaccard of $0.145$ under strict

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{External replication.} On the field's de-facto external hold-out, the \nglory\ GLORYx
drugs \citep{de_Bruyn_Kops_2020}, the result rests on neither contaminated arm: $24$ of the $37$ sit
in GRAIL's own train or validation split, so its row uses the $13$ unseen. Under strict InChIKey
matching MetaPredictor trails SyGMa by $0.130$ and one rung later the advantage is gone. The
endpoint is the interaction: MetaPredictor gains $+0.135$ $[+0.072,+0.209]$ more than SyGMa across
the stereo step, rejected at either family size and again by a sign-flip test. The criterion erases
a significant advantage instead of reversing it, the relaxed gap covering zero while still admitting
SyGMa ahead by $0.071$ (Appendix~\ref{app:holm}).





\subsection{The aggregate and the budget are one variable, and the decomposition predicts it}
\label{sec:metric}

\looseness=-1
Intervals here are exploratory except the budget sweep, whose family is declared below. A method
emitting eighty candidates to catch a few more references is rewarded by recall and punished by F1,
so on deployed output the two aggregates order the methods differently: against GRAIL the
disagreement is separated both ways, SyGMa leading on recall by $0.253$ $[0.222,0.283]$ and sitting
$0.088$ $[0.076,0.099]$ below on F1 (Appendix~\ref{app:props}).

\looseness=-1
The budget does the same work and it is what identifies the variable, because truncating a ranked
list moves the emitted count with the method and its ranking held fixed, so a reordering along the
sweep cannot be a property of any one system. Swept over $k$ in macro F1 the three methods take
three distinct orderings: SyGMa sits above GRAIL at every budget up to $13$, separated from $k=1$ to
$k=10$ and by $0.071$ $[0.057,0.086]$ at $k=5$, and below from $14$ on, where the field's $15$ sits.
A fourth method exhausts the field. On $291$ substrates carrying all four, MetaTox at $33.6$
candidates is last at $k\le5$ and first from $k=15$; four of the six pairs change sign along that
sweep and three do so between margins that each separate from zero and survive Holm over the
$54$-cell grid that axis admits. Its lead is volume, since on macro F1 at the same budget it is
second at $0.125$ behind MetaPredictor's $0.156$ and last at $k=1$
(Appendices~\ref{app:fourmethod} and~\ref{app:props}).

\looseness=-1
Every corrected reversal along the sweep involves the one method that overproduces, and none
survives correction among methods emitting alike, which is a failure to detect rather than a null:
those pairs leave intervals up to $0.158$ open. The two choices act on different comparisons. The
matching rule still separates methods at fixed output size, GRAIL's sensitivity sitting below both
comparators by $-0.0195$ $[-0.029,-0.010]$ and $-0.025$ $[-0.035,-0.015]$ when all are matched at
about eight emitted candidates (Appendix~\ref{app:matchedemission}); and where the budget reorders,
the criterion instead erases, the GRAIL--MetaPredictor F1 gap being separated under four of the five
criteria at $-0.026$ $[-0.038,-0.013]$ and indistinguishable from zero under plain
\textsc{inchikey}. The decomposition says why the aggregate splits them at all: SyGMa's selection
factor is $1$ (\S\ref{sec:decomp}), so a pipeline with no selection stage is scored relatively
higher by recall than by precision (Appendix~\ref{app:props}).


\looseness=-1
\paragraph{A global budget is the wrong object.} It is not only undeclared, it is the wrong shape.
Enzymes compete for one substrate, so its annotated products are alternatives from a shared pool:
the median substrate here has one and the mean $2.22$, while the deployed policy emits $8.4$ for
every substrate alike. Holding the ranking fixed and changing only how many are emitted, the
deployed $k=15$ scores $0.136$ macro F1, the best global constant $0.195$, and truncating each
substrate at its own annotated count $0.214$. Emitting while a candidate scores within half the
leader's score gives $0.209$, and the arm that decides the claim is not the deployed policy this
paragraph has just discredited but the best constant, chosen with the same hindsight on the same
split: $+0.0147$ $[+0.0059,+0.0237]$ of an available $+0.019$. Forecasting that count directly is
worse than the constant it replaces, so what the pool supports is a relative rule. The advantage
holds on the $845$ parent drugs, $+0.021$ $[+0.010,+0.032]$, and not on the $325$ substrates that
are themselves metabolites, $-0.002$ $[-0.016,+0.013]$ (Appendices~\ref{app:emission}
and~\ref{app:negative}).


\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\looseness=-2
\paragraph{What the metabolite numbers bound.} The five-method table rests on $\nshared$ shared
substrates of frozen predictions, because MetaTrans's pipeline no longer reproduces and
BioTransformer's configuration is unscripted; the other three are scored on the full $\ntest$. An
unannotated but real product counts as a false positive, so every precision and F1 figure is
pessimistic by a bounded amount the annotation graph measures (Appendix~\ref{app:closure}). Depth-2
application lifts the ceiling by ${\sim}0.012$ at $8.5$ times the cost, so $\ceiling$ bounds this
formulation and not the task; and this system's rule labels are built with the substrate expanded
while its rules fire without it, so locating its shortfall in selection is conditioned on a mismatch
of about half the rule--substrate positives (Appendix~\ref{app:enginereach}).

\looseness=-2
The cross-domain numbers come from files produced elsewhere, the reordering there is a top-1
phenomenon, the robust order rests on twenty-three leaderboards in four domains, and two results are bounded nulls.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\looseness=-2
Four choices are settled before any model is compared and none is routinely declared. What a
published ranking entitles a reader to quote is smaller than the ranking in two ways: most of its
pairs were never resolved, and some that were are reversed by a choice no paper reports. Two of the
four choices change a published ordering --- the budget reverses margins an interval separates from
zero, the criterion exchanges systems a mature benchmark never separated --- and which pairs either
reaches follows from an equivalence. Declaring is not the whole remedy, as the one bank of mixed
provenance here shows.

\subsubsection*{Reproducibility statement}

Every quantity reported here derives from a committed artifact and regenerates from the released
material, and the checks that establish this are released as executable scripts. One of them is
intended for prediction files produced elsewhere: given a template bank it reports which templates
depend on the hydrogen convention and whether the bank mixes conventions, and given two or more
prediction files it reports whether they were produced on the same population and how far apart
their output sizes are. It requires no reference data and no trained model, and applied to the
eleven released retrosynthesis files it recovers the three test sets in seconds.

The cost of regeneration differs by analysis. Every split-level result regenerates from the
committed artifacts alone, which comprise the frozen predictions, the matching-key tables and the
per-substrate records. The ablations that rank or re-select candidates additionally require the
trained checkpoints, which the anonymised archive contains together with the frozen predictions of
every method compared, the audits, the evaluation harness, the split construction and the analysis
code.

Three constraints bear on exact, not approximate reproduction. One result depends on a
third-party distribution that is not redistributed here: the reach of BioTransformer's template set
is computed from the $994$ SMIRKS shipped with that tool, which an installed copy supplies; its
weights and those of MetaPredictor are likewise third-party and are cited rather than shipped.
Results measured on a subsample of a split record the cap and the seed that drew it, without which
the substrate set is not recoverable, since sampling proceeds without replacement and a different
cap yields a different set of the same size, not a subset. Confidence intervals are
regenerable as well: per-substrate rows are sorted before resampling, so a repeated run reproduces
an artifact exactly.

Two standing reconciliations guard the analyses against the code they analyse. Where an analysis
path re-derives a deployed output, the two are verified equal before use, and the released
per-candidate score dump reproduces the deployed ranking on all $\ntest$ substrates. Where a
reimplemented loop stands in for the deployed one, it is gated against a figure the deployed loop
produced, at the scale at which the claim is made.

Every set treated here as external or third-party has been audited for overlap with the training and
validation substrates. The GLORYx set overlaps on $24$ of its $37$ drugs, and \S\ref{sec:rankflip}
reports GRAIL on the $13$ that do not overlap; the $\nshared$-substrate shared subset overlaps on
none of its $150$. The audit is released as a script.
