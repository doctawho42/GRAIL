\begin{abstract}
A position on a molecular machine-learning leaderboard is decided partly by choices no paper
declares: the rule for when two structures are the same molecule, how many candidates a method may
emit, and --- wherever reaction templates are involved --- how the engine applying them spells a
hydrogen. We measure all three on frozen predictions: nothing is retrained and only the convention
varies. Re-scoring eleven published retrosynthesis systems on the test sets they actually share, the
criterion moves systems by certifiably different amounts in both groups and reorders the tighter
one: among seven separated by a median $0.013$, a system fourth under canonical matching is first
under stereo-blind matching, five of twenty-one pairs exchange and $109$ of $448$ paired
interactions survive Holm; among three separated by $0.047$, none exchange. Reordering needs a
leaderboard packed tighter than the criterion's differential effect --- which is what a benchmark
looks like as it matures.

A single recall number cannot say which of three failure modes is responsible. Where the rule base
can be enumerated it factorises \emph{exactly} into coverage, selection and ranking, and
enumerability makes the first factor an exhaustive measurement rather than a sample estimate or a
beam bound. On metabolite prediction this explains a leaderboard instead of restating one: the wider
system reaches \ceiling\ against $0.539$ and converts a third of it, while the narrower converts
nearly all of its own, having no selection stage to lose anything at.

The first factor is not a property of the rule base. Holding the $152$ rules two banks share fixed
and swapping only the engine moves what they reach by $+0.188$ $[+0.170,+0.205]$, as large as the
whole gap between the banks, and it is one line of preprocessing neither paper reports. What depends
on the convention is a class of construct rather than a domain, with three mechanisms and one
instance each: a primitive counting explicit structure breaks the match before any product exists
(all $42{,}554$ patterns of a standard retrosynthesis library); a rewrite not naming the hydrogen it
consumes breaks the product instead; and a bank assembled from two conventions has no right answer
at all. Only the third is repairable: dispatching per template recovers $+0.006$ $[+0.003,+0.010]$.

A fourth undeclared choice is which population a familiar name means: those eleven files sit on
three disjoint test sets, this paper's own copy of the name is one of the deviations, and changing
only which substrates are in reorders $10$ of $60$ comparisons on our own split. Output
size is the plainest --- every certified reversal runs through the one system emitting $81$
candidates against everyone else's $8$ to $11$. We release the protocol, the split, the frozen
predictions and the checks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A leaderboard in molecular machine learning ranks methods by how often a prediction matches a
reference. Between a set of predictions and a position on it sit choices that no paper declares:
when two structures count as the same molecule, how many candidates a method may emit, how an engine
applies a reaction template, and which population a familiar dataset name refers to. We measure each
on frozen predictions, in two fields, and each decides positions.

Metabolite prediction is where they can be measured \emph{exactly}, which is why the deep case study
is there. When a drug enters the body, enzymes oxidise, cleave and conjugate it into metabolites;
which ones form is a regulatory question and identifying them experimentally is slow. Rule-based
systems encode known biotransformations as templates and rank the products of every applicable one
\citep{Ridder_2008, de_Bruyn_Kops_2020, Djoumbou_Feunang_2019}, learned systems predict the
transformation directly \citep{Litsa_2020, Zhu_2024}, and hybrids use a learned model to choose
templates (\S\ref{sec:related}); all are compared on top-$k$ recall. That comparison confounds three
failure modes --- no rule expresses the transformation, the rule is not selected, the product is
ranked too low --- so a low score identifies no deficiency and suggests no remedy. Where the rule
base can be enumerated, recall factorises exactly into coverage, selection and ranking, the coverage
term obtained by applying the entire bank and asking what it reaches. Applied to two such methods it explains a leaderboard instead of
restating one: the wider system reaches more chemistry and converts a third of it, while the
narrower has no selection stage in which to lose anything and wins for that reason alone. An aggregate counting hits without charging
for the volume emitted is therefore not neutral between architectures, since recall charges the cost
of a selection stage only to architectures that have one. \S\ref{sec:metric} answers that benchmark-design question by changing the aggregate and watching
the leaderboard move as the factorisation predicts.

Making the factorisation well-posed exposes the second problem, which is why the paper addresses it
first. Every factor counts references matched against predictions, so all three move with the rule
deciding when a predicted structure is the same molecule as a reported one, and chemistry offers
several defensible rules disagreeing most about tautomers --- alternative structures of one compound
differing in the position of a mobile hydrogen. Appendix~\ref{app:worked} works one pair of
predictions through all of them, scoring a near-total miss or a full hit with the chemistry fixed.
Evaluations here adopt one each without saying why, and none has varied it; varying it erases a
significant advantage between two published tools. Two more follow: the aggregate and the budget.
What generalises past this benchmark is the decomposition rather than the numbers --- and within it,
not the identity, which is arithmetic and is already used to stage retrieval pipelines
(\S\ref{sec:related}), but what an enumerable candidate set does to it. Recall factorises exactly
wherever that set can be enumerated, and the coverage term then stops being estimated and becomes an
exhaustive measurement rather than a sampled or beam-bounded one, so it is reportable for any such
method, a rule bank or a wide decode alike. Exhaustiveness removes sampling and beam-budget error,
not dependence on the engine applying the rules (\S\ref{sec:decomp}). Adding a sixth method to what
we release needs that method's ranked output and nothing else.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Metabolite-structure prediction.} Rule-based prediction of metabolite structures was
established by SyGMa \citep{Ridder_2008}, which encodes phase I (oxidation, reduction, hydrolysis) and phase II
(conjugation) biotransformations as reaction templates and scores the resulting products by the empirical frequency with which each
template yields an observed metabolite. GLORYx \citep{de_Bruyn_Kops_2020} couples a comparable
template library to a machine-learned model of sites of metabolism, the atoms at which an enzyme acts, and BioTransformer
\citep{Djoumbou_Feunang_2019} extends the template approach to microbial and environmental
transformations. Learned alternatives dispense with templates: MetaTrans \citep{Litsa_2020} treats
metabolite prediction as sequence-to-sequence translation between molecular strings, and
MetaPredictor \citep{Zhu_2024} combines a transformer with a filtering stage. These systems are
evaluated on overlapping but non-identical datasets and, as discussed below, under non-identical
notions of a correct prediction.

\paragraph{Comparative evaluations in this domain.} Several studies have placed these tools side by
side and found the same coverage--precision trade-off: five predictors on 85 agrochemical parents
\citep{Scholz_2023}, six tools on 37 chemicals where the broadest-coverage system also produced the
most incorrect predictions \citep{Boyce_2022}, and human radiolabelled ADME data \citep{Gao_2026}.
Each fixes a single criterion for judging a prediction correct, and none reports how sensitive its
conclusions are to it.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Structure standardisation.} The difficulty of deciding when two molecular structures
denote the same compound is documented independently of metabolite prediction: \citet{Dhaked_2020}
catalogue tautomeric transformations and show the standard InChI identifier normalising only a
subset of them, \citet{Hahnke_2018} report roughly 60\% of PubChem records differing from their
canonical InChI representation, predominantly through tautomer choice, and \citet{Mansouri_2024}
describe a standardisation pipeline for quantitative structure-activity modelling. This literature
treats standardisation as preprocessing applied before analysis rather than as a parameter of the
evaluation itself.

\paragraph{Evaluation methodology in adjacent fields.} \looseness=-1 That methodological choices alter the
apparent ordering of methods is recognised more broadly, along every axis this paper varies.
On the matching rule, \citet{Maziarz_2025} re-evaluated retrosynthesis models under a standardised
procedure and reversed a published ordering, partly because one model had been scored under a
relaxed stereochemistry criterion its baselines did not get; it is the closest precedent here, and
the only one that varies a structure-matching rule. What differs is what is held fixed. There the
ordering moved because two papers had scored under different criteria, and standardising is the
remedy; here every system is scored under every criterion, so the ordering is a function of the
criterion itself, the interaction is certified pairwise and corrected across the family, and
standardising relocates the problem rather than removing it --- some criterion still has to be
chosen. \citet{Hastedt_2024} make that measurable at all by releasing the ranked predictions of
twelve retrosynthesis systems; \S\ref{app:xdomain} re-scores eleven of them and reports what reading
the files also shows, that they sit on three different test sets. \citet{liu2026massspecgymwild} audit published
mass-spectrometry work and name the mechanism directly, divergent metric implementations and
stereochemistry handling in InChIKey matching among them. Outside chemistry, re-scoring frozen submissions under a
different equivalence rule misrepresents the state of the art in text-to-SQL \citep{Zhong_2020} and
changes which question-answering system leads \citep{Kamalloo_2023}. On the aggregate, the choice of
metric inverts the winner in drug-response prediction \citep{agarwal2026metric}; in structure-based
drug design the standard score rises with the output's atom count rather than its quality
\citep{Gao_2025_reframing}, which is why that benchmark was rebuilt around four axes
\citep{Lin_2025_cbgbench}, and weighting examples by difficulty reorders natural-language
leaderboards \citep{Mishra_2021, Rodriguez_2021}. On the budget the prior work is older and not
ours: retrieval and recommendation have long evaluated ranked output at a declared cut-off
\citep{Jarvelin_2002}, under incomplete relevance judgements \citep{Buckley_2004}, corrected for
recording propensity \citep{Joachims_2017}, and shown a cut-off metric's winner changing with how
the candidates entering it are counted \citep{Krichene_2020}.
That an unpenalised recall@$k$ rewards enumeration is a property of the measure that literature
settled decades ago; we re-instantiate it here rather than discover it.

\paragraph{Remaining gap.} \looseness=-1 None of the four axes is in doubt in general, and none has been varied
with everything else held fixed --- neither for metabolite predictors, nor to ask whether a
criterion \emph{reorders} a leaderboard elsewhere rather than shifting it. What is unestablished is
how far each moves a ranking when it is the only thing changing. For the budget the new content is therefore measurement, not the
trade-off. Three distinct orderings appear across the sweep, and at $k=5$ both pairs involving our
own system separate while the other two do not. The budget truncates SyGMa on $92.7\%$ of substrates
and GRAIL's emitted list on none, so the comparison made at it is not budget-matched. And a
per-substrate budget from descriptors recovers almost none of the oracle headroom out of sample,
which bounds that direction rather than refuting it (Appendix~\ref{app:props}). The decomposition is likewise not ours in general, and the priority is
worth conceding as plainly as on the budget axis. Splitting a staged system's recall into what
candidate generation reaches and what a later stage retains is standard practice. Cascade ranking in
retrieval prunes the candidate set stage by stage \citep{Wang_2011_cascade}; recommendation is built
and evaluated as candidate generation followed by ranking \citep{Covington_2016};
\citet{Maziarz_2025} separate single-step accuracy from search in retrosynthesis. Open-domain
question answering reports retriever recall@$k$ beside end-task accuracy \citep{Karpukhin_2020}, at
a retrieval budget rather than over everything the retriever could return. What differs here is not
the identity but what its first factor can be. A sampled corpus quotes first-stage recall at a
budget, and a beam is a budget that widens with compute, so in both the term is estimated or
bounded. A finite enumerable rule bank makes it an exhaustive measurement, which is what makes it
reportable as a ceiling. No
stage-wise attribution of a predictor's score has been reported for metabolite predictors either,
and that is what would let the two be explained rather than only reported.

\section{TAME: an evaluation protocol with a declared matching criterion}
\label{sec:tame}

TAME is a \emph{protocol, an audited split and a re-scoring harness}, not a leaderboard service: the
numbers in \S\ref{sec:results} are comparable and regenerable from committed artifacts.

\paragraph{The matching criterion.} The field has never agreed what counts as a match. GLORYx uses a stereo-blind InChIKey skeleton,
MetaTrans compares Morgan fingerprints, and rule-based systems that canonicalise their own output,
SyGMa among them, effectively compare canonical SMILES. Each is a different equivalence relation,
and each is applied silently. TAME exposes all of them as named quotients: predicted and reference
structures are mapped through one of
\[
q \in \{\textsc{canonical},\ \textsc{inchikey},\ \textsc{inchi-no-stereo},\ \textsc{tanimoto1},\
\textsc{inchikey-tautomer}\}
\]
and a prediction counts as a hit iff $q(\hat{y}) = q(y)$ for some reference $y$. Each map is
computed with RDKit 2022.09.5, the version that reproduces the columns reported here. A ceiling
reported under this protocol declares the \emph{application} procedure too --- engine and version,
sanitisation, enumeration and filtering policy, depth --- since at fixed rules that procedure moves
reach by as much as the reach gap reported here (Appendix~\ref{app:enginereach}).
\textsc{canonical} is canonical SMILES equality, \textsc{inchikey} the full InChIKey and
\textsc{inchi-no-stereo} its first block, the connectivity skeleton blind to stereochemistry, charge
and isotopes; \textsc{tanimoto1} is Tanimoto similarity of one over Morgan fingerprints at radius 2
over 2048 bits; \textsc{inchikey-tautomer} standardises both sides before hashing, taking the
fragment parent, neutralising charges, canonicalising tautomers and dropping stereochemistry.

Our recommendation is conditional on the reference set rather than universal: report all five, and
choose the default by whether the references carry stereochemistry and tautomeric variety. For this
corpus that is \textsc{inchikey-tautomer}. Standard InChI normalises only a
\emph{subset} of tautomers \citep{Dhaked_2020}, so a rule engine emitting a keto form of an enol
reference fails to match under plain \textsc{inchikey} despite producing the same entity. Two
properties of this default bear on every number below: it is the most permissive of the five, and
it is stereo-blind as well as tautomer-blind, so a predicted D-alanine matches an L-alanine
reference. The stereo relaxation is not incidental: several methods here, GRAIL among them,
standardise their output without stereochemistry and cannot emit a stereocentre, so a
stereo-sensitive criterion would score them on a representation they never produce --- which is
also why the choice suits our own system, and why it is conditional rather than defended.

Unless stated otherwise all results use \textsc{inchikey-tautomer};
\S\ref{sec:rankflip} quantifies how much the ranking depends on that choice. Standardisation of this
kind is established preprocessing (\S\ref{sec:related}); what is new here is adopting it as the
\emph{matching} rule.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Split and leakage audit.} All splits are substrate-disjoint: no substrate in test or
validation appears in train, and no test substrate appears in validation. Clean triples are then
audited read-only by a separate script that canonicalises SMILES and verifies zero substrate and
zero positive-pair overlap across every split pair, emitting a machine-checkable report.
Leakage-aware splitting is itself not novel, since DataSAIL \citep{Joeres_2025} minimises
cross-split similarity on MoleculeNet-style tasks; the leak that matters here is
substrate--metabolite \emph{identity} overlap, which is domain-specific. As a secondary check, the deployed configuration
gives macro recall@15 $=0.344$ on validation against $0.334$ on test (293 and 291 substrates). The
$+0.010$ gap matches the seed-to-seed standard deviation, so it corroborates the zero-overlap audit
rather than standing as independent evidence.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Metrics.} We lead with recall@$k$ co-reported with the mean output size, not with
precision alone: under incomplete annotation precision is a \emph{pessimistic lower bound}, an
unannotated but real metabolite scoring as a false positive,
while recall without output size misleads in the other direction, a method being able to buy recall
by enumerating (Appendix~\ref{app:budget}). Three comparisons are confirmatory and each is corrected for multiplicity: the differential
sensitivity of \S\ref{sec:rankflip}, the learned-versus-prior gap of \S\ref{sec:xmc}, and the
cross-domain interaction, whose $448$ paired tests form one family (Appendix~\ref{app:xdomain}).
Every other interval is exploratory. The first two designations are made here; the third was
specified with its run. Selection
happens on validation, with two exceptions we name rather than imply: the deployed filter's encoder
variant, chosen by comparing both on test (Appendix~\ref{app:arch}), which reaches every GRAIL
number here including the headline; and the exploratory analyses of Appendix~\ref{app:negative},
scored on test before this discipline was tightened, which support no claim. Everything else we report on test --- the
decomposition, the learned-versus-prior comparison, the breadth sweep --- is measurement, not
selection.

\section{The decomposition and the coverage ceiling}
\label{sec:decomp}

\looseness=-1
A rule-grounded predictor reaches a reference metabolite only by passing three gates in sequence:
the transformation must be
\emph{expressible} by some rule in GRAIL's bank of \nbank\ reaction rules, that rule must be
\emph{selected} into the substrate's candidate pool under a finite budget, and the resulting
product must be \emph{ranked} into the top $k$. Write, for a substrate set $S$ with reference sets
$Y_s$:
\[
\underbrace{\frac{|H|}{|U|}}_{\text{recall@}k}
\;=\;
\underbrace{\frac{|C_{\text{full}}|}{|U|}}_{\text{coverage}}
\;\cdot\;
\underbrace{\frac{|C_{\text{bud}}|}{|C_{\text{full}}|}}_{\text{selection}}
\;\cdot\;
\underbrace{\frac{|H|}{|C_{\text{bud}}|}}_{\text{ranking}},
\qquad
|U|=\textstyle\sum_{s\in S}|Y_s| ,
\]
where $C_{\text{full}}$ are references recovered by applying the \emph{entire} bank without a
budget, $C_{\text{bud}}$ those surviving the selector's budgeted pool, and $H$ those actually
returned in the top $k$. The first factor requires an enumerable candidate set: the bank for rule-grounded methods, a wide
decode for a sequence-to-sequence one, where the same three gates apply --- MetaPredictor factorises
$0.612\times0.804\times1.000$, exactly its realised recall, though a beam is a budget rather than a
knowledge base and bounds that decode rather than the model (Appendix~\ref{app:seq2seq}). The identity is exact by construction (each set contains the next), so
the three factors partition the loss with no residual: it is an accounting device, not a model. Two
aggregations are in use and every figure is labelled with which: Figure~\ref{fig:decomp} reports
\emph{micro}, the pooled ratio of sums written above, because only that form makes the factors
multiply out exactly; head-to-head comparisons report \emph{macro}, the mean of per-substrate
recalls and the comparator tools' convention. The two differ, $\realised$ against $\grailmacro$
(Appendix~\ref{app:formal}), and coverage moves the same way: the
macro ceiling is the higher, $\ceilingmacro$ against $\ceiling$, so writing the identity in micro understates
the coverage factor rather than flattering it.

\paragraph{The ceiling as a primitive.} The first factor is measurable without any model: apply all
\nbank\ rules to every test substrate and ask what fraction of references appears anywhere in the
union. That \textbf{coverage ceiling} is the best recall achievable if selection and ranking were
both perfect, and on the clean test split it is \ceiling\ (cluster-bootstrap 95\% CI
$[0.794,0.839]$). It should be
reported alongside recall for any rule-grounded method, and as \emph{system} reach --- a
rule set together with a declared application procedure --- rather than as what a knowledge base can
express, since swapping only the engine moves it by as much as the reach gap below: a ceiling quoted
without its engine is not comparable across papers. Inside one system it
is exactly what it claims, and no ranking improvement can exceed it.

\paragraph{What the decomposition says.}\looseness=-1 Figure~\ref{fig:decomp} localises the loss: the deployed
pipeline converts only $32.5\%$ of its own ceiling into realised recall, the three factors separate
with no pair of intervals overlapping (split across selector breadths in
Table~\ref{tab:selection-breadth}), and selection is where the loss sits at this operating point:
barely a third of what the bank can reach survives the selector's rule budget, while ranking is
close to lossless at $0.960$ $[0.941,0.976]$. That third factor is truncation at GRAIL's own output
policy, itself a cap at $k=15$: it emits at the cap on $308$ of \ntest\ substrates ($26.3\%$), and all $29$
references lost between the budgeted pool and the emitted output fall on those. Selection binds
jointly with the cut-off rather than alone.
Widening the selector moves the loss downstream --- what selection recovers, the cut-off gives back
--- so the honest statement is that the deployed \emph{pair} caps the pipeline near $0.41$, macro on
$245$ substrates, against \ceiling\ micro on the full split (Appendix~\ref{app:props}).

\looseness=-1
Removing selection entirely tests that reading rather than restating it. Applying the bank with no
selector and no threshold, ranking the whole pool and truncating at SyGMa's output, the bank passes
SyGMa by $0.167$ $[0.112,0.223]$ at $k=82$ and already by $0.063$ $[0.001,0.125]$ at $k=32$. It is
behind at $k=8$ and indistinguishable at the field's $k=15$, so it converts only above the budget
the convention sets --- \S\ref{sec:metric}'s variable acting for us here where it acts against us
later (Appendix~\ref{app:noselect}).

\looseness=-1
Applied to a second method it stops being self-diagnosis. SyGMa is rule-grounded with an enumerable
bank and decomposes on the same split in the same frame, with the inverse profile. Its reach is the
lower, $0.539$ against $\ceiling$, comparator minus GRAIL throughout. The two are measured through
their own engines and the paired interval on that difference is reported with the engine term it
contains (Appendix~\ref{app:enginereach}) rather than here. But it applies every applicable rule and emits the result, converting almost all of that
reach where we convert a third of a wider one. Its selection factor is $1$ by construction, because it
emits every product its applicable rules generate; the same computation returns $0.539$ for its
coverage and $0.953$ for its ranking, and those two are measured. The gap between us is therefore
not a worse rule base but the cost of \emph{this} selection stage, which loses more than no
selection stage does at the same budget --- $0.352$ against $0.528$, macro at $k=15$ on the same
$245$ substrates (Appendix~\ref{app:noselect}) --- and which recall charges only to methods that
select.

\looseness=-1
The frame also bounds this architecture: a perfect selector and ranker would stop at \ceiling\ as
our own engine realises it (\S\ref{sec:enginereach}), limited by the corpus rather than by tuning,
$71\%$ of uncovered transformations being types absent from the bank, so generalising the templates
it holds is worth $0.038$. It is carried by the mined majority, $0.785$ on $245$ substrates against
the curated fifth's $0.471$, four published collections adding under two per cent beyond what
mining the training split reaches (Appendices~\ref{app:props},~\ref{app:negative}).

\paragraph{The coverage factor belongs to the system, not the rule base.}
\label{sec:enginereach}
\looseness=-3
Reporting a ceiling presumes it is a property of the bank, and it is not. $152$ of SyGMa's $175$
rules sit verbatim in ours, so those can be held fixed while only the procedure applying them
changes: a depth-1 enumeration that expands the substrate with explicit hydrogens against SyGMa's
own, which does not, same substrates, same matcher, one step each. They reach $0.189$
$[0.151,0.230]$ under the first and $0.381$ $[0.333,0.433]$ under the second,
an engine term of $+0.188$ $[+0.170,+0.205]$ paired --- against an entire bank-to-bank gap of
$0.201$ on those substrates, with the $23$ rules we do not hold worth $+0.143$ $[+0.107,+0.180]$ at
fixed engine and depth no confound. Almost all of what reads as a difference between knowledge
bases is a difference between the programs that query them --- and that difference is one line, the
expansion itself. Our own generator does not expand, which is why \ceiling\ is quoted without it;
measured with it the same bank reaches $0.735$. Toggling that one call, bank and substrates
untouched, carries reach from $0.200$ to $0.390$ --- the whole of the engine term, leaving $+0.002$
$[0.000,+0.005]$ micro between the two programs, though a macro estimator separates them at $+0.006$
$[+0.002,+0.010]$. The loop's other two choices move reach by nothing
(Appendix~\ref{app:enginereach}).
The term is thus not a fact about two particular programs but about one decision neither reports: what a bank covers is uninterpretable without the engine
that realised the figure (Appendix~\ref{app:props}). Nor is the dependence metabolism's: every one of the
$42{,}554$ patterns in the standard retrosynthesis template library constrains atoms by degree and
none names a hydrogen, so the same call costs that library $82\%$ of its applicability --- by the
other of two mechanisms, a match lost before a product is built
(Appendix~\ref{app:retroconvention}).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The criterion the decomposition rests on}
\label{sec:rankflip}

\looseness=-2
Every factor counts matched references, so the criterion is settled first: published methods have
their \emph{frozen} predictions re-scored under all five criteria, so only the match rule changes
across columns, never the chemistry. GRAIL, SyGMa \citep{Ridder_2008} and MetaPredictor
\citep{Zhu_2024} are compared on the whole clean test split, paired on identical substrates
(Table~\ref{tab:matchsens}); BioTransformer \citep{Djoumbou_Feunang_2019} and MetaTrans
\citep{Litsa_2020} on the $\nshared$-substrate shared subset (Appendix~\ref{app:xdomain}).

\looseness=-1
Methods are not equally sensitive to the convention. Over the full split GRAIL gains $0.016$
$[0.010,0.023]$ from canonical to tautomer matching against MetaPredictor's $0.040$
$[0.030,0.050]$, a differential of $+0.024$ $[0.014,0.034]$ surviving Holm--Bonferroni over the
three pairs. Unequal headroom runs the wrong way here: GRAIL enters from the
lower baseline, so has more left to recover, and gains the less (Table~\ref{tab:matchsens}). The same differential against BioTransformer on the 150-substrate subset is $+0.120$,
five times larger; we treat the full-split figure as the estimate, since a subset chosen for data
availability overstated the effect. Output size is a second, orthogonal confound: SyGMa emits $81$
candidates per substrate against GRAIL's $8.4$ (Appendix~\ref{app:budget}).

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Primary endpoint.} The endpoint is the \emph{differential} sensitivity, not any individual
reversal: the protocol $\times$ method interaction, with a paired bootstrap over substrates
($10{,}000$ resamples). Every method gains under a more tolerant criterion, so only the differential
can reorder anything. The design was fixed before the data, the five criteria and the re-scoring of frozen predictions
specified twelve days before the first result; the estimator was not, and no family was
pre-specified either. Multiplicity is corrected \emph{post hoc}, with the family declared
conservatively as the grid the two axes admit rather than the comparisons we report --- method
pairs crossed with criterion steps, twelve externally and twenty internally. Every raw and
Holm-adjusted p-value entering either family is tabulated in Appendix~\ref{app:holm} with its
estimator, a normal approximation to the paired-bootstrap interval; externally all four
interactions called significant survive. On the $\nshared$-substrate subset two pairs separate
along two protocol axes, BioTransformer gaining \interA\ \interAci\ more than GRAIL from
canonical$\rightarrow$tautomer and SyGMa \interB\ \interBci\ more than MetaTrans from
canonical$\rightarrow$InChIKey; the first survives the correction at that family size, the second
misses its threshold and is reported as an uncorrected interval from here on.
The convention is a \textbf{method-dependent confounder}, not a
neutral scoring choice. Two pairs do exchange places between criteria on this subset, though every
pairwise $\Delta$ spans zero at $n=\nshared$ (Appendix~\ref{app:xdomain}): the interaction is
established, the reversals illustrate it.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{External replication.} The results above live on a $\nshared$-substrate subset of our own
split, so we repeated the analysis on the field's de-facto external hold-out, the \nglory\ GLORYx
drugs \citep{de_Bruyn_Kops_2020}, whose own tool is not scored here for want of runnable weights. It is
external for three of the four methods and not for ours, which we checked late: $24$ of the $37$ are
in GRAIL's train or validation split, so its row uses the $13$ unseen (Table~\ref{tab:matchsens}).

\looseness=-1
The result this section rests on involves neither contaminated arm. Under strict InChIKey SyGMa
leads MetaPredictor by $-0.130$ $[-0.196,-0.065]$; one rung later the advantage is gone ($+0.005$
$[-0.071,+0.086]$). The endpoint is not that transition, which contrasts a significant
result with a null one, but the interaction: MetaPredictor gains $+0.135$ $[+0.072,+0.209]$ more
than SyGMa across the stereo step, rejected at either family size (Appendix~\ref{app:holm}). That interval is what is certified: the relaxed gap covers zero
and still admits SyGMa ahead by $0.071$, so a criterion erases a significant advantage here rather
than reversing it.
Unequal headroom runs \emph{with} the effect here rather than against it, and normalised by the
headroom left it does not vanish, at $+0.209$ $[+0.111,+0.322]$ (Appendix~\ref{app:holm}). On the uncontaminated drugs GRAIL's stereo sensitivity survives at $+0.126$
$[+0.028,+0.239]$, as does its interaction with SyGMa, whose stereo step here is zero; the
\emph{external} interaction with BioTransformer does not survive there and is not claimed.
This set's coverage ceiling stays on all $\nglory$ for a measured reason: most of the bank is mined
from training pairs, so applying it is \emph{not} independent of training, yet the ceiling is
$0.719$ on the twenty-four seen drugs against $0.684$ on the thirteen unseen, a difference
whose interval spans zero (Appendix~\ref{app:external}).



\paragraph{The criterion moves human agreement, and that agreement bounds what a recall figure
means.} The
strongest evidence for declaring the criterion involves no model. Twenty-five external drugs are
independently curated by our corpus too, so the two metabolite lists compare as a prediction does to
a reference: the two expert curations of one drug agree at a Jaccard of $0.145$ under strict
\textsc{inchikey} matching and $0.406$ under the tautomer-aware default, on the same drugs
either way. Most of what
looks like disagreement about observed chemistry is disagreement about notation.

\looseness=-1
They also bound what a recall figure means rather than what it can reach: a competent independent
curation records only $0.539$ of the corpus annotation, so past that level recall measures one
source's inclusion choices. That $0.539$ is measured on the $25$ drugs both sources chose to
curate, which carry $5.7$ to $6.5$ references each against $2.2$ across the clean test split, and we cannot sign the
direction of that bias; the caveat travels with the number. Where models sit against it depends on
the budget, and both matched-budget analyses belong here, not only the significant one. MetaPredictor's uncapped $0.562$ is a tie inside noise bought
with $10.6$ metabolites per drug against the curation's $6.0$; capped at each drug's own curated set
size it recovers $0.301$, behind by $0.238$ $[0.123,0.354]$, while under the more conservative
symmetric match, a fixed $k{=}6$ that truncates the curation too, it reaches $0.394$ against
$0.472$, a gap of $+0.078$ $[-0.050,+0.210]$ that does not reach significance
(Appendix~\ref{app:xdomain}).

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Mechanism.} The spread tracks how far a method's outputs diverge
\emph{representationally} from the references, and the intermediate column says which divergence is
responsible: here stereochemistry alone, every separating gain realised by the stereo step. GLORYx
references carry stereocentres that several of these systems cannot emit; internally, where
references are already stereo-stripped, tautomerism fails instead. Which relaxation dominates is therefore a
property of the reference set, and we state that as the effect's condition rather than as a caveat:
it needs references carrying representational variety to diverge from, and a leaderboard packed
tighter than the differential it produces.

\paragraph{It reorders a published leaderboard in another field.} Eleven released retrosynthesis
prediction sets sit on three test sets; within the largest group sharing one, seven systems scored
on their $4{,}999$ common reactions change places at top-1 on predictions that never move. Under
canonical matching the leaders run GraphRetro $0.5377$, LocalRetro $0.5339$, Retroformer $0.5325$,
Graph2SMILES $0.5293$; disregard stereochemistry and they run Graph2SMILES $0.5571$, Retroformer
$0.5541$, GraphRetro $0.5501$, LocalRetro $0.5469$ --- fourth becomes first. Five of twenty-one pairs
exchange, $109$ of $448$ paired interactions surviving Holm. The condition is the packing: these
seven sit a median $0.013$ apart against a differential of $0.015$, while three $0.047$ apart are
moved as much and not reordered. Over $439$ such comparisons in three domains the movement exceeds
the gap once in nine and reverses the pair once in seventeen, so a leaderboard becomes vulnerable
exactly as it matures (Appendix~\ref{app:xdomain}).


\subsection{The aggregate and the budget are one variable, and the decomposition predicts it}
\label{sec:metric}

\looseness=-1
Every interval here is exploratory by the \S\ref{sec:tame} designation and corrected for nothing.
A method emitting eighty candidates to catch a few more references is rewarded by recall and
punished by F1 and Jaccard. On each method's deployed output set over the full split, untruncated
since precision and Jaccard are set quantities, the orderings disagree. Against GRAIL that is
certified in both directions: SyGMa leads on recall by $0.253$ $[0.222,0.283]$ and is $0.088$
$[0.076,0.099]$ below it on F1. Against MetaPredictor only one direction is: SyGMa sits
$0.113$ $[0.107,0.120]$ below it on F1, certified, while its recall lead of $0.023$ has an interval
whose bound rests on zero --- a lead bought by emitting $80.7$ candidates against $10.8$, and one
a single candidate decides, since dropping the substrate itself from every ranked list costs
$0.003$ of it.

\looseness=-1
The budget does the same work, and it is what identifies the variable. Truncating a ranked list
moves the emitted count with the method and its ranking held fixed, so a reordering along the sweep
cannot be a property of any one system. MetaPredictor's count has been moved the other way with the
model fixed too, $10.9$ distinct candidates to $34.3$ at a wider beam (Appendix~\ref{app:seq2seq}). It
reorders without any budget having to be chosen: swept over $k$ the three methods take three
distinct orderings, SyGMa above GRAIL at every budget up to $13$ --- certified from $k=1$ to $k=10$,
by $0.071$ $[0.057,0.086]$ at $k=5$ --- and below only from $14$ on, where the field's $15$ sits. A
fourth method exhausts the field: on $291$ substrates carrying all four, MetaTox at $33.6$
candidates is last at $k\le5$ and first from $k=15$, the budget this field reports at
(Appendix~\ref{app:fourmethod}). A budget binds a method only when it emits more, so at $k=15$ SyGMa is cut on $92.7\%$ of
substrates and GRAIL on none (Appendix~\ref{app:props}).

\looseness=-1
The sweep identifies the variable; a tally of which comparisons cleared significance does not.
Every certified reversal involves the one method that overproduces and none certifies among methods
emitting alike --- but that second half is a failure to detect, those pairs leaving intervals up to
$0.158$ open against a median certified reversal of $0.197$. The matching rule is independent of it:
matched at about eight emitted, GRAIL's sensitivity sits below both others by $-0.020$
$[-0.029,-0.010]$ and $-0.025$ $[-0.035,-0.015]$ paired, so the criterion separates methods at fixed
output size, which output size cannot explain (Appendix~\ref{app:xdomain}).

\looseness=-1
The decomposition says why. SyGMa's selection factor is $1$ (\S\ref{sec:decomp}), which is what
recall rewards and precision punishes, so a pipeline with no selection stage is scored relatively
higher by the first than the second --- though reach remains free and it need not lead on recall.

\looseness=-1
The matching criterion acts on these metrics too, and where it barely touched recall. Over the full
split the recall@15 ordering is stable to within noise, the leading pair exchanging places by at
most $0.0014$ with a differential sensitivity of $-0.001$ $[-0.009,+0.006]$. The F1 gap between
GRAIL and MetaPredictor is another matter: it is certified under four criteria, at $-0.026$ $[-0.038,-0.013]$ under the
tautomer-aware default, and indistinguishable from zero under plain \textsc{inchikey}, at
$+0.003$ $[-0.009,+0.015]$. That is an erased gap rather than a certified reversal, and we claim it
as no more than that: the matching rule and the output size do not act on the same comparisons.
Precision here is a lower bound and threatens this result most, since the method the metric demotes
emits the most candidates; under a uniform-propensity model its ordering is identified and F1's
survives to $c\approx0.055$ (Appendix~\ref{app:props}).





\subsection{Selection as extreme multi-label classification}
\label{sec:xmc}
\label{sec:diagnosis}

\looseness=-1
GRAIL trails both published comparators and leads at no output budget --- SyGMa at $0.572$ on the
$1{,}168$ substrates they share, a paired $-0.242$ $[-0.271,\,-0.212]$. Selection being its dominant
shortfall, what makes selection hard is structural: choosing which of \nbank\ rules apply is
\textbf{extreme multi-label classification} with a near-empty positive set, a median of five per
substrate against far more applicable --- a regime where frequency baselines are hard to beat
\citep{Jain_2016}. They are not beaten here. Through the same enumeration and filter, a learned
selector reaches $0.266$ where rule-firing frequency alone reaches $0.410$, a paired $-0.144$
$[-0.196,\,-0.095]$; the prescribed remedy fails too, inverse-propensity weighting moving it to
$0.249$ (Appendix~\ref{app:props}).

\label{sec:evidence}
The diagnosis rests on nine measurements (Table~\ref{tab:levers}), each designed to move one factor;
six failed and are the more useful half, among them the stratification meant to show a rule bank
transfers better off distribution (Appendix~\ref{app:props}).

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Sample sizes differ, and two comparators cannot be re-run here.} MetaTrans's inference
pipeline no longer reproduces and BioTransformer's configuration is unscripted, so the five-method
table rests on $\nshared$ shared substrates of frozen predictions; the other three are scored on the
full $\ntest$.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Two bounds on the metabolite numbers.} An unannotated but chemically real product scores
as a false positive, so every precision and F1 figure is pessimistic and \S\ref{sec:metric} ranks on
them only under a uniform-propensity model, assumed rather than estimated. And some references are
multi-generation products no single rule application reaches: depth-2 lifts the ceiling by
${\sim}0.012$ at $8.5$ times the cost, so $\ceiling$ bounds this formulation rather than the task.

\looseness=-1
\paragraph{Evaluation hygiene of this paper itself.} Headline numbers come from one evaluation of
the deployed configuration, and selection is on validation but for the two exceptions
\S\ref{sec:tame} names: the filter's encoder variant, chosen on test, and
Appendix~\ref{app:negative}'s exploratory analyses.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\looseness=-2
Four things decide a leaderboard position before any model does: the matching rule, the output size,
the convention an engine applies rules in, and which population a dataset name means. Each is
readable from a released file, none is declared, and one caught this paper.

\subsubsection*{Reproducibility statement}

Every number in this paper traces to a committed artifact and regenerates from what we release, and
the checks that establish it are released as scripts rather than asserted. One of them is meant for
other people's files rather than ours: given a template bank it reports which templates are
convention-dependent and whether the bank is mixed, and given two or more prediction files it
reports whether they are on the same population at all and how far apart their output sizes are.
It needs no reference data and no model, and on the eleven released retrosynthesis files it recovers
the three populations in seconds. What regeneration costs
differs by analysis: every split-level result regenerates from the committed artifacts alone ---
frozen predictions, key tables, per-substrate records --- while the ablations that rank or re-select
candidates require running a trained model and so also need the GRAIL checkpoints. The anonymised
archive holds those, together with every method's frozen predictions, the audits, the harness, the
split construction and the analysis code.

Three things a reader needs in order to reproduce rather than approximate. One result depends on a
third-party file we do not redistribute: the reach of BioTransformer's template set is computed from
the $994$ SMIRKS its own distribution ships in \texttt{metabolicReactions.json}, its
\textsc{envmicro} counterpart and \texttt{standardizationReactions.json}, which a reader reproduces
by pointing the script at an installed copy; its weights and MetaPredictor's are third-party too and
are cited rather than shipped. Results measured on a subsample of a split carry the cap and seed that
drew it, without which the substrate set is not recoverable, since sampling draws without replacement
and a different cap yields a different set of the same size rather than a subset. And
\emph{regenerable} is meant to include the intervals: per-substrate rows are sorted before
resampling, so a repeated run reproduces an artifact byte for byte.

Two standing reconciliations guard the analyses against the code they analyse. Where an analysis path
re-derives a deployed output the two are checked equal before use --- the released per-candidate
score dump reproduces the deployed ranking on all $\ntest$ substrates --- and where a reimplemented
loop stands in for the deployed one, it is gated against a figure the deployed one produced, at the
scale the claim is made on.

Every set this paper treats as external or third-party has been audited for overlap with GRAIL's
training and validation substrates, not only the one where a problem was found. The GLORYx set
overlaps on $24$ of its $37$ drugs, $64.9\%$, and \S\ref{sec:rankflip} reports GRAIL on the $13$
that do not; the $\nshared$-substrate shared subset overlaps on $0$ of $150$. The audit is a
released script rather than a claim, and it is the check whose absence produced the GLORYx error:
it is implied by none of the others reported here and had never been run.
