\section{Introduction}

A drug that reaches the bloodstream does not stay the compound that was administered. Enzymes,
chiefly in the liver, convert it into a series of other compounds, and those compounds carry
their own activity, their own toxicity and their own persistence. A metabolite can be the agent
that makes a drug work, as with the antiviral prodrugs activated by phosphorylation; it can also
be the agent that makes one fail, as with the reactive intermediates behind several
withdrawals. The same question arises outside the clinic: what an environmental chemical becomes
in an organism determines how far it travels and how long it lasts.

Knowing which metabolites will form, as chemical structures rather than as a count or a rate, is
therefore useful early, when a compound exists only on paper and no experiment has been run.
Predicting them computationally has been attempted for three decades, and two families of method
now dominate. Rule-based systems, among them SyGMa \citep{Ridder_2008},
BioTransformer \citep{Djoumbou_Feunang_2019}, GLORY and
GLORYx \citep{de_Bruyn_Kops_2019, de_Bruyn_Kops_2020} and MetaTox \citep{Rudik_2017}, encode
known biotransformations as reaction templates and enumerate the products of applying them to a
query structure. Sequence-to-sequence systems, among them MetaTrans \citep{Litsa_2020} and
MetaPredictor \citep{Zhu_2024}, treat the problem as translation, mapping a text representation
of the substrate to text representations of its metabolites. Recent work extends both lines: a
chemical language model \citep{Larsson_2025}, a mechanistically informed graph
framework \citep{Zhou_2025}, a server predicting pathway, site and product
together \citep{Zhou_2026}, and a preprint predicting all three jointly \citep{Chavan_2026}.

What is missing is not another predictor but a way to tell these apart. The usual measure is
recall: the fraction of the metabolites recorded for a compound that a system returns. That
fraction depends on two decisions made outside the model. The first is what counts as a match,
since two chemical structures can denote the same molecule while differing in how a hydrogen is
placed or how a ring is drawn. The second is how many candidates the system is allowed to return,
since any system recovers more of the answer if permitted to list more guesses. Published
predictors differ on both and report them inconsistently, and the difference is not small: the ones
compared here emit between \numEmittedBiotransformer{} and \numEmittedSygma{} candidates
per substrate by default, so a recall figure quoted for one is not on the scale of a recall
figure quoted for another. Independent evaluations of these tools report the same obstacle
\citep{Boyce_2022, Scholz_2023, Gao_2026}.

That a comparison can turn on an undeclared choice is not new, and two recent statements of it
bound what this paper adds. \citet{Ash_2025} give a protocol for method comparison in small-molecule
drug discovery, arguing that a difference should be shown practically significant rather than
merely significant, and prescribing how a comparison ought to be set up. \citet{Ozcelik_2025} show
what happens when it is not, in generative design: the size of the library and the choice of metric
reorder the models. The gap between the two is where this work sits. A protocol tells an author
what to declare; a demonstration on generative metrics does not transfer to recall against an
incomplete annotation, where the axis that reorders methods is how many candidates each may emit
and no analogue of it exists in distributional metrics. What is offered here is the measurement in
that setting: the axes swept, the whole grid printed, and a bound on what any bank mined from this
corpus could reach.

This work addresses that. We present GRAIL (Graph-scored Rule Application with Inspectable Localisation), a metabolite
structure predictor built from a bank of reaction templates, a learned model that selects which templates to apply, and a learned model
that ranks the resulting candidates. Every prediction it emits carries the template that produced
it and the atoms that template acted on, so a chemist can accept or reject a candidate on
mechanistic grounds rather than on a score. How much mechanism that carries depends on the
template: \numRsizeLethreesharePct{} of the bank, \numRsizeLethree{} of \numRsizeParsed{}
parseable templates, is patterns of three atoms or fewer, for which the named site is a report of
where the pattern matched rather than of a mechanism, and
Section~\ref{sec:offers} measures both the size of that part and how often the named site is the
site that changed. We evaluate it with the two decisions above promoted
to declared axes, meaning that each is stated and varied across its full range instead of being
fixed silently, against \numComparatorsColumnsWord{} published predictors we obtained
and ran on the same substrates, and with the two comparators whose knowledge is built on a source
of this corpus named and their overlap measured. One system is absent and its absence was not
forced: DeepCYP was not run for the cost of submission, its interface taking one substrate at a
time (Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-comparators}). The comparison does not produce a winner:
which system leads depends on how many candidates it is allowed to return, and we report that
dependence rather than a single figure. We further measure how much of the recorded chemistry
lies outside the reach of any single template application, and bound how much of it is chemistry
the training corpus does not contain: between \numBoundShortfallsharemin{} and
\numBoundShortfallsharemax{}, which no template mined from that corpus could supply.

The article is organised as follows. Section~\ref{sec:methods} describes the corpus, the three
stages of the system and the evaluation protocol. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports the
comparison against published predictors, the contribution of each stage, the sensitivity of the
result to the evaluation choices, a worked example, and the coverage bound.
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses where the advantage lies and what the measurements do not
establish, Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} states the conclusions and
Section~\ref{sec:limitations} the limitations. Procedures, ablations and supporting measurements are in the Supporting Information.

\section{Materials and methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Which population carries which number}
\label{sec:populations}

The corpus divides into three substrate-disjoint splits: \numSplitTrainSubstrates{} training
substrates carrying \numSplitTrainPairs{} annotated pairs, \numSplitValSubstrates{} validation
substrates carrying \numSplitValPairs{}, and \numSplitTestSubstrates{} test substrates carrying
\numSplitTestPairs{}. Disjointness by substrate is the discipline a molecular dataset requires if
a held-out figure is to mean anything \citep{Joeres_2025, Kretschmer_2025}, and a read-only audit
verifies zero substrate and zero positive-pair overlap across all three split pairs.

Three populations carry the figures here, and no figure is given without naming one.

The \emph{evaluated test set} is the \numPopulationTestsubs{} test substrates carrying at least
one reference, holding all \numPopulationTestrefs{} references of that split. Both corpus-level
measurements are made on it: the coverage ceiling and the census of the chemistry the remainder
needs. The \emph{validation draw} is a declared sample of \numValdrawCap{} substrates at seed
\numValdrawSeed{} from the validation split, of which \numValdrawPaired{} are held by both
operating modes in a paired comparison. Registered ranking and timing thresholds are checked here,
because a threshold checked on the population whose rule it chose is not a check; three of the ten
reported predictions were in the event adjudicated on the comparison set instead, and
Table~\ref{tab:hyp} marks which. The \emph{comparison set} is the \numPopulationN{} substrates
every method has an entry for, carrying \numPopulationReferences{} references, and every comparison
against a published predictor is measured on it.

That third population has a defect worth stating before any number is read on it. It is not a
random quarter of the test split: it is the submission list sent to the comparator that is a web
service, and how those \numPopulationN{} were drawn is recorded nowhere in this repository, which
is the same defect the corpus assembly carries. So the comparison is read again on the whole
evaluated test set, \numPopdefWholesubstrates{} substrates, against the
\numPopdefWholearmsWord{} comparators that ran over all of it
(Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-population}). The wide-budget leads survive that move and the narrower
lead over SyGMa at a budget of fifteen does not, so that one is restricted to the comparison set
wherever it is stated. The move also costs more than it takes: at a budget of ten the exhaustive
arm trails SyGMa by $\numPopdefExhwholesygmaOneZero$ $[\numPopdefExhwholesygmaOneZeroLo,
\numPopdefExhwholesygmaOneZeroHi]$ and MetaPredictor by
$\numPopdefExhwholemetapredictorOneZero$ $[\numPopdefExhwholemetapredictorOneZeroLo,
\numPopdefExhwholemetapredictorOneZeroHi]$ over the whole set, both intervals excluding zero, where
on the comparison set neither cell separates at all. Neither is therefore among the
\numHolmSeparating{} cells the family-wise correction is applied to, and the
\numHolmLeadsremovedWord{} it removes are leads of this work's.

The annotated substrate--metabolite pairs are assembled from four sources: ChEMBL
\citep{Zdrazil_2024}, DrugBank \citep{Wishart_2018}, MetXBioDB \citep{Djoumbou_Feunang_2019} and
the GLORYx reference set \citep{de_Bruyn_Kops_2020}. The versions used are \CorpusVersions{}. Their
licences do not combine into one redistributable derivative, which the Supporting Information sets
out.

One property of that assembly bears on everything downstream. The selection that drew records from
the four sources is not recoverable: the corpus files predate this repository's first commit, no
script in it reads the four sources, and the accession field is empty in every record. The
apparatus built on top makes every step from the corpus onwards reproducible and cannot make that
step so. What can be checked instead is a rebuild, and
\texttt{scripts/verify\_rebuilt\_corpus.py} reports how a reader's own corpus stands against this
one, substrate by substrate.

The one source held on disk says that selection was not blind to chemistry. Between the pairs of
MetXBioDB that entered the corpus and those that did not, the transformation-type distributions
differ by a total variation distance of \numMetxdropTv{}, with a permutation test over
\numMetxdropPerm{} shuffles putting the chance of a distance that large at
$p=\numMetxdropP$, and \numMetxdropDroppedtypesabsent{} of the dropped pairs carry a type the
kept half never shows. The second source this repository holds points the same way
without settling it: of the GLORYx reference set's \numGloryxdropPairs{} pairs,
\numGloryxdropInside{} are inside the corpus, the distance between the two type distributions is
\numGloryxdropTv{} and \numGloryxdropAbsentsharePct{} of the dropped pairs carry a type the kept
half never shows, but with \numGloryxdropKeptp{} typed pairs on one side and
\numGloryxdropDroppedp{} on the other the same permutation test returns $p=\numGloryxdropP$ and
does not separate. ChEMBL and DrugBank are the other two sources, neither is held here, and
nothing tests them. Every statement below about what the corpus does and does not contain is a
statement about a corpus assembled that way.

The comparison set carries no training contamination: \numPopulationInTrainOrVal{} of its
\numPopulationN{} substrates appear as a substrate of train or validation under a canonicalised
structure key. Molecular overlap of the other kind is expected and is reported in the Supporting
Information: a molecule annotated as a product in one split can be a substrate in another, and
\numLeakSubAsMol{} test substrates are molecules of train.

No external evaluation set is reported, and the reason is the assembly rather than an audit
finding. GLORYx is one of the four sources, so its \numGloryxN{} parent substrates are not
external to this corpus at all, and the \numGloryxOverlap{} of them that fall in train or
validation are there by construction. Removing those leaves thirteen substrates, which is neither
external nor large enough to separate anything. Holding out a source would not fix this either,
because the sources overlap one another.

That has a consequence for the comparison which no split can remove. Rule-based comparators have
no training set to hold out, and their expert rules may encode the same metabolism reported in
these sources; BioTransformer is built on MetXBioDB, and GLORYx was developed against the
reference set of the same name. Their knowledge therefore overlaps the test substrates by construction, in a direction that
favours them. Both are columns of the comparison, both overlaps are measured in
Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-corpus}, and neither is corrected for: a correction would need a model of
how much of a rule set a reference set produced, which nothing here supplies. The comparison
therefore contains two arms whose development sources this corpus draws on, with the advantage
stated and left in place, and a reader who wants the comparison without them can read the other
three columns.
The remaining three arms are not measured against this corpus and are not thereby cleared:
MetaTox and MetaPredictor publish no development set that could be intersected with it. What is
reported is the one overlap that could be computed, not a survey of all four.

\subsection{What this system does not represent}
\label{sec:methods-scope}

Nothing in this pipeline is stereochemistry-aware. The standardiser strips configuration, the
matching criterion is chosen to ignore it, and the annotation carries none, so sulfoxidation,
epoxide hydrolysis and most conjugation are predicted and scored as constitutions. For a predictor
of metabolite structures that is a restriction on what may be asked of it rather than a
technicality: sulfoxide and epoxide configuration carry the pharmacology of several drug classes,
and a system that cannot represent configuration cannot be asked which enantiomer an enzyme makes.
Every recall figure in this paper is therefore recall over constitutions, and the size of what that
sets aside is bounded in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Overview}

Each design choice below is stated with the measurement that decided it and the prediction it was
registered as, rather than with a pointer forward to Results: a configuration and the evidence for
it are one object, and separating them is what lets a configuration be reported without its
evidence.

GRAIL predicts in three stages, each separately inspectable. A generator scores every rule in a
bank of \numBankRules{} reaction templates, written as SMIRKS transformation patterns, against
the substrate. The selected rules are then
applied with RDKit 2022.09.5, and their products are
standardised \citep{Hahnke_2018, Mansouri_2024} and deduplicated. A template that breaks a bond returns more
than one species, and every product and every disconnected component of it enters the pool
separately provided it has at least \numFragmentFloorWord{} heavy atoms, which drops a lone leaving
group and keeps a small metabolite such as formate; no annotated metabolite in the evaluated test
set is multi-component, so that convention decides what is offered and not whether a reference is
reached. A scorer scores each
(substrate, candidate) pair, encoding the two molecules as separate graphs rather than as one
merged graph, and the candidates are ordered by a rank fusion of that scorer's and the generator's
orderings. The merged variant, which joins the two graphs along their maximum common substructure,
was built and measured and is not what ships (Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-arch}). Every emitted candidate carries the identity of the rule that produced
it and the site at which it fired.

A candidate reachable by several rules, or by one rule firing at several sites, receives a single
score. Writing $R(c)$ for the rules that produce $c$ and $p_r$ for the generator's probability
that rule $r$ applies, the aggregation is noisy-or,
\begin{equation}
p(c) = 1 - \prod_{r \in R(c)} \left( 1 - p_r \right),
\label{eq:noisyor}
\end{equation}
so evidence from independent rules accumulates rather than competing. The independence is an
assumption this bank does not satisfy, since it holds published rule sets verbatim and the mined
half is full of near-duplicates. What that costs is swept over the four rules the
implementation offers (Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-knobs}). The blend of Equation~\ref{SI-eq:blend} is
better at the head of the list, by at most $+\numAggCmpHybridMax$ $[+\numAggCmpHybridMaxLo,
+\numAggCmpHybridMaxHi]$ at a budget of \numAggCmpHybridMaxkWord{}. On this population it is
indistinguishable from the deployed rule at every budget a lead is claimed at; on the validation
draw it separates as far as \numAggValHybridSepto{}, which includes two of them, and that reading
is in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}. Equation~\ref{eq:noisyor}
is why deduplication is not a cosmetic step: the key under which two products are judged the same
decides which scores are combined, and changing that key moves the ranking. Tautomers are the
hard case, and their treatment is not settled by the standard identifiers \citep{Dhaked_2020}.

Both learned stages were trained on a subsample: \numTrainSubstrates{} of the split's
\numSplitTrainSubstrates{} training substrates, drawn at a recorded seed, for
\numTrainEpochsWord{} epochs, with early stopping that never engaged. The subsample was fixed when
the run was configured and is not a consequence of anything measured here, so what the ablations
below compare is the value of each stage at that training budget rather than at the split's full
size. Every setting is in the Supporting Information.

\subsection{Rule bank}

The bank holds \numBankRules{} templates spanning \numBankTypes{} distinct reaction types, where a
type is the multiset of bonds that change between mapped atoms. It is the deduplicated union of \numAttribNamed{} templates
from three curated collections and \numMinedRules{} mined from the training split alone,
together with \numAttribUnattributed{} that were carried as a fourth curated collection and are
not one: they are an earlier machine extraction from the same annotated pairs, identified in the
Supporting Information by three marks that agree. The bank therefore holds two extractions and
one curated body, and the curated body is \numAttribNamed{} templates. Most of that body is not this
work's chemistry: \numThirdpartyBorrowed{} of the \numAttribNamed{},
\numThirdpartyBorrowedsharePct{}, appear verbatim in rule sets published by others,
\numThirdpartyBiotransformerShipped{} of BioTransformer's, \numThirdpartySygmanamed{} attributed
to SyGMa and \numThirdpartyGloryxnamed{} to GLORYx. That is a lower bound, because string equality
cannot trace a template somebody rewrote. What is this work's own is the mining, the ranking and
the measurement, not the writing of the templates, and Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-bank} gives the
counts per rightsholder with the terms each requires. The split is disjoint by substrate rather
than by molecule, so a molecule that is a substrate in training may appear as a product in test;
the split counts are in Section~\ref{sec:populations} and the molecular overlap between splits is
in the Supporting Information. For each annotated substrate--product pair a ring-aware maximum common substructure is
located, the atoms and bonds that changed are flagged as the reaction centre, the centre is
expanded by one bond and emitted as a mapped SMIRKS. A template survives only if applying it to
its own source substrate regenerates the annotated product after canonicalisation.

Support is heavily skewed: \numRareSingletonsharePct{} of the mined templates rest on a single
training pair. Withholding pairs and counting the templates that survive gives a rarefaction curve
still climbing at \numRareSlope{} templates per thousand further pairs
(Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-rarefaction}), so template discovery from this corpus is not finished.

\subsection{Rank fusion}

Both learned stages produce an opinion about each candidate, and their product is scale-sensitive:
if one scorer occupies a narrow range and the other a wide one, the wide one decides the order. The
deployed combination is reciprocal rank fusion \citep{cormack2009},
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RRF}(c) = \sum_{i} \frac{1}{K + r_i(c)}, \qquad K = 60,
\end{equation}
where $r_i(c)$ is the rank of $c$ under scorer $i$. Only positions enter, so the two opinions
contribute equally whatever their scales. $K = \numFusionkDeployed$ is the value the method was
published with, and it is swept rather than merely disclosed. Every constant of
\numFusionkFlatfrom{} or more is indistinguishable from it at budgets of 5, 15 and 50, and the null
is stated as the bound it is: no such constant differs from the deployed value by more than
$\numFusionkBandOneFive$ at fifteen. At a budget of 30 it is not a null, where
$K=\numFusionkBreakconstant$ falls $\numFusionkBreakgap$
$[\numFusionkBreaklo, \numFusionkBreakhi]$ short, so flatness holds at three of the four budgets
and not at all of them (Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-knobs}).

The change was registered as prediction P1, threshold $+\numHSevenThreshold$ fixed before it was
checked, and evaluated on a validation draw where the fusion had not been selected:
$+\numHSevenDiff$ $[+\numHSevenLo, +\numHSevenHi]$. On the capped pool the deployed system ranks
the same contrast is $+\numHSevenDeployed$, because the cap and the fusion recover overlapping
ground (Table~\ref{SI-tab:si-ranking}).

\subsection{Pool cap}

Before fusion the pool is truncated by generator score, a quantity available before any pair graph
is built:
\begin{equation}
\hat{C}(s) = \operatorname*{arg\,top}_{c \in C(s)}{}^{\,\numHNineCap} \; p(c),
\end{equation}
where $C(s)$ is everything the applied rules enumerate. A cap before a fusion is not a filter: the
fusion ranks over whatever survives, so removing a candidate changes the rank every other receives,
which is why truncation can raise recall rather than only bound it. Registered as prediction P2 with
a threshold of $+\numHNineThreshold$ and measured $+\numHNineDiff$
$[+\numHNineLo, +\numHNineHi]$, mean pool \numHNinePoolBefore{} to \numHNinePoolAfter{}. The
value was fixed by a rule rather than by a search: the cap must be at least twice the widest budget
reported, so the widest column is never just the whole pool, and 100 is the smallest round number
that satisfies it.

\subsection{Rule budget and two operating modes}

How many templates the generator selects before any is applied is the \emph{rule budget}. It is
one continuous setting, and the two modes reported throughout are two points on it rather than two
designs. The \emph{interactive} mode applies the \numHOneZeroTopk{} templates the trained
checkpoint records and returns in about a second, which is what a user waiting at a web form
requires; the \emph{exhaustive} mode applies the whole bank, the \numBankParses{} of
\numBankRules{} templates that parse, and takes tens of seconds. The interactive value is where
the released checkpoint was trained, which is a fact about how the run was configured and not an
argument for the number, so the curve either side of it is measured on validation
(Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-budget}). What the difference buys was registered as prediction P3 with a
ceiling of $+\numHOneZeroThreshold$: on validation $+\numHOneZeroBought$, 95\% CI
$[\numHOneZeroLo, +\numHOneZeroHi]$, mean pool on that draw \numHOneZeroPoolBank{} to
\numHOneZeroPoolTrained{} candidates before deduplication.

\subsection{Emission}

Under the product combination the system emitted candidates scoring within $\alpha =
\numEmitAlpha$ of the leader. That rule does not transfer to a rank fusion, and the reason is
structural: the worst candidate scores $\numEmitFusionmedian$ of the best under fusion against
$\numEmitProductmedian$ under the product, because a reciprocal-rank sum is a rank statistic
without scale. At that $\alpha$ the rule admits $\numEmitByfusion$ candidates from a pool of
$\numEmitPool$ on the comparison set, truncating nothing. The deployed emission is therefore $\hat{C}(s)$ itself, ordered by fusion and truncated by no
threshold, so $|\hat{Y}_s| = |\hat{C}(s)|$. For the interactive mode that length is set by the
rule budget and the substrate's chemistry, and its mean of \numEmittedTrained{} on the comparison
set sits well below any cap. That figure is not the one Table~\ref{tab:modes} prints, which is
measured on the validation draw; every candidate count in this paper is listed with the stage and
the population it belongs to in Table~\ref{SI-tab:si-counts}, because the same words name
different quantities at different points of the pipeline. The exhaustive mode differs: its pool is capped at 100 by the cap prediction P2 registers, and
its mean output of \numEmittedBank{} is close enough to that cap that the cap sets its length on
most substrates. That cap is a parameter of this system, it is declared, and it applies to that
arm and not to the comparators. Registered as prediction P4 with a criterion of zero lost cells
over a grid of five matching criteria by ten budgets and the comparator's own uncapped output:
\numHOneOneLost{} of \numHOneOneCells{}. That ladder was fixed with the prediction and is not the
nine budgets read everywhere else here; it is released with the register and with the grid it was
adjudicated on.

\subsection{Evaluation}

The rule deciding when a predicted structure counts as one of the references is the
\emph{matching criterion}. It is treated here as a declared axis with five settings: canonical
SMILES equality, the full InChIKey, its stereochemistry-blind first block, Tanimoto similarity of
one over Morgan fingerprints, and a tautomer-aware key that canonicalises tautomers on both
sides. The last is the
default, because the references neither carry stereochemistry nor distinguish tautomers, and
because standard identifiers normalise only a subset of tautomers \citep{Dhaked_2020}. That the
annotation carries no stereochemistry is also a limit on what any of these systems can be asked
for: nothing in this pipeline is stereochemistry-aware, so sulfoxidation, epoxide hydrolysis and
most conjugation are predicted as constitutions and not as configurations. All five are reported for every
comparison, with the levels behind every verdict in Table~\ref{SI-tab:si-criterion-levels}.

A third choice sits behind both: how the substrate itself is drawn. Templates are matched
against the structure the corpus stores, and that corpus draws amides as imidic acids, so the
choice is not neutral. It is a deployment question and not only an evaluation one, because a user submits the drawing a
chemist would make: given gemcitabine as a chemist draws it, the interactive mode returns
\numCaseInterdrawnCandidates{} candidates and none of the four annotated metabolites, against one
of four on the stored drawing. Pooled over the comparison set the effect is small because the
standardiser changes only \numDialcondChanged{} of \numDialcondSubstrates{} substrates; on those it
changes it is $\numDialcondConditional$ at a budget of 15 against $\numDialcondPooled$ pooled. Rebuilding both GRAIL arms with every substrate redrawn by the
standardiser moves \numDsweepMoved{} of \numDsweepCells{} arm-against-comparator verdicts, none
of them a lead claimed here, and the coverage ceiling on that population by
$\numDsweepCeilingdiff$ with the interval covering zero.

The arms did not all meet that choice the same way, and the asymmetry belongs here rather than in
a sweep. Five of the six were handed the substrate exactly as the corpus stores it; MetaTox was
not, because the file submitted to the service re-tautomerises the substrate to the natural form
for \numDialectMetatoxretaut{} of the \numDialectMetatoxn{}. The direction is not neutral, since
this bank's mined half requires the imidic reactant more often than the amide, and the size of it
is measured for every arm that can be re-run: it raises SyGMa's recall by
$+\numSygdialectThirty$ at $k=30$ and MetaPredictor's by $+\numMpdrawAllDiffThreeZero$, and
changes BioTransformer's by $\numBtarmDrawingdelta$. Only MetaTox cannot be re-run. The whole
comparison with the other five on the drawing a user would submit is
Table~\ref{SI-tab:si-equalised}, and it moves \numEqualisedMovedWord{} of the nine verdicts:
every arm's level falls or rises with the drawing and the division between them does not.
Table~\ref{tab:sweep} is read in the corpus dialect, in which every reference is also stored.

The arms are also not held at one enzymatic depth, and no reading here equalises it. SyGMa's
deployed scenario composes a phase-one and a phase-two ruleset, BioTransformer is run for one
step, MetaTox's depth is not a setting we chose or can read, and a mined template may perform
more than one enzymatic edit in a single application:
\numCompositeUnionsharePct{} of this bank's mined templates do
(Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-singlestep}). The depth is swept for SyGMa, whose scenario is a documented
setting, and the lead survives it. BioTransformer's step count is also ours to turn and was not
swept, a decision taken after the matched-length control had been read and stated as such;
MetaTox's depth is neither ours to turn nor readable (Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-comparators}). For
those two the difference between the arms is stated rather than removed, and the wide-budget
cells should be read with it in view.

The number of candidates a system is permitted to return is the \emph{output budget}, written
$k$, and it is the second declared axis. Recall at budget $k$ for substrate $s$ with references
$Y_s$ and the first $k$ predictions $\hat{Y}^{(k)}_s$ is $|Y_s \cap \hat{Y}^{(k)}_s| / |Y_s|$. Two aggregations are distinguished
throughout and never mixed within a comparison,
\begin{align}
\mathrm{macro} &= \frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{s \in S}
                   \frac{|Y_s \cap \hat{Y}^{(k)}_s|}{|Y_s|}, \\
\mathrm{micro} &= \frac{\sum_{s} |Y_s \cap \hat{Y}^{(k)}_s|}{\sum_{s} |Y_s|},
\end{align}
and every figure states which it is. Intervals are a paired bootstrap over substrates: for each of
$B = 10\,000$ resamples $S^{(b)}$ drawn with replacement,
\begin{equation}
\delta^{(b)} = \frac{\sum_{s \in S^{(b)}} \left( h^A_s - h^B_s \right)}
                     {\sum_{s \in S^{(b)}} |Y_s|},
\label{eq:bootstrap}
\end{equation}
with $h^A_s$ and $h^B_s$ the hits of the two arms, and the reported interval is the empirical
$2.5$ and $97.5$ percentiles of $\{\delta^{(b)}\}$. Resampling substrates rather than
predictions is what makes the interval one on the \emph{difference} between two arms read on the
same items. Precision is reported in Table~\ref{SI-tab:si-precision} but is not used to order systems: under incomplete
annotation an unannotated but real metabolite counts as a false positive, so a system emitting
fewer candidates is rewarded for reasons unrelated to its chemistry. It does order them
differently, which is the point of printing it.

Every method is prevented from returning the substrate itself as one of its own metabolites:
a prediction equal to the substrate is discarded before the budget is applied, for all arms
alike. That convention is swept like the other declared choices.
The arms differ sharply in how often they return the substrate, from none of SyGMa's lists to
\numPdropReturnsBank{} of the exhaustive mode's \numPopulationN{}, so the convention had room to
favour one over another. It does not: the largest effect on recall anywhere in the grid is
\numPdropMaxEffect{} and no cell separates from zero (Table~\ref{SI-tab:si-parentdrop}).

Every interval in this paper is a per-comparison 95\% interval with no family-wise adjustment.
Both readings are reported. Treating the sweep as one family of \numHolmTests{} tests, two
arms against the three comparators the family was declared over at every budget, and applying
Holm's correction to bootstrap $p$-values from the same resamples, \numHolmSurviving{} of the
\numHolmSeparating{} cells that separate per comparison still separate; the \numHolmChangedWord{} that do not are marked in
Table~\ref{SI-tab:si-intervals}. Of those, \numHolmLeadsremovedWord{} are leads, and every one of the
\numHolmLeadsremovedWord{} is a lead this paper claims somewhere: the exhaustive arm over
MetaTox at $k=30$, over SyGMa and over MetaPredictor at $k=15$, and the interactive arm over
MetaTox at $k=10$. Each is marked where it is claimed. Two
comparators were obtained after that family was fixed, so their contrasts are reported with their
own intervals and are not folded into a correction declared without them
(Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-comparators}).

That leaves the obvious objection, which is that this paper prints more contrasts than the family
covers, and it is answered by measurement rather than by the declaration. Applying the same
correction to every contrast printed anywhere here, \numHolmWidetests{} of them,
\numHolmWidesurviving{} still separate, and it removes \numHolmWideremovesdeclaredWord{} cells the
declared family keeps. Of those \numHolmWideremovesdeclaredWord{},
\numHolmWideleadsremovedWord{} is a lead of this work's, over MetaTox at $k=10$, and the other two
run against it. So the wider
reading costs this paper one cell and gains it two, and neither reading changes the wide-budget
result.

No single budget is privileged. A claim that the system leads is made for a cell only where that
cell's interval excludes zero, and where an ordering is not established the table says so.
Reporting the whole space of defensible analysis choices rather than one chosen
cell is the multiverse and specification-curve discipline \citep{Steegen_2016, Simonsohn_2020},
applied here to the two choices this literature leaves undeclared.

\subsection{What the ranking is worth, and what did not work}

Re-ranking one pool several ways, with the pool, the matching rule and the budget fixed, separates
what each learned stage contributes from what the form of their combination contributes; no model
runs, because the pools carry both component scores. At $k=15$ the deployed fusion separates from
the generator's ordering alone, from the filter's alone and from their product, by
$+\numRankCmpVsgenerator$, $+\numRankCmpVsfilter$ and $+\numRankCmpVsproduct$. It also separates
from a baseline this literature does not report and every learned ranker over (substrate, candidate)
pairs competes with whether or not it is reported: similarity to the substrate, which needs no
training and no labels, and which the fusion beats by $+\numSimCmpGapOneFive$
$[+\numSimCmpLoOneFive, +\numSimCmpHiOneFive]$ at every budget on both populations. The
substrate-specific part of the rule choice is worth less than either: against the same number of
applicable rules picked by training frequency it gains $+\numSelVspriorApplicable$
$[+\numSelVspriorApplicablelo, +\numSelVspriorApplicablehi]$, small at every budget and separating
at all of them, and it is reported as that rather than as the stage's justification
(Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-ablations}).

Two registered mechanisms failed and are reported as failures. Emitting whole groups of candidates
as blocks cost $\numHEightDiff$ against a registered gain of $+\numHEightThreshold$ (P6), and using
the same group signal as a gate before the fusion cost $\numHOneFourDiff$ against
$+\numHOneFourThreshold$ (P7). The second is closed by an oracle rather than by model quality: even
a perfect group ordering through that gate is worse than the generator cap it replaces. The signal
helps only where it adjusts an order that already exists, which is what P5 does and why P5 is a
measurement rather than a confirmation (Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-oracle}).

Every figure here comes from one trained checkpoint, so the intervals quantify the population and
not the training. Retraining both stages under \numSpreadSeedsWord{} seeds at the configuration
these tables report gives the training noise, \numSpreadInteractiveSd{} for the interactive arm at
a budget of fifteen and \numSpreadExhaustiveSd{} for the exhaustive one. Read against the arm whose
knob each is, the rank fusion of P1 is \numSpreadPOneInsd{} times that spread, the pool cap of P2
\numSpreadPTwoInsd{} times it and the rule budget of P3 \numSpreadPThreeInsd{} times it. The last
runs this work's way and is bounded where it is given: the effect is measured on the validation draw
and the spread on the comparison set, so the ratio is indicative and not a test.

\subsection{Whether the named site is the site that changed}
\label{sec:site}

Every prediction names the transformation rule that produced it and the site at which it fired,
which lets a candidate be rejected on mechanistic grounds instead of on a score. Predicting the
site alone is a task with its own literature and its own tools, reactivity-based
\citep{Rydberg_2010} and learned \citep{Sicho_2019, Jacob_2025_uncertainty}, resting on
annotation curated for the purpose \citep{Mazzolari_2019}, and that literature has recently had to
confront how its own labels are derived \citep{Jacob_2025_autosom}; the claim here is narrower and is about consistency rather than
about site prediction, namely that the site this system names is the site that changed. It is
measured. On the interactive arm, whose rule
budget of \numSiteRulebudget{} the audit records, of \numSiteScored{} correctly predicted
metabolites the reported site touches the atoms that actually differ from the substrate in
\numSiteTouchingPct{}, against \numSiteConnectednullPct{} for a connected fragment of the same
size grown at random from the same molecule, and lies wholly inside the change in
\numSiteInsidePct{} (Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-site}). An agreement rate near nine in ten is not the
size of the effect; the margin over that null is, and it is \numSiteConnectedmargin{}.

Two things bound that reading. The reference centre is derived by the routine that located the
reaction centre when a template was mined, so for a mined template the two are not independent and
the agreement is partly a self-consistency check; split by provenance, the
\numSiteCuratedscored{} matches carried by a hand-written template, for which they are
independent, agree at \numSiteCuratedPct{} against \numSiteMinedPct{} for the mined ones, so the
claim does not rest on the templates whose provenance would guarantee it. And the exhaustive arm is
not audited this way, because it applies the whole bank rather than a budget of
\numSiteRulebudget{} rules and the population of scored matches would be a different one. How much
a chemist gets from an attribution also depends on the template: \numRsizeLethreesharePct{} of the
parseable templates have three reactant atoms or fewer, and \numRsizeCasetop{} of the twenty
highest-ranked candidates in the worked example come from templates that small, where the site
discrimination comes from the learned stages rather than from the rule.

\numHOneThreeEnumeration{} times the remaining work, run once per product of every template; a
cache had concealed it, a repeated substrate running many times faster.

Two registered predictions followed. Moving standardisation to surviving candidates (P8) freed
recall but failed on time, \numHOneThreeFactor$\times$ against a registered $10\times$: removing
most of the work does not remove most of the time once the remainder is the new majority. Adding a
tautomer enumeration budget of \numHOneFiveBudget{} (P9) passes both halves, taking the median from
\numHOneThreeTimeBefore~s to \numHOneFiveTime~s against a registered target below ten, at a recall
loss of $\numHOneFiveRecallDiff$ against a cap of $0.01$. The two arms ran under different machine
loads; correcting for that puts the median at \numHOneFiveCorrectedmedian~s, still below the target.
The correction, the safeguard that kept the budget from moving what counts as a hit, and the
per-stage decomposition are in the Supporting Information.

Cost is reported at the tail as well as at the median, because a service is used at both. The
interactive mode's mean is six times its median, and its slowest substrate is nearly six hundred
times it.

\subsection{The ceiling, and how much of it is the corpus}
\label{sec:ceiling}

Applying the whole bank to every test substrate recovers \numCeilingCoverage{} of references in
one step, under the same tautomer-aware matching criterion the comparison defaults to and on the
same evaluated test set, leaving \numCeilingUncovered{} uncovered. A second loop measuring the
same hydrogen convention counts \numCeilagreeAuditimplicitUncovered{}, and four counts of this
bound exist in all; they are set out below rather than left for a reader to discover, because the
number the rest of this section is about is the one that moves with the instrument. It carries no
resampling interval: it is a census of this population under one instrument, and the census keeps
its counters per shard rather than per substrate, so what is reported beside it is the spread
across instruments and not across resamples. The remainder divides by the reaction-centre change it requires: \numCeilingNovel{} need
a transformation type the bank does not hold, \numCeilingKnown{} need a type it holds whose rule
did not fire, and \numCeilingUntypeable{} cannot be typed.

That number is what orients the two directions this work could go next, so it is worth putting
beside the recall. On the comparison set the ceiling is \numHeadroomCeiling{} and the exhaustive
arm at a budget of fifty reaches \numHeadroomReached{}, which is \numHeadroomSharePct{} of what
the bank can reach. The headroom a better ranking could take is \numHeadroomRanking{}; the
headroom no ranking can take, because the bank does not produce those structures at all, is
\numHeadroomCoverage{}. The census below is about the second and the larger.

It is a one-template ceiling and not a one-enzyme one, which matters for how the number reads. The
annotation itself is not all single steps: applying the composite instruments to the references
rather than to the templates, \numRefstepEither{} of the \numRefstepTyped{} pairs those
instruments can type, \numRefstepEithersharePct{}, are flagged as reached in more than one
turnover and recorded by the corpus as one pair. That is what the instruments flag rather than a
count of such transformations: the union includes the locus instrument, whose error is unmeasured
in both directions (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}), and the registered instrument alone flags
\numRefstepEdits{}. A hit on one of those is a single template standing in for
several turnovers, so \numCeilingCoverage{} is not the share of annotated metabolism one enzymatic
step reaches.

How hydrogen is presented to a template changes what fires, so the convention is named here
rather than left to the code. The bound stated above, \numCeilingUncovered{} uncovered, is
measured with hydrogens implicit through the loop a caller runs, which is the convention every
deployed firing path uses. The three other counts are these. A bound that moves with the
instrument has to be read with that movement beside it.
Measured instead through the loop that audits both conventions from one code path, implicit
hydrogens leave \numCeilagreeAuditimplicitUncovered{} uncovered and hydrogens made explicit with
the templates completed to match leave \numCeilagreeAuditcompletedUncovered. Choosing the
convention per template, which no caller can request, leaves \numCeilagreeDispatchUncovered. A
fourth arm, hydrogens made explicit without completing the templates, is an incomplete loop rather
than a convention and reaches only \numHydUncoveredexpanded{}; the instrument's own record
excludes it.

The first two are the same convention measured through two different loops, and they disagree by
\numCeilagreeDisagreementWord{} references because the two loops clean products differently. All four span \numCeilagreeSpread{} references,
\numCeilagreeSpreadofmarginPct{} of \numCeilagreeNarrowestmargin{}, which is the narrowest
margin that separates from zero anywhere in the comparison table, so the choice of instrument
cannot decide a claim here. The residual dependence of the bound on the convention, measured per template, is
$\numHydResidual{}$, 95\% CI $[\numHydResiduallo{}, \numHydResidualhi{}]$, an interval excluding
zero: the bound is convention-dependent, by \numHydResidualrefs{} references in
\numHydReferences{}.

Relaxing the convention-dependent primitives in the templates recovers three of the
\numCeilingKnown{}, moving the ceiling by $+0.0012$. An informal estimate made before the run put
it at about eight; that estimate is not one of the registered predictions and the register says so,
so the gap between eight and three is reported and carries no verdict.

The \numCeilingNovel{} misses of absent type collapse into \numCensusTypes{} distinct types, of
which \numCensusOnce{} occur once and carry \numCensusOnceSharePct{} of the mass. The objection that
this is a consequence of how finely a type is defined is answered by varying the definition. What
that answer covers should be said exactly: it covers the \numCeilingNovel{} misses of absent
type and not the other \numCeilingKnown{} plus \numCeilingUntypeable{} of the
\numCeilingUncovered{}. A miss whose type the bank holds and whose rule did not fire is an
implementation failure by construction, and three of the \numCeilingKnown{} were recovered by
relaxing convention-dependent primitives, so that arm is not invariant to anything.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Where the advantage is, and where it is not}

The comparison does not produce a winner. SyGMa recovers more at the head of the list, and at
$k \le 5$ the interval separates against GRAIL. GRAIL continues to improve at budgets where the
other systems stop. At a budget of three candidates SyGMa is the better choice; at fifty this
work's exhaustive arm recovers more, and against SyGMa it does so for a reason that survives
matching the two on list length, while against MetaTox and MetaPredictor it does not.

The first half of that is a result about the field and not about this system. SyGMa is the
oldest and by far the smallest method in the comparison, \numSygmaRules{} published rules against
this bank's \numBankRules{}, and at every budget of five or fewer it recovers more of the
annotated metabolites than any other method here, including two published since. A chemist
reading a short list is working at exactly those budgets. That a rule set of this size is not
beaten at the head of the list by any of the systems here, two of them released since, is worth
stating plainly, and it is
visible only because the budget is swept: at a budget of fifty the same table has this work's
exhaustive arm ahead of SyGMa by $+\numGapBanksygmaFiveZero$, and ahead of every comparator by at
least $+\numGapBankmetatoxFiveZero$, so the ordering at the head of the list reverses.

The division has a consequence for how such tools are compared. A single recall figure at a
single budget would have named a different winner depending on which budget its author chose,
and each of those choices would have been defensible. That is the case for reporting the sweep
rather than a single cell, and it is made here on this system's own numbers.

\subsection{Choosing an operating point}
\label{sec:offers}

The two modes are two settings of one rule budget, and which to run is decided by the budget the
service answers at rather than by a default. Below about ten candidates the interactive mode is the
right setting and the faster of the two by the margin Figure~\ref{fig:cost} reports; against
MetaTox it even leads at $k=10$, $+\numGapTrainedmetatoxOneZero$
$[\numGapTrainedmetatoxOneZeroLo, \numGapTrainedmetatoxOneZeroHi]$, one of the four leads the
family-wise correction removes.

Beyond a short list that setting is the weaker one, and the cause is the pool rather than the
ranking. It returns \numEmittedTrained{} candidates on average against MetaTox's
\numEmittedMetatox{}, and at $k=20$ already \numShortTrainedTwoZero{} of its \numPopulationN{}
lists are shorter than the budget; a list that has ended cannot gain a hit from a wider window, so
the deficit grows with $k$ whatever the order within it. It trails MetaTox at every budget above
fifteen, $\numGapTrainedmetatoxThreeZero$ $[\numGapTrainedmetatoxThreeZeroLo,
\numGapTrainedmetatoxThreeZeroHi]$ at $30$ and $\numGapTrainedmetatoxFiveZero$
$[\numGapTrainedmetatoxFiveZeroLo, \numGapTrainedmetatoxFiveZeroHi]$ at $50$, both intervals
excluding zero and both surviving the correction. A service deployed in that mode would recover
less than the system it replaces wherever it answers with thirty candidates or more.

So a service answering with thirty or fifty must either run the exhaustive mode, which leads MetaTox
at both, or raise the rule budget, which is the same knob: on validation, raising it to the whole
bank is worth $+\numBudgetKSevenFiveEightOneGapthirty$
$[+\numBudgetKSevenFiveEightOneLothirty, +\numBudgetKSevenFiveEightOneHithirty]$ at a budget of
thirty and nothing that separates at fifteen (Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-budget}). Either way it pays
in runtime and in what the reader is handed: at fifty the exhaustive arm's precision is
\numPrecBankFiveZero{}, about thirty wrong structures for each right one. Precision under an
incomplete annotation is a lower bound and is not used here to order systems, but a list of that
length is what the wide-budget figures describe, and it is why the next property matters more than
the recall figure does.

\subsection{What is and is not distinctive}

What distinguishes this system is not that it reports a site but that the site is audited.
BioTransformer's output names the reaction and the enzyme behind each structure it returns, and
MetaTox's method reports a biotransformation type and a site of metabolism \citep{Rudik_2017}, so
reporting one is the norm rather than a contribution. Checking it against the atoms that actually
changed, against a null of the same size, is not done anywhere in this literature that we can find,
and Section~\ref{sec:site} is that check. Ordering is not a
differentiator either: \numArmreturnOrderingWord{} of the \numComparatorsNWord{} comparators order
their whole output, and this system is one more that does. What is left, and it is the property this
paper rests on, is that every evaluation choice is declared with every cell of the resulting grid
reported.

\subsection{What the numbers do not support}

GRAIL does not lead at the tight budgets. At $k=15$ neither arm separates from the strongest
comparator, and at $k=20$ the interactive arm separates against it. The established lead is the exhaustive arm's, at $k=30$ and $50$. The system is not compared against the 2025--26 wave, because that wave cannot be run as
released: of \numComparatorsUnavailableWord{} such systems none ships a runnable metabolite
predictor \citep{Larsson_2025, Zhou_2025, Zhou_2026, Chavan_2026}, one of them releasing weights
whose reaction module was withdrawn, and the Supporting Information says what each is missing. No ranking in this literature can presently be verified independently, which
is the concrete case for leaderboards releasing per-item predictions rather than aggregate scores
\citep{Rodriguez_2021, Mishra_2021}. Precision figures are bounded by annotation completeness. And the advantage at
wide budgets is partly a statement about output length on all sides rather than about ranking.

The argument made here about other people's figures was turned on this work's own release, and it
found settings that decide a number and were not declared. The rule that combines the
templates reaching one candidate is one, and a selection on validation prefers a different one.
The fusion constant is a second, left at the value the method was published with. A rule gate the
released checkpoint carries is a third, and no figure in this paper was measured under it. A set
of candidate pools scored by a checkpoint this repository no longer ships is a fourth, and the
drawing of the substrate and the pool cap are two more of the same kind, both now declared and
one of them swept. Each is declared and measured here rather than described, and a reader may take that either way: as the
discipline doing its work, or as a measure of how easily an undeclared setting survives even in a
paper whose subject is undeclared settings. The second reading is why the first is worth
reporting.

\subsection{The ceiling bounds this formulation, not the task}

The shortfall is a property of the corpus the templates are mined from, and this is measured
directly rather than argued from the bank's coverage. Of the \numTypeoverlapBanklacks{} test
references whose transformation type the bank does not hold, \numTypeoverlapCorpuslackstoo{},
\numTypeoverlapCorpussharePct{}, have a type the training annotation does not hold either; the
remaining \numTypeoverlapMinerslost{} are chemistry the corpus contained and the miner did not
carry across. Two counts appear in that sentence and they measure different things.
\numTypeoverlapBanklacks{} counts every test reference whose type the bank does not hold, whether
or not the bank reaches it. \numCeilingNovel{}, above, counts only those it also fails to reach.
The gap between them is \numTypeoverlapReached{} references: chemistry of a type the bank does not
hold that the bank nonetheless reaches. That is not an error in either count, because a template can
reach a product while being typed differently from the pair that product came from.
Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-typeoverlap} cross-tabulates a different pair again, whether a type is in the
bank against whether it is in the training annotation, and its four cells carry no reach dimension
at all. So the containment of \numTypeoverlapCorpussharePct{} is computed over the
\numTypeoverlapBanklacks{} references of the first kind and not over the \numCeilingNovel{} of the
second.

The restricted claim does not need that join, because the marginals bound it. The
\numCeilingNovel{} lie inside the \numTypeoverlapBanklacks{}, and only
\numTypeoverlapMinerslost{} of the \numTypeoverlapBanklacks{} carry a type the training annotation
does hold, so at most \numTypeoverlapMinerslost{} of the \numCeilingNovel{} can:
\numBoundCorpusabsentmin{} of them at least, \numBoundCorpusabsentsharemin{}, are chemistry the
corpus does not contain whatever the cross-tabulation would return. Carried to the whole shortfall,
between \numBoundShortfallsharemin{} and \numBoundShortfallsharemax{} of the
\numCeilingUncovered{} references one application of this bank fails to reach are of a type the
training annotation never holds. That is what supports the word \emph{most}, and it is a bound
rather than a point because the join itself is not reported here. Template discovery from that
corpus is separately unfinished, its rarefaction curve still climbing, so mining harder would yield
more templates and could not supply a type the corpus does not contain.
Extending coverage therefore requires new reaction corpora, and the census narrows the search.
Synthetic corpora are excluded by measurement, which leaves enzymatic and regulatory sources. The
regulatory ones are the interesting half: metabolite identifications from radiolabelled human
ADME studies are human \emph{in vivo} data on exactly the population predicted here, they have
been used to benchmark this class of tool \citep{Gao_2026}, and they are, to our knowledge, not
systematically mined as a source of rules. Whether the same bound binds a predictor that
applies no templates at all is not measured here; what is measured is that the chemistry is absent
from the corpus, which is a property of the training data rather than of the method reading it.

It is worth saying plainly what that licenses, because the corpus it is a property of has two
defects this paper also reports. Its assembly cannot be reconstructed, and the one source held on
disk shows the selection was not blind to chemistry. So the bound says: extending the bank by
mining \emph{this} corpus harder cannot close the shortfall, because the chemistry is not in it. It
does not say what fraction of human metabolism is of a type no corpus contains, and it cannot,
because that would need a corpus whose assembly is recoverable and whose selection is documented.
What makes the narrower statement worth having anyway is that this corpus is assembled from the
four sources this field trains on, so a bank mined from any of them inherits the same absence
unless the assembly is changed. The claim is about a training corpus and about the practice of
mining it, not about metabolism.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

GRAIL predicts metabolite structures by selecting templates from a bank of \numBankRules{}
reaction rules, applying them, and ranking the products by a reciprocal rank fusion of the
generator's score and the filter's. The form in which the two learned scores are combined mattered more
than what produced them: replacing their product with the rank fusion gained more recall than any
architectural change tested here. Every prediction carries the template that produced it and the
atoms it acted on, and the whole output is ranked.

The two operating modes divide the same way, and they are two settings of one rule budget rather
than two designs: the setting cheap enough to answer a web form is beaten by the incumbent beyond
a short list, and the setting that leads returns about a hundred structures at a precision near
\numPrecBankFiveZero{} and takes tens of seconds. Neither is the system; both are, and which rule
budget a service should run follows from the output budget it answers at.

Measured against the published predictors that could be run on the same substrates, the
comparison divides by output budget rather than resolving into an ordering, and the division is
the result. Under the evaluation choices swept here, the verdict moves with the matching
criterion and with the budget, so a single recall figure would have named a different leader
depending on decisions its author never had to state. Held to the stricter test the same argument
demands, every arm cut to the comparator's own list length on every substrate, one of the
wide-budget leads is an ordering result and the rest are statements about emission. The class-by-class
reading says the same thing from the other side: the aggregate advantage is carried by unusual
cleavages and by transformations this vocabulary cannot name, and on several classical
functionalisations this system is not the best arm and is sometimes beaten by its own cheaper
setting. Reporting the grid is what makes the comparison checkable, and releasing every arm's
per-substrate predictions, with the references as the descriptors the criteria are decided by, is
what lets a third party recompute it.

What one application of one template bank cannot reach is mostly chemistry the corpus does not
contain. Of the \numTypeoverlapBanklacks{} test references whose transformation type the bank does
not hold, \numTypeoverlapCorpussharePct{} have a type the training annotation does not hold either.
That population is every reference of absent type, including the \numTypeoverlapReached{} the bank
reaches by a template typed differently from the pair. The \numCeilingNovel{} uncovered references
of absent type are not cross-tabulated against it, and they do not need to be: only
\numTypeoverlapMinerslost{} references of absent type carry a type the training annotation holds,
so at least \numBoundCorpusabsentsharemin{} of those \numCeilingNovel{}, and between
\numBoundShortfallsharemin{} and \numBoundShortfallsharemax{} of the whole
\numCeilingUncovered{}-reference shortfall, is chemistry the corpus never contained. What the
figures support is that enlarging this bank from this corpus would not supply the missing
chemistry, which is a statement about the corpus and about no particular bank.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Applying one template once is not the same as one enzymatic step, and two instruments measure the
gap. The registered one counts core-incident edits and calls a template composite at
\numCompositeThreshold{} or more, above every single enzymatic turnover; it flags
\numCompositeEdits{} of the \numCompositeScored{} mined templates it can score, one short of the
\numMinedRules{} in the bank because one does not parse for this purpose, \numCompositeEditsharePct{}, and
clears the bar registered prediction P10 set at \numCompositeBarPct{} before either count existed.
The other counts reaction-centre loci and flags \numCompositeLocisharePct{}, but it errs in both
directions and neither error is measured, so its share is neither a floor nor a bound; the two agree
on only \numCompositeBoth{} templates and their union is \numCompositeUnionsharePct{}
(Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-singlestep}).

The corpus annotates metabolites several enzymatic steps from their parent, and a template mined
from such a pair performs those steps together: inspection finds a rule oxidising two ring
positions at once and a rule that both substitutes and conjugates glutathione. The coverage
ceiling this paper reports is therefore a one-template ceiling and not a one-enzyme one, and the
corrected share is why a second application of the bank buys so little: a fifth of the mined
bank already carries the compositions a second application would have to discover, so it mostly
rediscovers them. A depth-two probe run earlier put the lift in the low percent at several times
the candidate cost, and no figure from it is quoted here, because its result file names no
producer and predates the provenance apparatus the rest of this work is held to. The direction is
what the composite measurement above establishes; the size is not on this paper's footing.

Both learned stages are trained short of what the split allows. The stages saw
\numTrainSubstrates{} of \numSplitTrainSubstrates{} training substrates for
\numTrainEpochsWord{} epochs with early stopping that never engaged, which is an underfitting
signature and not a converged run. The spread is measured at this configuration and reported above,
so what a headline figure is read against is known; what is not reported is retraining on the full
split rather than on the truncated one, under more than \numSpreadSeedsWord{} seeds, which is the
experiment that would say whether the level as well as the spread would move
(Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-retrain}).

The register is weaker than its count of confirmations suggests. Three of the seven confirmed
predictions clear the falsification condition as written, which compares a point estimate to a
threshold, and not the stricter reading that asks the whole interval to clear it: P1, P2 and P5
each have their threshold inside their own interval, so what the data establish for those three is
that the effect exceeds zero and not that it exceeds the registered bar. P5 is weaker again, and
the register is what shows it: its threshold was fixed in advance but its population was not, and
it was adjudicated on the comparison set, which is derived from the test split and was recorded
afterwards. A confirmation whose population became visible before it was chosen is a measurement,
and Table~\ref{tab:hyp} calls it one. P3's threshold is in any
case a property of the budget requested rather than of the system. The group-ranking headroom is smaller than the
uncontrolled figure suggests: over a partition of matched granularity, only the transformation-type
grouping carries anything, and what it carries is unreached by both registered compositions, for
structural reasons.

Two of the reported quantities are bounded by things outside the system. Precision is bounded by
how completely the corpus annotates, so every precision figure here is a lower bound of unknown
depth. And every method runs out of candidates at wide budgets, so the widest columns measure list
length on all sides as much as they measure ordering.

The comparison population is the \numPopulationN{} substrates every method has an entry for, a
quarter of the evaluated test split and not a random quarter of it: it is the submission list sent
to the comparator that is a web service, and how those substrates were drawn from the split is not
recorded anywhere in this repository. Nothing was excluded for emitting nothing, and the population
still contains substrates a method answers nothing for. Two comparators, BioTransformer and GLORYx, are built on
sources this corpus is assembled from, which favours both, and that is not corrected for either.

The deployed configuration is not the best one this work knows of. A blend that discounts
duplication beats the released noisy-or on the validation draw at every budget from one to
\numAggvalHybridwinsmax{}, with the interval excluding zero
(Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-aggregation}), which includes the budget the headline is read at. The
release keeps the noisy-or because every number in this paper is measured under it and switching
would leave the paper describing a system nobody ran; the choice is a parameter rather than a
retraining, so a reader who prefers the blend can make it. GLORYx is the one arm not run on this
machine: the models it needs are not distributed with its source, so its column comes from the
service its authors operate, at the version Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-comparators} records.

The scope statement in Section~\ref{sec:methods-scope} bounds how much stereochemistry could
matter, and the bound is the size of the question rather than an estimate of what it would cost:
\numStereoEither{} of the \numStereoPairs{} annotated pairs, \numStereoSharePct{}, produce a
metabolite carrying a stereogenic element its substrate does not have. The criterion sweep cannot
price a configuration-aware criterion, because two of its five settings return the identical verdict
at every budget: the annotation has no configuration for them to disagree about. Protonation is in
the same position and is bounded the same way, at \numChargeSharePct{} of the annotated metabolites
carrying a formal charge (Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-sensitivity}).

No measurement of a system here is external. Every figure is measured on one corpus assembled from
four sources, of which the only published reference set is one of the four, so a held-out split of
it is not an independent test of the chemistry. The set that would serve is named in
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}: metabolite identifications from radiolabelled human ADME studies,
already used to benchmark this class of tool \citep{Gao_2026}. Measuring a system here on one is
not possible from what that benchmark publishes, because its reference structures appear only as
drawings; until it is, the comparison establishes an ordering within a corpus and not a ranking of
the methods.

The claim about evaluation is a different matter, and it can be checked outside this corpus. Those
authors deposited the prediction files their tools returned, so what each emitted can be counted
rather than inferred. Counted, the axis their design leaves undeclared spans a factor of
\numExtbudgetRatio{} across \numExtbudgetDrugs{} drugs, from \numExtbudgetNarrowest{} candidates
per drug to \numExtbudgetWidest{}. That alone carries the argument: an undeclared quantity that
varies twelvefold between the arms of a comparison is part of what the comparison measures.

The arms that emit more score higher. Over the \numExtbudgetCountedcells{} cells of the
\numExtbudgetGrid{}-cell grid where both the count and the published recall are available, the rank
correlation is $\numExtbudgetRho$, and within a drug, where the reference set is fixed, it is
positive for \numExtbudgetWithinpositive{} of \numExtbudgetWithinn{}. The remaining cells are
accounted for: \numExtbudgetAbsentnodeposit{} belong to the \numExtbudgetArmsnotdepositedWord{}
arms whose predictions were never deposited, and \numExtbudgetAbsentnorecallWord{} are counted but
carry no published recall. Shuffling emission among the arms of a drug, which preserves every
drug's set of emissions and every recall and breaks only their pairing, reproduces the correlation
in fewer than one permutation in \numExtbudgetPermutations{}.

That null holds a drug's emissions fixed but not an arm's, so it cannot say at what level the
association lives, and with \numExtbudgetResidualarmsWord{} counted arms differing more than
tenfold in what they emit, most of the variance is between them rather than inside them. Removing the additive arm and drug
effects from both sides leaves $\numExtbudgetResidual$, so the association is mostly between the
arms and does not vanish inside them. Between the arms is in any case the level a benchmark orders
at: if the arms that emit more rank higher, the ordering measures emission, whatever mechanism puts
it there. The axis is counted from the deposited files and not reconstructed from recall, which
matters: emitted candidates can also be recovered as hits over precision, and since hits are recall
times the reference set, recall would then enter the axis by construction and the same correlation
would arise with nothing behind it (Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-external}).

It is not the whole story and this paper's argument does not need it to be. Ordering the arms by
what they emit does not reproduce ordering them by recall: one arm returns fewer candidates than
another and recovers more. So output size is a confound in that benchmark rather than its content,
which is the same thing this paper says about its own budget axis, and the remedy is the same, to
declare the axis and vary it.

\section{Data and Software Availability}

Source code, the deployed generator and filter checkpoints, the released rule bank, the split
manifest, the annotated references every recall figure in this paper is measured against, the
frozen per-substrate predictions of every comparator, the evaluation harness, the provenance sweep
and the preregistration with every hypothesis and its outcome are at
\texttt{github.com/doctawho42/GRAIL}, and are available to reviewers at submission.

The released bank is not the measured bank, and the difference is a courtesy rather than a
correction. This paper's figures are measured on \numRelbankMeasured{} templates, of which
\numRelbankRemoved{} are present verbatim in BioTransformer's published reaction set. That
distribution is LGPL and grants redistribution with credit, a link to the licence, an indication of
changes and the notices retained, all of which the repository's \texttt{NOTICE} provides; what it
reserves is \emph{commercial} use or redistribution, and this system is intended to run as a
service, so the shipped bank drops those templates to narrow what a commercial user would have to
establish. What ships is the remaining \numRelbankReleased{}. Dropping them from the bank is not
the same as not carrying them, and the distinction is checked rather than asserted: of
\numRelbankCarrierscanned{} tracked files, \numRelbankCarriersWord{} hold at least one of the
\numRelbankRemoved{}, a trained checkpoint that persists the bank it was built against and the
three curated collections the bank was mined from
(\texttt{scripts/check\_no\_withheld\_templates.py}). The removal is free: every reference those templates reach is reached by
another template in the bank, so the change in reach is exactly zero, which
Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-bank} prices alongside what dropping each other rightsholder's templates
would cost. A reader who wants the measured bank can rebuild it by obtaining BioTransformer's
reaction set from its own project and re-adding the templates
\texttt{scripts/build\_released\_bank.py} names, whose digest that script records.

One obligation is open rather than settled, and it is named because it is not ours to close:
BioTransformer's permission to redistribute is the authors' to grant, and until it is, its templates
stay out of the release. The second is closed. Its environmental-microbial file is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0,
which no release under \RepoLicence{} can carry, and \numRelbankEnvmicroonly{} of the
\numRelbankEnvmicroWord{} templates of it this bank holds are unique to that file, so the removal
above already takes both. The repository's licence is \RepoLicence{}, which is what the remaining
GPL templates permit rather than a preference, and \texttt{NOTICE.md} carries the decisions file by
file.

The annotated references are released as the descriptors the matching criteria are decided by rather
than as structures, so a reader who does not hold the corpus sources' licences can still recompute
every recall figure and every cell of the criterion sweep; a check reports zero disagreements
between the two. Releasing the checkpoints is the bar this paper applies to others: a system whose
weights cannot be obtained cannot be compared against, which is why one of the
\numComparatorsUnavailable{} systems of 2025--26 is excluded here. The train and validation
candidate pools are deposited at \ZenodoDoi{}. The corpus is assembled from ChEMBL
\citep{Zdrazil_2024}, DrugBank \citep{Wishart_2018}, MetXBioDB \citep{Djoumbou_Feunang_2019} and
the GLORYx reference set \citep{de_Bruyn_Kops_2020}, at versions \CorpusVersions{}.

The corpus is not redistributed as a corpus, and the reason is a limitation of this release rather
than an open question. ChEMBL is released under a ShareAlike licence and DrugBank under a
NonCommercial one, and a single derivative drawing on both cannot satisfy each: the non-commercial
clause is a restriction ShareAlike forbids adding, and dropping it breaches DrugBank's terms. So the
question of the derived corpus's licence is \CorpusLicence{}, and what is released is narrower and
named exactly: the evaluated test substrates and their annotated metabolites as structures, not the
training and validation splits, not the negatives, and not the source records. A reader holding the
four sources' licences can obtain the records, compute the same tautomer-canonical hashes and join
them to everything released here, and the split manifest confirms a rebuild matches rather than
merely reporting that it differs. The one step that cannot be reproduced is the extraction that
selected from the four sources, which is not in the repository and is recorded as such.

The hydrogen convention is an argument to the rule-application entry point rather than a
constant, and its default is the presentation every deployed firing path uses, which is the one the
paper's figures are measured at. The choice is consequential and the entry point should not make it
silently: reproducing the census through it under the other presentation gives
\numHydUncoveredexpanded{} uncovered references and not \numCeilingUncovered{}. That other
presentation is not an alternative reading of the bound. It expands the substrate's hydrogens
without completing the templates to match, which Section~\ref{sec:ceiling} disqualifies as an
incomplete loop rather than a convention; the number is quoted here only to show how far a silent
default could move a figure. Every call site
that produced a figure here names the convention it was measured under rather than taking it from
a default.

Almost every figure printed in this paper reaches the page from a stored result file through a
generated macro rather than typed; \numProvHandtyped{} measurements do not, they are enumerated
in the checker rather than covered by a wildcard, and the Supporting Information says which.
Two properties are involved and they are not the same one. A file is \emph{stamped} when it
records the script that wrote it and the version of that script; it is \emph{pinned} when it is on
the list this work maintains of the artifacts a check must find current. \numProvPinned{} files are
pinned, and \numProvPinnedcurrent{} of them pass the currency check. Of the pinned set,
\numProvSources{} are the ones this paper's figures are drawn from, and none of those figures comes
from outside it.
 The chain from artifact to printed
macro runs end to end in a clone, which is not where it started
(Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-prov}).

The wider sweep is over \numProvFiles{} files and its parts are worth printing, since a total is
easier to accept than to add up: \numProvUnstamped{} carry no stamp, \numProvChanged{} have a
producer that has changed since, \numProvProducerunknown{} have a producer the sweep cannot
identify, \numProvCosmetic{} changed only cosmetically, \numProvCurrentunpinned{} are stamped and
current without being pinned, and \numProvPinnedinresults{} are the pinned files that live under
the results directory the total names. Those six add to the total. A split manifest pins by
digest the triple files, the substrate sets, the evaluated test set, the rule bank and every
third-party artifact, and a verify mode recomputes and names any file that has moved. Candidate
pools record, by digest, the two checkpoints that scored them; both stages of the released system
come from one training run, and for the pools frozen before that field existed the run is
established by reproduction rather than by recollection. The Supporting Information describes the checks and states
what they do not cover.

Two pinned artifacts are too large to commit: the train and validation candidate pools, which
hold every candidate the generator produced for every substrate of their population together with
both component scores, and which are the deposit named above. The repository pins the
SHA-256 of both the raw file and the uploaded archive in
\texttt{paper2/zenodo\_manifest.json}, and \texttt{scripts/zenodo\_deposit.py -{}-verify}
checks a download against it, so the deposit is verifiable against the version used here rather
than merely available.

The guarantee covers the pinned set and not the directory: of \numProvFiles{} files under
\texttt{results/}, \numProvUnstamped{} carry no stamp and \numProvChanged{} were written by a
producer that has since changed. Those files support no number in this paper.
