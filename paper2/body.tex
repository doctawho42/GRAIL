\section{Introduction}

When a drug or an environmental chemical enters an organism, enzymes convert it into other
compounds. Which compounds, as structures and not merely as a count, matters for toxicology, for
drug design and for environmental fate assessment, because a metabolite can be more toxic, more
persistent or more active than the parent.

Two families of predictor dominate. Rule-based systems encode biotransformations as reaction
templates and enumerate products by applying them. Sequence-to-sequence systems translate the
substrate's string representation into metabolite strings. A further wave has appeared recently,
including a chemical language model, a mechanistically informed graph framework and a server
predicting pathway, site and product together.

Comparing any two of these is harder than it looks, and the difficulty is not effort. A recall
figure depends on at least two settings that papers in this area report apart from their rankings
or not at all: the rule deciding when a predicted structure counts as the reference, and the
number of candidates a system may emit. A system emitting seventy candidates and one emitting
eight are not measured on the same axis.

GRAIL is built on the opposite premise: every evaluation choice is declared and the system is
reported under all of them rather than under one. The claims below are therefore a sweep rather
than a single number, and the artifacts sufficient to recompute any cell are released.

The design contribution is narrower than the evaluation contribution and we say so. The
architecture is a rule bank, a learned rule selector and a learned pair filter, a familiar shape.
What changed the outcome was neither a new network nor new chemistry but the form in which two
existing scores are combined: replacing their product with a rank fusion is worth
$+\numHSevenDiff$ of recall on held-out data, more than any architectural change we tested.

\section{Materials and methods}

\subsection{Which population carries which number}

Three populations appear here and no figure is stated without one. The \emph{test split} is
\numPopulationTestsubs{} substrates carrying \numPopulationTestrefs{} references and is where the
coverage ceiling and the type census are measured. The \emph{validation split} is
\numHOneFiveNpaired{} substrates carrying \numHOneFiveNreferences{} references and is where every
registered ranking and timing threshold is checked, since a threshold checked where its rule was
chosen is not a check. The \emph{comparison set} is the \numPopulationN{} substrates on which
every method predicts.

\subsection{Overview}

GRAIL predicts in three stages, each separately inspectable. A generator scores every rule in a
bank of \numBankRules{} SMIRKS templates against the substrate; selected rules are applied with
RDKit 2022.09.5 and the products standardised and deduplicated; a filter scores each
(substrate, candidate) pair, and candidates are ordered by a rank fusion of the filter and
generator orderings. Every emitted candidate carries the identity of the rule that produced it and
the site at which it fired.

\subsection{Rule bank}

The bank holds \numBankRules{} templates spanning \numBankTypes{} distinct reaction types, where a
type is the multiset of bonds that change between mapped atoms. It is the deduplicated union of
four curated collections together with templates mined from the molecule-disjoint training split
alone. For each annotated substrate--product pair a ring-aware maximum common substructure is
located, the atoms and bonds that changed are flagged as the reaction centre, the centre is
expanded by one bond and emitted as a mapped SMIRKS. A template survives only if applying it to
its own source substrate regenerates the annotated product after canonicalisation.

Support is heavily skewed and mining is saturated: re-running the miner on the same split
re-derives every template it derived before and none new.

\subsection{Rank fusion}

Both learned stages produce an opinion about each candidate. The deployed system previously
ordered candidates by the product of the two scores. A product is scale-sensitive: if one scorer
occupies a narrow range and the other a wide one, the wide one determines the order.

The deployed combination is reciprocal rank fusion \citep{cormack2009},
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RRF}(c) = \sum_{i} \frac{1}{K + r_i(c)}, \qquad K = 60,
\end{equation}
where $r_i(c)$ is the rank of $c$ under scorer $i$. Only positions enter, so the two opinions
contribute equally whatever their scales.

This was registered as hypothesis H7 with a threshold of $+\numHSevenThreshold$ and evaluated on
the validation split, where it had not been selected: $+\numHSevenDiff$, 95\% CI
$[+\numHSevenLo, +\numHSevenHi]$. On the set where the variant had been chosen among four it gave
$+0.109$; the shrinkage to two thirds is the measured cost of that selection.

\subsection{Pool cap}

Before fusion the pool is truncated to the \numHNineCap{} highest-scoring candidates by generator
score, a quantity available before any pair graph is built. Registered as H9 with a threshold of
$+\numHNineThreshold$; measured $+\numHNineDiff$, 95\% CI $[+\numHNineLo, +\numHNineHi]$, mean
pool \numHNinePoolBefore{} to \numHNinePoolAfter{}. The cap was registered to gain and it gains:
it removes a few candidates that were a substrate's only correct answer and many more that were
occupying the middle of the list. The value was fixed by a rule rather than by search, the
smallest round cap at twice the largest reported budget, so that the widest column is not a
tautology.

\subsection{Rule budget and two operating modes}

The trained checkpoint records a rule budget of \numHOneZeroTopk{}, while every pool in the
project had been built with the whole bank and what that setting costs had never been measured.
Registered as H10 with a ceiling of $+\numHOneZeroThreshold$ for what the whole bank buys: on validation
$+\numHOneZeroBought$, 95\% CI $[\numHOneZeroLo, +\numHOneZeroHi]$, mean pool
\numHOneZeroPoolBank{} to \numHOneZeroPoolTrained{}.

\subsection{Emission}

Under the product combination the system emitted candidates scoring within $\alpha = 0.5$ of the
leader. That rule does not transfer to a rank fusion, and the reason is structural: the worst
candidate scores $0.88$ of the best under fusion against $0.016$ under the product, because a
reciprocal-rank sum is a rank statistic without scale. At $\alpha = 0.5$ the rule admits
$16.22$ candidates from a pool of $16.3$, truncating nothing.

The deployed emission is therefore the whole pool of the operating budget, with no threshold and
no parameter. Registered as H11 with a criterion of zero lost cells over a grid of five matching
criteria by eleven budgets: \numHOneOneLost{} of \numHOneOneCells{}.

\subsection{Evaluation}

What counts as a hit is a declared axis with five settings: canonical SMILES equality, the full
InChIKey, its stereochemistry-blind first block, Tanimoto similarity of one over Morgan
fingerprints, and a tautomer-aware key that canonicalises tautomers on both sides. The last is the
default, because the references neither carry stereochemistry nor distinguish tautomers. All five
are reported for every comparison.

Two aggregations are distinguished throughout and never mixed within a comparison: macro, the mean
of per-substrate recall, and micro, the ratio of sums. Intervals are paired bootstrap over
substrates, 10\,000 resamples, seed 0. Precision is reported but is not used to order systems:
under incomplete annotation an unannotated but real metabolite counts as a false positive, so a
system emitting fewer candidates is rewarded for reasons unrelated to its chemistry.

No single budget is privileged. A claim that the system leads is made for a cell only where that
cell's interval excludes zero, and where an ordering is not established the table says so.

\subsection{Group ranking, and two mechanisms that failed}

Candidates group by molecular formula. An oracle ordering these groups by whether they contain a
reference is worth $+\numOracleHeadroom$ over the ranking it is measured against, which is the
product ordering with each group emitted as a block on the uncapped pool. That is not the deployed
ranking, so the figure bounds a configuration the server does not run.

H8 trained a listwise group scorer applied to whole groups. Registered threshold $+\numHEightThreshold$; measured
on the comparison set, $\numHEightDiff$. The deficit splits into $\numHEightDesign$ from the
design, block emission being worse than interleaved (\numHEightBlocked{} against
\numHEightInterleaved{}), and $\numHEightModel$ from the model. The oracle had been measured in
blocked form, so a third of the headroom it promised was the return of what the design spends.

H12 used the same model untrained further, entering its group score as a third ranking in the same
fusion. Measured $+\numHOneTwoDiff$, 95\% CI $[+\numHOneTwoLo, +\numHOneTwoHi]$. The effect is not
selection: all twenty configurations beat the base on validation and 70\% of that margin survived
the move.

H14 tested the converse, the group signal as a gate before fusion, at a registered
$+\numHOneFourThreshold$. Measured $\numHOneFourDiff$,
separated against the hypothesis, and the mechanism is closed by an oracle rather than by model
quality: a perfect group ranking through the gate gives $\numHOneFourCeiling$, worse than the
generator cap it replaces. The median substrate has one group containing a reference while filling
the cap admits at least seven, so after the single informative group the gate spends its budget on
groups its signal cannot separate. The two results are symmetric, $+\numHOneTwoDiff$ as a nudge on
the generator's ordering and $\numHOneFourDiff$ as a replacement for it.

A binary group ordering entered as the third term reaches \numHOneTwoCeilingrecall{}, the same the
trained scorer reaches. That is not an upper bound and we do not use it as one: a binary ordering
leaves every reference-free group tied and on validation the trained scorer exceeds it,
$+\numHOneTwoVal$ against $+\numHOneTwoValceiling$.

\subsection{Runtime}

Standardisation was the binding constraint and its diagnosis was wrong twice before it was right.
Three explanations were tested and rejected by measurement: the filter's maximum-common-substructure
step, rule application, and substrate size as an axis. The cause was product standardisation, at
\numHOneThreeEnumeration{} times the remaining work, run once per product of every template. A
cache had concealed it, a repeated substrate running many times faster.

Moving standardisation to surviving candidates (H13) freed recall, $\numHOneThreeRecallDiff$
against a cap of $0.01$, but failed on time, $\numHOneThreeFactor\times$ against a registered
$10\times$. The mechanism was right and the arithmetic was not: enumeration collapsed, and
standardising the survivors became the majority of the new arm. Removing most of the work does not
remove most of the time once the remainder is the new majority.

Adding a tautomer enumeration budget of \numHOneFiveBudget{} (H15) passes both halves: median
\numHOneThreeTimeBefore~s to \numHOneFiveTime~s against a registered target below ten, recall loss
\numHOneFiveRecallDiff{} against a cap of $0.01$. Of \numHOneFiveCandidates{} candidates,
\numHOneFiveMoved{} standardise to a different molecule under the bounded budget.

Part of the speed-up is machine load. The enumeration step is identical in the two arms, so the
ratio of its medians, \numHOneFiveLoad{}, measures load alone; dividing it out leaves
\numHOneFiveAttributable$\times$ attributable to the budget, and applying it to the whole arm puts
the median at \numHOneFiveMedianUnderload~s under the other arm's load, still inside the target.

The budget lives in a module-level singleton shared with the matching key, so lowering it would
silently have moved what counts as a hit. A private enumerator was introduced and the keys were
verified unchanged before the run.

\subsection{The ceiling is a property of the corpus}

Applying the whole bank to every test substrate recovers \numCeilingCoverage{} of references in
one step. The remainder divides by the reaction-centre change it requires: \numCeilingNovel{} need
a transformation type the bank does not hold, \numCeilingKnown{} need a type it holds whose rule
did not fire, and \numCeilingUntypeable{} cannot be typed.

Relaxing the convention-dependent primitives in the templates recovers three of the
\numCeilingKnown{}, moving the ceiling by $+0.0012$. The a-priori bound had been computed in
advance and predicted about eight.

The \numCeilingNovel{} misses of absent type collapse into \numCensusTypes{} distinct types, of
which \numCensusOnce{} occur once and carry \numCensusOnceShare{} of the mass. The objection that
this is an artefact of how finely a type is defined is answered by varying the definition.

The tail holds at every granularity at which a type still determines a product and collapses only
where it stops doing so. What the table supports is not that \numCensusOnceShare{} of the gap is
singletons under our definition; it is that the bound is invariant to the definition of a type
across the whole range in which the definition is meaningful, which makes it a fact about the
annotation corpus rather than about our choice.

Nor is the missing chemistry available in the largest reaction library we hold. Typing
\numUsptoTemplates{} retrosynthetic templates in the metabolic direction gives \numUsptoTypes{}
types, of which \numUsptoHit{} are among the \numCensusTypes{} missing, carrying \numUsptoMass{}
of the \numCeilingNovel{} misses. The control that makes this readable: bank and library share
\numUsptoSanity{} types, so the intersection measures real absence rather than two incomparable
formalisms.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Where the advantage is, and where it is not}

The comparison does not produce a winner and we do not present one. SyGMa recovers more at the
head of the list and at $k \le 5$ the interval separates against us. What GRAIL does is keep
improving where the others stop. A user reading three candidates is better served by SyGMa; a user
reading fifty is better served here, and the paper says both.

That division has a consequence for how such tools are compared. A single recall figure at a
single budget would have named a different winner depending on which budget its author chose, and
every one of those choices would have been defensible. This is the argument for reporting the
sweep rather than a cell, made on our own numbers rather than about someone else's.

\subsection{What the system offers that its predecessor does not}

Three things, none a recall figure. Every prediction names the transformation rule that produced
it and the site at which it fired, so a user can accept or reject a candidate on chemical grounds.
The whole output is ranked, where the incumbent ranks part of its output and leaves the remainder
in file order. And every evaluation choice is declared with every cell of the resulting grid
reported, which is what makes the comparison reproducible by a third party.

\subsection{What the numbers do not support}

GRAIL does not lead at the tight budgets. At $k=15$ and $20$ no arm separates from the strongest
comparator. The established lead is at $k=30$ and $50$. The system is not compared against the
2025--26 wave, because that wave cannot be run as released: of \numComparatorsUnavailable{} such systems, one releases
training code without a checkpoint, one has a withdrawn module, one offers only a web form and one
has no repository. Precision figures are bounded by annotation completeness. And the advantage at
wide budgets is partly a statement about output length on all sides rather than about ranking.

\subsection{The ceiling bounds this formulation, not the task}

The shortfall is a property of the corpus the templates are mined from, established by invariance
rather than asserted, and mining is saturated. Extending coverage requires new reaction corpora,
and the census narrows the search: synthetic corpora are excluded by measurement, leaving
enzymatic and regulatory sources. Metabolite identifications from radiolabelled human ADME studies
reported in regulatory submissions are human \emph{in vivo} data on exactly the population
predicted here and are, to our knowledge, not systematically mined. The bound applies to any
predictor trained on a comparable corpus, including sequence-to-sequence systems, which do not
report it.

\section{Limitations}

Predictions are single-step; applying the bank at depth two lifts the ceiling by about $0.012$ at
$8.5$ times the candidate cost and is not deployed. Precision is bounded by annotation
completeness. The rule-budget threshold in H10 proved to be a property of the requested budget
rather than of the system. The group-ranking headroom is real and unreached by both registered
compositions for structural reasons. Every method runs out of candidates at wide budgets, so the
widest columns measure list length on all sides. The comparison population is the
\numPopulationN{} substrates on which all methods predict, a quarter of the evaluated test split
and not a random quarter of it.
