\title[Metabolite prediction under a declared evaluation]
      {Undeclared Choices Reorder Metabolite Predictors, and the Corpus Bounds Any Bank Mined From It}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Published predictors of metabolite structure cannot be compared with one another. Recall depends on
how a predicted structure is judged to match a reference and on how many candidates a system is
allowed to return, and papers in this area declare neither. We promote both decisions to declared
axes and sweep them, running published predictors and two configurations of a rule-grounded system
of our own (GRAIL) on a common set of drug-like substrates. The comparison does not resolve into an
ordering: which system leads depends on the matching criterion and on the number of candidates
allowed, and holding every system to the same list length removes most of the leads that a wide
budget produces. The same confound is present in an independent benchmark of this class of tool, in which
the systems that emit more score higher. We then bound what one application of one template bank
can reach, and find that most of what it misses is transformation chemistry the training corpus
never contains, which makes the bound a property of that corpus rather than of any one bank.
\end{abstract}
