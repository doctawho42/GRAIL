\title[GRAIL: rule-grounded metabolite prediction]
      {GRAIL: rule-grounded metabolite prediction under a declared evaluation}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Predicting which metabolites an enzyme system will produce from a drug or an environmental
chemical is central to toxicology, drug design and environmental fate assessment, yet published
predictors cannot be compared with one another: recall depends on how a predicted structure is
judged to match a reference and on how many candidates a system is allowed to emit, and papers in
this area report neither consistently. We present GRAIL (Graph-scored Rule Application with
Inspectable Localisation), a rule-grounded predictor that names the transformation rule and the
site behind every prediction, evaluated with those two choices promoted to declared axes and
swept, against every published predictor we could obtain and run on the same substrates; the
comparison does not resolve into a single ordering, so we report the whole grid rather than a
cell. We then bound what one application of one template bank can reach, by measuring how much of
the annotated chemistry it does not recover. Most of that shortfall is
transformation chemistry the training corpus never contains: of the references whose transformation
type the bank does not hold, nine in ten have a type the training annotation does not hold either,
which makes the shortfall a property of the corpora this literature is built on rather than of any
one bank.
\end{abstract}
