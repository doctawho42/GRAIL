\begin{abstract}
Tools that predict xenobiotic metabolite structures report recall under evaluation choices their
papers leave unstated, so their numbers are not comparable. We present GRAIL, a rule-grounded
predictor that names the transformation rule and site behind every prediction and reports its
performance under \numCritCriteria{} matching criteria and \numCritBudgets{} output budgets against every comparator with
predictions on the same substrates. On \numPopulationN{} shared substrates the comparison divides
by budget rather than resolving into an ordering, with SyGMa ahead at $k\le5$ and GRAIL ahead at
$k=30$ and $k=50$, each with the paired interval excluding zero. We also bound the approach:
\numCeilingUncovered{} of \numPopulationTestrefs{} reference metabolites lie outside what the rule
bank reaches in one step, and that bound moves by at most eight references across the hydrogen
conventions our own instrument admits.
\end{abstract}
