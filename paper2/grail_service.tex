\title{Predicting xenobiotic metabolite structures at a budget:\\
what decides the ranking, what bounds it, and what it costs}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A metabolite-structure predictor is used at a budget: a chemist reads a handful of candidates, not
a ranked list of six hundred. We report a rule-based-plus-learned predictor evaluated against
MetaTox on \numPopulationN{} substrates carrying \numPopulationReferences{} annotated metabolites,
at every output budget the field reports, with a paired bootstrap on each difference.

Two changes to how existing scores are combined account for the result, and neither is a new model.
Ranking candidates by reciprocal rank fusion of the filter and generator scores rather than by
their product is worth $\numHSevenDiff$ of micro recall@15 on a held-out split
($95\%$ CI $[\numHSevenLo, \numHSevenHi]$), and capping the candidate pool at
$\numHNineCapped$\footnote{See Section~\ref{sec:cap}.} before ranking is worth a further
$\numHNineDiff$ $[\numHNineLo, \numHNineHi]$ while cutting the pool from
$\numHNinePoolBefore$ candidates to $\numHNinePoolAfter$. Applying the whole
$\numBankRules$-template bank rather than the $\numHOneZeroTopk$ templates the trained model records
buys $\numHOneZeroBought$ $[\numHOneZeroLo, \numHOneZeroHi]$ at that budget, an interval containing zero.

Against MetaTox the deployed configuration leads at every budget, with the interval separating at
seven of nine, while emitting $\numOutputTrained$ candidates against MetaTox's
$\numOutputMetatox$. The comparison is reported as the whole sweep because no single budget is
privileged.

The bound on all of this is the rule bank's coverage, $\numCeilingCoverage$ of annotated
transformations. We decompose what is missing: $\numCeilingNovel$ of $\numCeilingUncovered$
uncovered references need a reaction type absent from the bank, and those collapse into
$\numCensusTypes$ distinct types, $\numCensusOnce$ of them seen once and carrying
$\numCensusOnceShare$ of the mass. The largest synthetic-chemistry template library available to
us, $\numUsptoTemplates$ USPTO templates, supplies $\numUsptoHit$ of those types, covering
$\numUsptoShare$ of the gap. The ceiling is a property of the annotation corpus and not of the
rule formalism, and we state it as a boundary rather than as future work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Predicting which structures a xenobiotic becomes in the body is a step in drug safety assessment
and in environmental fate analysis. Tools that do it fall into two families. Rule-based systems
apply a curated bank of transformation templates and rank the enumerated products; learned systems
generate products directly. The rule-based family dominates deployment because its output is
auditable: every prediction names the template that produced it.

The evaluation of such tools is usually reported at one output budget, most often the top fifteen
candidates. That is a choice, and it is not the only one a user makes. A screening application
reads three candidates and a regulatory dossier reads fifty. We therefore report every budget the
comparators are read at, together, with an interval on each difference, and privilege none.

Our contributions are as follows.

\begin{itemize}
\item The ranking gains come from combining existing scores differently, not from a new model. We
  fix each combination rule in a preregistration before checking it, and report the checks whether
  they hold or fail.
\item We locate the cost. Standardising enumerated products, not applying templates and not
  scoring pairs, is $\numHOneThreeEnumeration$ times the rest of the generator's work, and moving
  it off the enumeration loop with a bounded tautomer budget brings the median substrate from
  $\numHOneThreeTimeBefore$ seconds to $\numHOneFiveTime$.
\item We decompose the coverage ceiling into what a template library could supply and what only a
  new annotation corpus could, and we report the second as a boundary.
\end{itemize}

\section{System}

The predictor runs in three stages. A \emph{generator} scores each of the bank's
$\numBankRules$ SMIRKS templates against the substrate graph and selects which to apply. RDKit
applies the selected templates and enumerates products. A \emph{filter} scores each
(substrate, product) pair. Candidates carry two scores, one from each learned stage, and the
ranking is a function of the two.

The bank contains $\numBankTypes$ distinct reaction types, where a type is the multiset of bonds
that change between mapped atoms. Coverage, ranking and cost are three separate questions about
this system and the sections below take them in that order.

\subsection{Evaluation}

Predictions and references are matched by tautomer-canonical InChIKey: both sides pass through the
same standardisation before the key is taken, so a proton shift does not separate a prediction from
its reference. Recall is micro, the ratio of sums over the population, and every difference carries
a $95\%$ paired bootstrap over substrates with $10{,}000$ resamples.

The comparison population is the \numPopulationN{} substrates for which MetaTox predictions are
available, carrying \numPopulationReferences{} annotated metabolites. None of them appears in the
data the model was trained or selected on. We checked this rather than assumed it: the same audit
finds $\numGloryxOverlap$ of $\numGloryxN$ substrates of a set this project had called external
inside the training split, and figures on that set are reported on the remainder.

\section{What decides the ranking}

\subsection{The combination of the two scores}

The deployed system ranked candidates by the product of the filter and generator scores. The
product is scale-sensitive: a candidate the filter is confident about and the generator is not
falls to the bottom whatever its rank under either. Reciprocal rank fusion \citep{cormack2009}
combines the two orderings instead of the two values,
\[
\mathrm{score}(c) = \frac{1}{60 + r_{\mathrm{filter}}(c)} + \frac{1}{60 + r_{\mathrm{generator}}(c)},
\]
with the published constant and no weight between the terms, so the rule fits nothing.

On a held-out split micro recall@15 is $\numHSevenProduct$ under the product and
$\numHSevenFusion$ under fusion, a difference of $\numHSevenDiff$ with a $95\%$ interval of
$[\numHSevenLo, \numHSevenHi]$. The rule and the margin were fixed in advance of this measurement,
at half the margin the rule showed on the population where four combinations had been compared and
the best taken.

\subsection{The cap on the pool}
\label{sec:cap}

The bank enumerates $\numHNinePoolBefore$ candidates for the median substrate. Keeping the
$\numHNineCapped$ highest by generator score before ranking, a quantity available before any pair
graph is built, raises micro recall@15 from $\numHNineUncapped$ to $\numHNineCapped$ on the
held-out split. The difference is $\numHNineDiff$ $[\numHNineLo, \numHNineHi]$ against a
registered $\numHNineThreshold$.

The cap was expected to cost recall and to be adopted anyway, since it bounds the filter's work.
It gains instead. The shape says where: the cap costs a little at the tightest budgets and pays
with depth, which is what removing candidates that crowd the middle of a list looks like.

\subsection{The rule budget}

Every pool in this project had been built by applying all $\numBankRules$ templates, while the
trained model records $\numHOneZeroTopk$. Nobody had measured the difference. On the held-out split the
whole bank buys $\numHOneZeroBought$ of micro recall@15 over the trained budget, with an interval of
$[\numHOneZeroLo, \numHOneZeroHi]$ that contains zero, while the mean pool falls from
$\numHOneZeroPoolBank$ candidates to $\numHOneZeroPoolTrained$.

That comparison is bounded to budgets the smaller arm can fill. Thirty templates yield seventeen
candidates, so above a budget of fifteen the arm is not ranking worse, it is returning a short
list. We report the count of exhausted lists at every budget alongside the recall, on both sides.

\subsection{What did not work}

Two further ways of using the same signals were registered and failed, and both failures are
informative.

The ordering loss is almost entirely a matter of which product formula to spend the budget on.
Handing the ranker a perfect choice of formula group lifts micro recall@15 from
$\numOracleAsranked$ to $\numOracleBetween$; a perfect choice of isomer inside a group lifts it to
$\numOracleWithin$. That headroom does not yield to a decoding rule: capping how many candidates
one formula may take in the early slots hurts at every strength tested, from
$\numDecodeRrf$ uncapped down to $\numDecodeCapOne$ at one per group. The oracle's advantage is
selective and not diversifying, and spreading a budget across formulas is the opposite of choosing
among them rather than an approximation to it.

Nor does it yield to a trained group scorer. A listwise model over formula groups, ordering the
groups and leaving the order inside one alone, scores $\numHEightDiff$ against fusion. The failure
splits: the design costs $\numHEightDesign$ before the model acts, because ordering groups and
leaving their interiors alone forces every member of a formula to be emitted adjacently, and the
same fusion ranking blocked that way is worse than interleaved. The model costs a further
$\numHEightModel$.

Entering the same group score as a third ranking of the fusion rule works and is bounded. It is
worth $\numHOneTwoDiff$ $[\numHOneTwoLo, \numHOneTwoHi]$, and a \emph{perfect} group ranking
entered the same way is worth $\numHOneTwoCeiling$: a reciprocal-rank term is bounded, so however
good the group ordering is it can only nudge. Using the score to select instead, admitting whole
groups until the cap is reached, loses $\numHOneFourDiff$, and a perfect group ranking used that
way loses $\numHOneFourCeiling$. The between-group signal is worth something as a nudge over the
generator's ordering and less than nothing as a replacement for it.

\subsection{The emitted set}

A ranked list at a fixed budget is an evaluation device. A service returns a set. Our emission rule
has no parameter: the service returns what the trained rule budget produces, ordered by the fusion
rule, truncated by nothing, which comes to $\numHOneOneOutputRule$ candidates against MetaTox's
$\numHOneOneOutputMetatox$.

Compared at each method's own emission over five matching criteria crossed with the budgets MetaTox
is read at, the rule loses $\numHOneOneLost$ of $\numHOneOneCells$ cells on macro $F_1$. That
comparison is biased toward the smaller emitter, because precision counts an unannotated true
metabolite as a false positive and our rule emits less than half as much. The component that is not
open to that objection: read at a budget of fifteen, where MetaTox emits about what the rule emits,
its macro recall is $\numHOneOneRecallMetatoxOneFive$ against the rule's
$\numHOneOneRecallRule$.

\section{The ceiling}

The bank reaches $\numCeilingCoverage$ of annotated transformations on the test split. No ranking
changes that number, and it bounds everything above.

Of $\numCeilingUncovered$ uncovered references, $\numCeilingKnown$ need a reaction type the bank
already contains, $\numCeilingUntypeable$ admit no type, and $\numCeilingNovel$ need a type the
bank lacks. The first group is a template-generalisation problem and the relaxations we tried
recover three of it. The third is the interesting one, and its size alone does not say what to do
about it.

We therefore counted the types. The $\numCeilingNovel$ misses collapse into $\numCensusTypes$
distinct types; $\numCensusOnce$ of those are seen exactly once and carry $\numCensusOnceShare$ of
the mass, and half the mass needs $\numCensusHalf$ types.

A count like that is only as informative as the definition of a type behind it, and ours is a
multiset of changed bonds with multiplicities, which is nearly a fingerprint of a whole
transformation. Near-uniqueness could therefore be the definition rather than the chemistry, so we
report the tail against every coarsening of the definition available to us
(Table~\ref{tab:grain}), and mark for each whether a type at that level still determines a
product.

The tail survives every definition under which a type still names a transformation, and collapses
only at levels where it does not: knowing that a carbon-oxygen bond changes somewhere does not
determine a product. The statement the table supports is therefore not that
$\numCensusOnceShare$ of the gap is singletons under our definition. It is that the boundary is
invariant to the definition of a type across the whole range in which the definition is
meaningful, which makes it a fact about the annotation corpus and not about a choice of ours.

Nor is the chemistry sitting in a library we could import. We typed
$\numUsptoTemplates$ USPTO retrosynthetic templates in both directions, giving
$\numUsptoTypes$ distinct types, and $\numUsptoHit$ of the $\numCensusTypes$ missing types appear
among them, carrying $\numUsptoMass$ of the $\numCeilingNovel$ misses. That intersection is small
and it is not an artefact of incomparable vocabularies: the bank and USPTO share
$\numUsptoSanity$ types, so the two type systems do meet.

We state the consequence rather than defer it. Closing this gap needs new annotated metabolic
chemistry, one transformation at a time, and no rearrangement of the rule formalism or of the
ranking substitutes for it.

\section{Cost}

At the whole bank, $\numEnvelopeUnfinished$ of $\numEnvelopeN$ substrates do not finish within
$\numEnvelopeDeadline$ seconds, and the smallest that fails carries $\numEnvelopeSmallestUnfinished$
heavy atoms. The failures are ordinary drug-sized molecules, so the envelope is not an exotic case.

The cost is not where template application or pair scoring would put it. Applying all templates to
a $109$-heavy-atom substrate takes $5.2$ seconds. Standardising the enumerated products takes the
rest: it is $\numHOneThreeEnumeration$ times the remaining work, run once per product of every
template, about four thousand times per substrate to keep the hundred that survive the cap.

Two changes follow. Deduplicating during enumeration on a cheap canonical form and standardising
only the survivors moves the median substrate from $\numHOneThreeTimeBefore$ seconds to
$\numHOneThreeTimeAfter$, a factor of $\numHOneThreeFactor$, and costs
$\numHOneThreeRecallDiff$ of micro recall@15. Bounding the tautomer enumerator to
$\numHOneFiveBudget$ for those survivors, with the matching key left at the shipped setting and
computed by a separate enumerator, brings the median to $\numHOneFiveTime$ seconds at a recall
cost of $\numHOneFiveRecallDiff$. Of $\numHOneFiveCandidates$ candidates, $\numHOneFiveMoved$
standardise to a different molecule under the bounded budget.

Part of that speed-up is the machine and not the change. The two arms did not run under the same
load, and their enumeration step is identical, so its ratio measures the load alone:
$\numHOneFiveLoad$. Dividing it out leaves $\numHOneFiveAttributable$ attributable to the tautomer
budget.

\section{Limitations}

The ceiling of $\numCeilingCoverage$ is the dominant one and Section~5 is our account of it.

At a budget of fifteen and above, the interval on the difference to MetaTox does not separate for
either configuration. We report the point estimates and the intervals and make no claim there.

The between-group ordering headroom, worth $\numOracleBetween$ against $\numOracleAsranked$ under
an oracle, is not reached by either composition we registered. We do not know whether a mechanism
outside those two reaches it.

Matching is tautomer-canonical and its canonicaliser does not always converge: on enumerated
tautomers it sends all forms of one molecule to one key for $\numTautInvarianceShipped$ of sampled
products. Measured on the data instead, of $\numNearmissReferences$ validation references,
$\numNearmissUnmatched$ are matched by no candidate and $\numNearmissConfirmed$ of those are
present in the pool as a confirmed tautomer, so the shortfall costs nothing we can detect.

\section{Availability}

The rule bank, the trained checkpoints, the frozen split, the preregistration with every
hypothesis and its outcome, the per-substrate predictions and every analysis script are released.
Each reported artifact carries the hash of the code that wrote it, and a sweep refuses any artifact
whose producer has changed since it was written.
