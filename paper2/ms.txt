Abstract

1

Published metabolite-structure predictors cannot be compared with one another. Recall depends on how a predicted structure is judged to
match a reference and on how many candidates a
system is allowed to emit, and papers in this area
report neither consistently. We promote both
to declared axes and sweep them, running five
published predictors and two configurations of
a rule-grounded system of our own (GRAIL) on
291 substrates carrying 665 annotated metabolites. The comparison does not resolve into an
ordering. The verdict moves with the matching
criterion at 6 of 9 budgets, and cutting every
arm to the comparator’s own list length retires
the wide-budget leads against all but one of
the five comparators. The confound is not ours
alone; in an independent benchmark of this class
of tool, the arms that emit more score higher,
in all eleven of its drugs. We then bound what
one application of one template bank can reach,
at 0.8171 of the annotated references. Between
62.5% and 71% of the shortfall is transformation chemistry the training corpus never contains, which makes the bound a property of that
corpus rather than of any one bank.

A drug that reaches the bloodstream does not
stay the compound that was administered. Enzymes, chiefly in the liver, convert it into other
compounds carrying their own activity, toxicity
and persistence: a metabolite can be the agent
that makes a drug work, as with the antiviral
prodrugs activated by phosphorylation, or the
agent that makes one fail, as with the reactive
intermediates behind several withdrawals. The
same question arises outside the clinic, where
what an environmental chemical becomes in an
organism determines how far it travels and how
long it lasts. Knowing which metabolites will
form, as chemical structures rather than as a
count or a rate, is therefore useful early, when a
compound exists only on paper.
Two families of method now dominate. Rulebased systems, among them SyGMa 1 , BioTransformer 2 , GLORY and GLORYx 3,4 and MetaTox 5 , encode known biotransformations as reaction templates and enumerate the products of
applying them. Sequence-to-sequence systems,
among them MetaTrans 6 and MetaPredictor 7 ,
treat the problem as translation. Recent work
extends both lines: a chemical language model 8 ,
a mechanistically informed graph framework 9 ,
a server predicting pathway, site and product
together 10 , and a preprint predicting all three
jointly 11 .
1

Introduction

What is missing is not another predictor but
a way to tell these apart. The usual measure is
recall: the fraction of the metabolites recorded
for a compound that a system returns. That
fraction depends on two decisions made outside
the model. The first is what counts as a match,
since two chemical structures can denote the
same molecule while differing in how a hydrogen
is placed or how a ring is drawn. The second is
how many candidates the system is allowed to
return, since any system recovers more of the
answer if permitted to list more guesses. Published predictors differ on both and report them
inconsistently, and the difference is not small:
the ones compared here emit between 10.7 and
73 candidates per substrate by default, so a recall figure quoted for one is not on the scale
of a recall figure quoted for another. Independent evaluations of these tools report the same
obstacle 12–14 .
That a comparison can turn on an undeclared
choice is not new, and two recent statements
of it bound what this paper adds. Ash et al. 15
give a protocol for method comparison in smallmolecule drug discovery, arguing that a difference should be shown practically significant
rather than merely significant, and prescribing
how a comparison ought to be set up. Özçelik
and Grisoni 16 show what happens when it is not,
in generative design: the size of the library and
the choice of metric reorder the models. The
gap between the two is where this work sits.
A protocol tells an author what to declare; a
demonstration on generative metrics does not
transfer to recall against an incomplete annotation, where the axis that reorders methods
is how many candidates each may emit and no
analogue of it exists in distributional metrics.
What is offered here is the measurement in that
setting: the axes swept, the whole grid printed,
and a bound on what any bank mined from this
corpus could reach.
This work addresses that.
We present
GRAIL (Graph-scored Rule Application with Inspectable Localisation), a metabolite structure
predictor built from a bank of reaction templates, a learned model that selects which templates to apply, and a learned model that ranks
the resulting candidates. Every prediction it

emits carries the template that produced it and
the atoms that template acted on, so a chemist
can accept or reject a candidate on mechanistic grounds rather than on a score. How much
mechanism that carries depends on the template. 10.8% of the bank, 818 of 7,580 parseable
templates, is patterns of three atoms or fewer,
for which the named site reports where the pattern matched rather than a mechanism. Section 3.4 measures both the size of that part
and how often the named site is the site that
changed. We evaluate it with the two decisions
above promoted to declared axes: each is stated
and varied across its full range instead of being fixed silently. The comparison is against
five published predictors we obtained and ran
on the same substrates, with the two whose
knowledge is built on a source of this corpus
named and their overlap measured. One system is absent and its absence was not forced:
DeepCYP was not run for the cost of submission, its interface taking one substrate at a time
(Section S34). The comparison does not produce a winner: which system leads depends on
how many candidates it is allowed to return,
and we report that dependence rather than a
single figure. We further measure how much of
the recorded chemistry lies outside the reach
of any single template application, and bound
how much of it is chemistry the training corpus
does not contain: between 0.6253 and 0.7095,
which no template mined from that corpus could
supply.
The article is organised as follows. Section 2
describes the corpus, the three stages of the
system and the evaluation protocol. Section 3
reports the comparison against published predictors, the contribution of each stage, the sensitivity of the result to the evaluation choices, a
worked example, and the coverage bound. Section 4 discusses where the advantage lies and
what the measurements do not establish, Section 5 the limitations and Section 6 the conclusions. Procedures, ablations and supporting
measurements are in the Supporting Information.

Overview

Each design choice in this section is stated with
the measurement that decided it and the prediction it was registered as, rather than with a
pointer forward to Results: a configuration and
the evidence for it are one object, and separating
them is what lets a configuration be reported
without its evidence. Those predictions, each

the register (Table 7).
GRAIL predicts in three stages, each separately inspectable. A generator scores every
rule in a bank of 7,581 reaction templates, written as SMIRKS transformation patterns, against
the substrate. The selected rules are then applied with RDKit 2022.09.5, and their products
are standardised 21,22 and deduplicated. A template that breaks a bond returns more than
one species. Every product, and every disconnected component of it, enters the pool separately provided it has at least two heavy atoms.

as one of the references is the matching criterion.

settings: canonical SMILES equality, the full

the default, because the references neither carry

because standard identifiers normalise only a

any of these systems can be asked for: nothing in this pipeline is stereochemistry-aware, so
sulfoxidation, epoxide hydrolysis and most conjugation are predicted as constitutions and not

The attribution is what a score cannot supply.
All three phosphorylations fire at the same substrate atoms, the 5′ hydroxymethyl, under three
different rules, and the deamination fires on
the pyrimidine ring, so the output states which
transformation is claimed and where, and a candidate can be rejected on mechanistic grounds.

so the part of the bank with no expert provenance is what carries this molecule; on the drawing a chemist would use, the deamination is
found instead by a curated template, rule 251,
and at rank 32 rather than 18. Which templates
carry a molecule is itself a function of how the
molecule is drawn, and Figure 2 shows both.

Every prediction names the transformation rule
that produced it and the site at which it fired. A
candidate can therefore be rejected on mechanistic grounds instead of on a score. Predicting the
site alone is a task with its own literature and its
own tools, reactivity-based 27 and learned 28,29 ,
resting on annotation curated for the purpose 30 ;
that literature has recently had to confront how
its own labels are derived 31 . The claim here
is narrower, and it is about consistency rather
than about site prediction: the site this system
names is the site that changed.

records. Of 327 correctly predicted metabolites,
the reported site touches the atoms that actually differ from the substrate in 92.3%, and lies
wholly inside the change in 46.2% (Section S22).
The null is a connected fragment of the same
size grown at random from the same molecule,
which agrees at 35.2%. An agreement rate near
nine in ten is not the size of the effect. The
margin over that null is, and it is 0.5715.

mined, so for a mined template the two are
not independent and the agreement is partly a
self-consistency check. Split by provenance, the
124 matches carried by a hand-written template,

against 93.1% for the mined ones. The claim
therefore does not rest on the templates whose
provenance would guarantee it. The exhaustive
arm is not audited this way, because it applies
the whole bank rather than a rule budget of 30,
and the population of scored matches would be
a different one.

Choosing an operating point

The two modes are two settings of one rule budget, and which to run is decided by the budget
the service answers at rather than by a default.
Below about ten candidates the interactive mode

Beyond a short list that setting is the weaker

4.3

What the numbers do not
support

GRAIL does not lead at the tight budgets. At
k = 15 neither arm separates from the strongest
comparator, and at k = 20 the interactive arm
separates against it. The established lead is the
exhaustive arm’s, at k = 30 and 50. The system is not compared against the 2025–26 wave,
because that wave cannot be run as released: of
four such systems none ships a runnable metabolite predictor 8–11 , one of them releasing weights
whose reaction module was withdrawn, and the
Supporting Information says what each is missing. No ranking in this literature can presently
be verified independently, which is the concrete
case for leaderboards releasing per-item predictions rather than aggregate scores 34,35 . Precision
figures are bounded by annotation completeness.
And the advantage at wide budgets is partly
a statement about output length on all sides
rather than about ranking.
The argument made here about other people’s
figures was turned on this work’s own release,
and it found settings that decide a number and
were not declared. The aggregation that combines the templates reaching one candidate is
one, and a selection on validation prefers a different one. The fusion constant is a second, left
at the value the method was published with.
A rule gate the released checkpoint carries is a
third, and no figure in this paper was measured
under it. A set of candidate pools scored by a
checkpoint this repository no longer ships is a
fourth, and the drawing of the substrate and the
pool cap are two more of the same kind, both
now declared and one of them swept. Each is declared and measured here rather than described,
and a reader may take that either way: as the
discipline doing its work, or as a measure of
how easily an undeclared setting survives even
in a paper whose subject is undeclared settings.
The second reading is why the first is worth
reporting.

What is and is not distinctive

What distinguishes this system is not that it
reports a site but that the site is audited. BioTransformer’s output names the reaction and
the enzyme behind each structure it returns, and
MetaTox’s method reports a biotransformation
type and a site of metabolism 5 , so reporting one
is the norm rather than a contribution. Checking it against the atoms that actually changed,
against a null of the same size, is not done anywhere in this literature that we can find, and
Section 3.4 is that check. Ordering is not a differentiator either: three of the five comparators
order their whole output, and this system is one
more that does. What is left, and it is the property this paper rests on, is that every evaluation
choice is declared with every cell of the resulting
grid reported.

Limitations

Applying one template once is not the same as
one enzymatic step. The registered instrument
counts core-incident edits and calls a template
composite at 5 or more, above every single enzymatic turnover; it flags 15.4% of the mined
templates it can score, clearing the bar registered prediction P10 set at 13.4%. A second
instrument counting reaction-centre loci flags
8.9%, but errs in both directions with neither
error measured, so its share is neither a floor
nor a bound; the two agree on only 130 templates (Section S23). The coverage ceiling here
is therefore a one-template ceiling and not a oneenzyme one, and the composite share is why a
second application of the bank buys little: a
fifth of the mined bank already carries the compositions a second pass would have to discover.
A depth-two probe put the lift in the low percent at several times the candidate cost, and no
figure from it is quoted, because its result file
names no producer and predates the provenance
apparatus the rest of this work is held to.
Both learned stages are trained short of what
the split allows: 5,000 of 9,011 training substrates for eight epochs with early stopping that
never engaged, which is an underfitting signature and not a converged run. The spread is
measured at this configuration, so what a headline figure is read against is known; retraining
on the full split under more than three seeds,
which would say whether the level as well as the
spread moves, is not reported (Section S31).
The register is weaker than its count of confirmations suggests. Three of the six confirmed
predictions clear the falsification condition as
written, which compares a point estimate to a
threshold, and not the stricter reading that asks
the whole interval to clear it. P1 and P2 have
their threshold inside their own interval, and

Conclusions

GRAIL predicts metabolite structures by selecting templates from a bank of 7,581 reaction
rules, applying them, and ranking the products
by a reciprocal rank fusion of the generator’s
score and the filter’s. The form in which the two
learned scores are combined mattered more than
what produced them: replacing their product
with the rank fusion gained more recall than
any architectural change tested here. Every prediction carries the template that produced it
and the atoms it acted on. Its two operating
modes are two settings of one rule budget rather
than two designs, the cheap one beaten by the
incumbent beyond a short list and the wide one
returning about a hundred structures at a precision near 0.0321; which a service should run
follows from the output budget it answers at.
Measured against the published predictors
that could be run on the same substrates, the
comparison divides by output budget rather
than resolving into an ordering, and the division is the result. The verdict moves with the
matching criterion and with the budget, so a
single recall figure would have named a different
leader depending on decisions its author never
had to state. Held to the stricter test the same
argument demands, every arm cut to the comparator’s own list length on every substrate, one
of the wide-budget leads is an ordering result
and the rest are statements about emission. The
class-by-class reading says the same from the
other side: the aggregate advantage is carried by
unusual cleavages and by transformations this
