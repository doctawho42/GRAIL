\section{The corpus and its splits}

\input{si_table_splits}

The annotated substrate--metabolite pairs are assembled from four sources: ChEMBL
\citep{Zdrazil_2024}, DrugBank \citep{Wishart_2018}, MetXBioDB \citep{Djoumbou_Feunang_2019} and
the GLORYx reference set \citep{de_Bruyn_Kops_2020}. The versions used are \CorpusVersions{}.

Their licences do not combine. ChEMBL~34 is released under CC BY-SA 3.0, which requires a
derivative to carry the same or a compatible ShareAlike licence and forbids adding restrictions.
DrugBank~5.1.10 is released under CC BY-NC 4.0, which forbids commercial use. A single derived
work drawing on both cannot satisfy each: adding the non-commercial clause is a restriction
ShareAlike does not permit, and dropping it breaches DrugBank's terms. MetXBioDB~1.0 is
distributed with BioTransformer, whose terms require explicit permission for commercial use or
redistribution.

What the deposit contains follows from that, and the decision is taken here rather than deferred.
The candidate structures are produced by applying this work's rule bank and are not corpus
records, so they are released. Every corpus structure --- the substrates as well as the annotated
metabolites --- appears only as a tautomer-canonical InChIKey, which is a hash and does not carry
the structure. No source record is redistributed \emph{in the deposit}, and the licence of the
derived corpus is \CorpusLicence{}. The repository is a separate channel and ships something
narrower and deliberate: the evaluated test substrates and their annotated metabolites as
structures, which is what makes every recall figure in this paper recomputable. The question does not have to be answered, because the deposit does not raise
it. The cost is that a
reader must hold their own licences and rebuild the substrate set to join on the keys, and the
manifest is what tells them whether their rebuild matches.

\subsection{How the corpus was assembled}

The corpus files were written in a single run months before this repository's first commit, and
no script among its \numAssemblyCommits{} commits reads any of the four sources or writes the
structure files. The assembly cannot therefore be read off code. What follows separates what the
shipped files establish about it from what only their producer could, and states the second as a
bounded absence rather than as silence.

\paragraph{What each record carries.} The three splits hold \numAssemblyRecords{} structure
records, each with five fields: an identifier, an InChI, a state, a SMILES string and an index.
The identifier is the one field that could carry a source accession, and it is empty in every
one of the \numAssemblyRecords{}. Per-source counts are therefore not
recoverable from the data, and none is reported. The state field takes three values, and its
meaning is checked against the triples rather than assumed: every substrate of a positive triple
is marked as a substrate and every product of one is marked as annotated, the remaining records
being rule-applicable products carrying no annotation. The positive-unlabelled negatives this
work trains against are consequently not derived when the data is loaded. They are stored, and
which rule bank produced them is not recorded.

\paragraph{The drawing the standardisation left.} Every structure in the corpus is a fixed point
of the InChI round-trip: \numDialectTrainRtequal{} of \numDialectTrainRtof{} train records,
\numDialectValRtequal{} of \numDialectValRtof{} in validation and \numDialectTestRtequal{} of
\numDialectTestRtof{} in test are exactly what their own stored InChI reads back to, and the
\numDialectTrainRtdifferWord{} exceptions are salts whose counter-ion the SMILES has lost and the
InChI kept. That round-trip places a mobile hydrogen on oxygen, so an amide arrives as the imidic
acid and cytosine as its lactim. This is not the declared standardiser's output: run under
\numDialectRdkitversionsWord{} RDKit releases spanning four years, \texttt{standardize\_mol} returns
the amide in \numDialectRdkitamideWord{} of \numDialectRdkitversionsWord{}, from both the amide and the
imidic acid. The drawing therefore entered before the standardiser this work declares, and rules
are matched against it. Section~\ref{sec:si-presentation} treats it as the axis it is.

\paragraph{De-duplication, and the conflicts there were none of.} There is no de-duplication
code, and with the accession field empty there is no source field a conflict rule could have
arbitrated on. The files show that none was needed. \numAssemblyDuplicated{} substrate
structures appear under more than one index across the three splits, and the two annotation sets
differ in \numAssemblyDisagreementsWord{} of them; \numAssemblyBothwaysWord{}
pair in the whole corpus is listed both as positive and as negative. The effective rule is set
union when the data is loaded, applied to structures after standardisation, with no precedence
between sources because no source is recorded. This is also why the split table counts
\numAssemblyTestids{} test substrates by index and \numAssemblyTeststructures{} by structure.

\paragraph{What is inside, for the sources that can be checked.} Two of the four --- MetXBioDB
and the GLORYx reference set --- are physically present here and can be intersected with the
corpus by structure skeleton. The corpus holds \numAssemblyCorpuspairs{} distinct annotated
pairs. Of MetXBioDB's \numAssemblyMetxpairs{}, \numAssemblyMetxinside{} are inside it,
\numAssemblyMetxshare{}; of the GLORYx set's \numAssemblyGloryxpairs{} pairs over
\numAssemblyGloryxparents{} parents, \numAssemblyGloryxpairsinside{} pairs and
\numAssemblyGloryxparentsinside{} parents are. So the assembly ingested neither source
wholesale: a filter of unknown shape dropped nearly two fifths of MetXBioDB, and the measurement
bounds that filter without recovering it. What it does not do is attribute any pair to a source,
because a pair in MetXBioDB may equally have entered through DrugBank. ChEMBL and DrugBank are
not present here and admit no such measurement, so no fraction is claimed for them.

\paragraph{The partition, and its seed.} The three splits are substrate-disjoint and not
molecule-disjoint, which the leakage audit records and the main text states. The repair that
produced the shipped clean triples ran in the substrate-level mode of
\texttt{scripts/fix\_splits.py}; its molecule-disjoint mode is a different partition and was not
used. Test was passed through untouched, and its clean file is byte-identical to its input. The
three shipped clean files match the digests the split manifest declares. There is no split seed:
the repair is deterministic and draws on no random number generator, and the partition it repairs
has no producer in this repository. It is a fixed historical artifact carried forward by digest.

\paragraph{What a third party can and cannot rebuild.} With their own licences for the four
sources, a reader can obtain every source record. They cannot reconstruct the extraction and
filtering that selected from them, because that step is not in the repository and the data
carries no accession to reverse it by. They can, given the structure files, reproduce every step
from there: the standardisation is declared, the label convention is checked above, the repair is
deterministic and released, and the manifest's digests confirm a rebuild rather than merely
report that one differs. The unrecoverable step is the first one, and it is the whole of what is
missing.

The annotated metabolites of the evaluated test set are released as structures, in
\texttt{results/test\_references.json}, alongside every arm's per-substrate predictions. All five
matching criteria are therefore recomputable on the comparison population, not only the default
one: every row of Table~\ref{tab:si-criterion} can be rebuilt from what is released. An earlier
version of this section claimed otherwise, on the strength of what the Zenodo deposit holds rather
than what the repository ships.

The corpus is divided by substrate, so no substrate of one split appears in another. Molecular overlap between splits is non-zero and expected, because a molecule
that is a substrate in one split may appear as a product in another: \numLeakMolOverlap{}
molecules occur in both train and test, and \numLeakSubAsMol{} test substrates appear as molecules
in train. Both counts are recorded so that neither is later discovered and mistaken for leakage.

Three evaluation populations are drawn from these splits, and every figure in the paper names
one. The evaluated test set is the \numPopulationTestsubs{} test substrates that carry at least
one reference, holding all \numPopulationTestrefs{} references of the split; the substrates it
excludes, \numSplitTestSubstrates{} minus \numPopulationTestsubs{} of them, carry none. The
validation draw is a sample of \numValdrawCap{} substrates at seed \numValdrawSeed{}, of which
\numValdrawDeclared{} produced a candidate pool and \numValdrawPaired{} are held by both arms of a
paired comparison. The comparison set is the \numPopulationN{} substrates on which every method
predicts.

No external evaluation set is reported. GLORYx is one of the four sources this corpus is
assembled from, so its \numGloryxN{} parent substrates are not external to it, and the
\numGloryxOverlap{} that fall inside train or validation are there by construction rather than by
leakage. Holding out one source would not produce an external set either, because the sources
overlap one another: MetXBioDB and the GLORYx reference set both draw on the same published
metabolism, and DrugBank overlaps both.

The same fact bounds the comparison. Rule-based comparators have no training set that could be
held out, and their rules may encode the metabolism reported in these sources: BioTransformer is
built on MetXBioDB, and GLORYx was developed against the reference set of that name. Their
knowledge overlaps our test substrates by construction. No prior benchmark in this area controls
this asymmetry, and neither do we; it is reported because a reader comparing these numbers to
published ones should know that the rule-based figures are, in this respect, optimistic.

\section{What the annotation contains}

Every recall figure in this paper is a statement about this annotation, and the paper reported the
figures at length without describing it. The census is here. Nothing in it is a model output; it
is the corpus read back.

The evaluated test set carries \numRefauditReferences{} annotated metabolites over
\numRefauditSubstrates{} substrates, \numRefauditDistinct{} of them distinct under the paper's
default key. All \numRefauditReferences{} parse and all are keyable, so no reference is dropped
before scoring. \numRefauditShared{} keys are shared by more than one substrate, which is expected:
a common metabolite of two related drugs is one molecule.

By heavy-atom count against the parent, \numRefauditSmaller{} references are smaller,
\numRefauditSame{} are the same size and \numRefauditLarger{} are larger. The median change is
\numRefauditDeltamedian{} heavy atoms and the fifth and ninety-fifth percentiles are
\numRefauditDeltalo{} and \numRefauditDeltahi{}, but the tails are long in both directions: the
extremes are \numRefauditDeltamin{} and $+\numRefauditDeltamax{}$. A bank of single-template edits
is not the natural instrument for either tail, and the coverage census in the main text is where
that shows.

Ten elements appear. Carbon, oxygen and nitrogen account for almost all of the atoms, with
sulfur, chlorine, fluorine, phosphorus and bromine behind them and iodine and selenium in single
figures. Nothing in the annotation is outside what the rule bank's chemistry addresses.

Two properties bear directly on how the recall figures should be read.
\numRefauditSelfref{} references are equal to their own substrate under the default key --- an
annotation that names a molecule as its own metabolite --- and the parent-drop convention
discards exactly those, which is why that convention's effect is negative for the substrates
concerned rather than zero. And the annotation is positive-unlabelled throughout: a pair that
does not appear is not a pair that was tested and rejected. That is why precision is reported and
is not used to order systems, and why an absent prediction is never counted as a false positive
anywhere in this paper.

\section{Sensitivity of the comparison to the matching criterion}
\label{sec:si-sensitivity}

\input{si_table_criterion}

The paper argues that a recall figure depends on evaluation choices that this literature leaves
unstated. The argument is made about published numbers, and Table~\ref{tab:si-criterion} applies
it to our own: the same comparison, the same population, the same ranking, the same parent-drop
convention, with only the criterion moving.

Ranking is settled before any key is taken, so the criterion changes what counts as a hit and
never what is ranked. The parent-drop convention is re-derived under each criterion so that it
means the same thing in every column.

The two criteria under which GRAIL never leads sit at opposite ends of strictness. Canonical
SMILES equality is the strictest of the five, and under it a hit requires that two pipelines
produce the same string for the same molecule, which is a property of their standardisation
conventions rather than of their chemistry. Tanimoto similarity of one on Morgan fingerprints is
the loosest, and it merges structures a fingerprint cannot separate, which credits every method
with hits it did not earn and credits the comparators with more of them. Neither is a defensible
default, and the paper's argument is not that one criterion is right but that the choice has to be
declared and its consequences shown.

The reference row of Table~\ref{tab:si-criterion} reproduces the verdicts of the main text's
comparison exactly, though it is computed by a different script from the same frozen predictions.
The number of references differs by one between criteria, at \numPopulationReferences{} for three
of them and one fewer for two, and the two cases have different causes. Under the first block of
the InChIKey, the thiol and thiolate annotated for butyrylthiocholine merge, because that block is
the skeleton hash and carries neither the protonation layer nor stereochemistry; the full InChIKey
separates them in its final character. Under Tanimoto similarity of one, two of the carboxychromanol
metabolites of $\alpha$-tocopherol merge, because they differ by a single isoprenoid unit in the
side chain and a Morgan fingerprint of fixed radius counts the substructures a repeating unit
contains without counting how many times it repeats. Both
merges are the criterion acting as declared rather than a defect, and both are the reason the
count is reported per criterion rather than once.

\section{How the substrate is drawn}
\label{sec:si-presentation}

This paper sweeps five ways of judging whether a prediction is a hit and nine ways of deciding how
many predictions to make. It did not say how the substrate is drawn. That is a third choice of the
same kind, it is not neutral, and it decides which rules can fire at all.

\paragraph{The corpus speaks one dialect.} Every structure in the corpus is a fixed point of the
InChI round-trip, and the round-trip places a mobile hydrogen on oxygen. An amide is therefore
stored as the imidic acid, $\mathrm{R\text{-}C(=O)\text{-}NH\text{-}R'}$ arriving as
$\mathrm{R\text{-}C(OH)=N\text{-}R'}$, and cytosine as its 4-imino-2-hydroxy lactim. Across the
evaluated test set, \numDialectEvalMoved{} of \numDialectEvalN{} substrates are not fixed points
of the declared standardiser and \numDialectEvalImidic{} carry an imidic-drawn amide. The same
holds split by split, counted over the distinct structures each split marks as a substrate rather
than over the substrates that carry a clean triple, which is the population
Table~\ref{tab:si-splits} counts: \numDialectTrainMoved{} of \numDialectTrainN{} in train,
\numDialectValMoved{} of \numDialectValN{} in validation, \numDialectTestMoved{} of
\numDialectTestN{} in test. Every one of the \numDialectEvalMoved{} moves is a pure tautomer move,
invisible to this paper's default matching criterion: the drawing changes which rules fire and
does not change what molecule it is.

\paragraph{The bank learned that dialect.} Mining reads these pairs, so the templates inherit the
drawing. Counting templates by what their reactant side requires, the mined templates ask for an
imidic reactant \numDialectBankMinedimidic{} times against \numDialectBankMinedamide{} for a real
amide: in the portion of the bank this work built, the unpopulated tautomer of the amide is the more
frequently required of the two. The curated collections, written by chemists, run the other way,
\numDialectBankCuratedamide{} amide against \numDialectBankCuratedimidic{} imidic, and not one of
SyGMa's \numSygmaRules{} templates requires the imidic form. Amide hydrolysis and amide
$N$-dealkylation are core phase-I chemistry, so this is not a corner of the bank.

\paragraph{What it does to the worked example.} Gemcitabine is stored as the lactim. On that
drawing the interactive mode finds \numCaseInterFound{} of \numCaseReferences{} annotated
metabolites, and the one it finds is produced by mined template \numCaseInterRulehit{}, whose
reactant side requires the exocyclic imine and which cannot fire on the drug as a chemist draws
it. Presented with the 4-amino-2-oxo structure instead, the interactive mode finds
\numCaseInterdrawnFound{} of \numCaseReferences{}. The exhaustive mode still finds all
\numCaseExhdrawnFound{}, at ranks \numCaseExhdrawnRanks{} against \numCaseExhRanks{} on the stored
drawing, and the deamination it had attributed to a mined template is found instead by curated
template \numCaseExhdrawnDeamrule{} at rank \numCaseExhdrawnDeamrank{}. Both drawings are reported
in Section~\ref{sec:si-case} for that reason.

\paragraph{The arms did not all meet the same drawing.} Three of the five arms of the comparison
were handed the substrate exactly as the corpus stores it: both modes of this system, and SyGMa
and MetaPredictor. MetaTox was not. Its submission file re-tautomerises the substrate to the
natural form for \numDialectMetatoxretaut{} of the \numDialectMetatoxn{}, on the stated ground that sending an unnatural
imidic acid to an external service would be unfair to that service. The reasoning is right and the
asymmetry it leaves is real, and it runs against the arm that took the trouble: MetaTox met the
drawing its rules expect and SyGMa, whose published templates are written entirely in the amide
notation, did not.

\paragraph{What it does to the comparison.} The axis is swept like the others. Both GRAIL arms
were rebuilt on the identical substrates, presented as the declared standardiser draws them, with
the same checkpoints, the same rule budgets, the same pool cap and the same ranking; the pools
stay keyed by the corpus string, so the annotation is one annotation and the two drawings are
paired substrate by substrate. Table~\ref{tab:si-dialect} gives the paired effect on each arm at
each budget. The largest anywhere is \numDsweepExhLargest{} for the exhaustive mode and
\numDsweepInterLargest{} for the interactive one; \numDsweepExhSeparatingWord{} of
\numDsweepExhBudgetsWord{} cells separate from zero for the first and
\numDsweepInterSeparatingWord{} of \numDsweepInterBudgetsWord{} for the second.

Two properties of that measurement should be stated before it is read. The stored column
reproduces the comparison table of the main text at every one of its cells, which is the check
that the sweep measures the same quantity and not a near neighbour of it; and pointing both
dialects at the same pools returns exactly zero everywhere with every interval collapsed, which is
the check that the pairing is right. A sweep that could not show a non-difference as zero would
hide its own defect inside a plausible number.

Carried to the verdicts, the drawing moves \numDsweepMoved{} of \numDsweepCells{}
arm-against-comparator cells, and none of them is a lead the paper claims: the exhaustive mode's
leads at $k=30$ and $k=50$ and SyGMa's at $k \le 5$ stand under both drawings. The coverage
ceiling on this population is \numDsweepCeilingstored{} under the stored drawing and
\numDsweepCeilingdrawn{} under the standardiser's, a difference of $\numDsweepCeilingdiff$,
$95\%$ CI $[\numDsweepCeilinglo, \numDsweepCeilinghi]$, which does not separate from zero. It is
measured here rather than on the full evaluated split because the uncapped exhaustive pool exists
in both drawings for this population and not for that one.

The three remaining comparators are held at the corpus drawing, because MetaTox is a web service
and MetaPredictor a frozen delivery and neither can be re-run here. A difference against them
therefore measures how much of our own margin was the drawing, which is the question that matters
for reading the comparison.

\input{si_table_dialect}

SyGMa is the one comparator that can be re-run, so what that cost it is measured rather than
supposed. Given the substrate as the declared standardiser draws it, on the same \numSygdialN{}
substrates and against the same annotation, its micro recall rises at every budget above the
first: by $+\numSygdialDiffOneFive$ at $k=15$, $95\%$ CI
$[+\numSygdialLoOneFive, +\numSygdialHiOneFive]$, $+\numSygdialDiffThreeZero$ at $k=30$ and
$+\numSygdialDiffFiveZero$ at $k=50$. \numSygdialSeparating{} of the \numSygdialBudgets{}
intervals exclude zero, and they are the four widest budgets --- which are the budgets at which
the main text claims a lead over SyGMa. The corpus's drawing was costing the comparator recall,
and the amount is now on the record. It does not reverse the comparison: the exhaustive mode's
lead over SyGMa at $k=30$ is several times this correction. It does mean the lead is over a SyGMa
handed a drawing its rules were not written for, and a reader should subtract this before reading
it.

\section{The rule bank}

The bank holds \numBankRules{} templates spanning \numBankTypes{} distinct reaction types, of
which \numBankParses{} parse under RDKit 2022.09.5. It is the deduplicated union of three curated
collections, a fourth that was carried as curated and is a second machine extraction, and the
templates mined from the training split alone, which is disjoint by substrate and not by molecule.
The paragraphs below count them.

The two halves are counted rather than described, and the split between them turns out to be
drawn in the wrong place. \numCuratedTotal{} templates are nominally curated and
\numMinedRules{} mined. Three curated collections ship with the code:
\texttt{data/smirks.txt} (\numCuratedHydroxylation{}), \texttt{data/merged\_smirks.txt}
(\numCuratedMerged{}) and \texttt{resources/notebooks\_rules.txt}
(\numCuratedNotebooks{}), contributing \numAttribNamed{} distinct rules to the bank after
deduplication. The remaining \numAttribUnattributed{} were carried as unattributed because no
file in the repository was known to hold them.

They are not curated. Three independent marks identify them, and all three agree.
\numAttribPlaced{} of the \numAttribUnattributed{} are verbatim lines of a file whose one
commit in the history describes it as extracted reaction rules. They are written in element
notation with multi-component reactant groups, like that file and unlike every hand-written
collection here. And they carry the corpus's imidic drawing at the rate the mined templates do,
\numAttribUnattributedimidic{} against \numAttribMinedimidic{}, where the named expert
collections carry it at \numAttribNamedimidic{} --- a factor of thirty-seven, and the
fingerprint of a template extracted from these annotated pairs rather than written by a chemist.
The bank therefore holds two machine extractions and one curated body, in two different template
notations, and the curated body is \numAttribNamed{} templates rather than \numCuratedTotal{}.
The published source and licence of the three named collections is \CuratedProvenance{}.

One further fact about the curated collections bears on how the comparison in the main text is read.
\numSygmaInside{} of SyGMa's \numSygmaRules{} published rules are present in this bank
verbatim: \numSygmaSharePct{} of SyGMa's rule set, and \numSygmaOfcuratedPct{} of the
\numCuratedTotal{} templates nominally carried as curated.
All \numSygmaIncurated{} fall among the named collections and \numSygmaInmined{} in the mined
half, so the containment is inherited from an expert collection rather than rediscovered by
mining. Absorbing a published rule set is ordinary practice and is not the point. The point is
that the two banks in the comparison are not independent objects, and the reader is entitled to
know it before reading the result: SyGMa's lead at the tightest budgets is a ranking result over
chemistry the two systems largely share, and this work's advantage at the wider ones is that
shared chemistry plus the \numBankRules{} minus \numSygmaInside{} further templates around it.
The \numSygmaOutside{} SyGMa rules that are absent are the second half of the same fact and are
not claimed as coverage.

Mining locates a ring-aware maximum common substructure for each annotated substrate--product
pair. Atoms whose element, formal charge, hydrogen count, aromaticity or degree changed, atom
pairs whose bond order changed, and leaving or entering atoms are flagged as the reaction centre.
The centre is expanded by one bond and emitted as a mapped SMIRKS template. A template survives
only if applying it to its own source substrate regenerates the annotated product after
canonicalisation, and a selectivity filter rejects templates producing more than 200 distinct
products on average across a 50-substrate sample.

That bar bounds how unspecific a template may be; it does not make the bank specific, and the
distribution is worth reporting beside it. Counting the atoms on each template's reactant side,
\numRsizeLetwo{} of \numRsizeParsed{} parseable templates, \numRsizeLetwosharePct{}, have two
atoms or fewer and \numRsizeLethree{}, \numRsizeLethreesharePct{}, have three or fewer. A
template of that size matches almost anywhere it can match at all, so for those the site
discrimination is supplied by the learned stages rather than by the template --- which qualifies
the claim that a prediction is rule-attributable, without withdrawing it: the rule and the firing
atoms are still reported, and what is weaker is the rule's own selectivity. The tail is not
marginal in practice: \numRsizeCasetop{} of the twenty highest-ranked candidates in the worked
example come from templates with three reactant atoms or fewer.

Support across mined templates is heavily skewed: \numMinedSingleton{} of \numMinedRules{} rest on
a single training pair. Re-running the miner on the same split re-derives every template it
derived before and none new.

\section{Model architectures and training}

\subsection{Generator}

The generator estimates $P(r \mid s)$, the probability that rule $r$ applies to substrate $s$,
for the whole bank in one forward pass. Substrate graphs carry 16-dimensional node and
18-dimensional edge features. A three-layer GATv2 encoder ($16 \to 192 \to 256 \to 128$, batch
normalisation, ReLU, dropout $0.1$, mean-pooled readout) embeds the substrate, and a parallel
multilayer perceptron embeds a 1024-bit Morgan fingerprint, the two combined by a gated fusion.
Each rule is embedded independently by a rule-graph encoder of the same family
($128 \to 192 \to 128$) alongside a six-dimensional branch of global rule features.

The logit sums four learned terms, each with its own learned scalar: a cross-attention term in
which the rule embedding queries the substrate's atom states through single-head attention; a
global term, the cosine similarity between substrate and rule embeddings; a local term, the cosine
between the attention context and the rule; and a term reflecting whether the rule's SMARTS
matches. To these are added a per-rule bias and a frequency-prior term at weight $0.4$, the
log-odds that the rule yields a true metabolite estimated from training statistics. A penalty of
$7.5$ is subtracted for rules whose SMARTS does not match, which masks inapplicable rules before
the sigmoid.

Training is multi-label rule classification with a margin-ranking auxiliary at weight $0.25$ and
margin $0.45$. Optimisation uses Adam at $10^{-4}$ with early stopping on validation.

\subsection{Filter}

The filter scores a (substrate, candidate) pair. Substrate and candidate are embedded
independently, each through its own three-layer GATv2 encoder of the same dimensions with a
mean-pooled readout. The two 128-dimensional embeddings are concatenated with both Morgan
fingerprints, and the resulting 2304-dimensional vector is mapped through a three-layer perceptron
($\to 128 \to 64 \to 1$) to a single logit. The estimator is selectable between a non-negative
positive-unlabelled risk on logits and binary cross-entropy on probabilities; the released
configuration uses the second.

A variant encoding one merged substrate--product graph, with cross-edges placed by an
element-aware maximum-common-substructure correspondence, was implemented and measured. It loses
on accuracy (recall@15 $0.357$ against $0.366$, paired difference $-0.0090$, 95\% CI
$[-0.017, -0.001]$, $n = 1{,}170$) and on cost ($34.0$~ms against $9.1$~ms per pair), and is not
deployed.

\subsection{The objective is positive-unlabelled}

Rules that match the substrate but carry no annotation are down-weighted by half rather than
treated as negatives. An annotated metabolite is a positive and a rule-applicable product without
an annotation is unlabelled, because a missing annotation does not establish that a transformation
does not occur \citep{Jain_2016}. The consequence for the reported metrics is that precision is
pessimistic by a bounded but unknown amount, which is why the paper reports it without using it to
order systems.

\section{What a single step is}

The paper's predictions are single-step in the sense that one template is applied once. Whether
that is also one enzymatic step depends on the templates, and it is not always.

Every mined template comes from an annotated substrate--product pair, so the question can be put
to molecules rather than to templates. Two instruments do so, and neither alone is enough.

\paragraph{The first instrument: how many loci.} The reaction centre of the source pair, located
by the same maximum-common-substructure route the transformation types use, falls in more than one
piece for \numCompositeLoci{} of the \numCompositeScored{} mined templates,
\numCompositeLocisharePct{}. One mined template could not be scored, so the denominator throughout
this section is \numCompositeScored{} and not \numMinedRules{}. A centre in two pieces is one event when a bond appears or disappears
between them, which is what a cleavage or a cyclisation looks like, and two independent edits
otherwise; this instrument counts the pieces and does not separate the two cases. It has a blind
spot in the other direction as well, and the blind spot is the larger problem: two edits that
share or neighbour centre atoms arrive as one piece and are counted once. This share is therefore
a floor. An earlier version of this paper called it a bound, which it is not.

\paragraph{The second instrument, and its bar, fixed first.} The remedy is a second lens with no
threshold chosen after the fact, so the threshold was registered as H16 before the instrument was
written. On the same pair, let \emph{core} be the matched atoms and count $E$: every bond between
two core atoms whose presence or order differs, plus every attachment point at which a substituent
is gained or lost, each once. Bonds internal to an added or removed fragment are not edits to the
substrate and are not counted, so a glucuronide costs one and not twelve. The cut is set from
chemistry rather than from a histogram: a conjugation breaks one bond and forms one, $E=2$; an
epoxide hydrolysis reaches $E=3$; an aromatic hydroxylation through the arene oxide within one
enzyme reaches $E=4$ at worst. Nothing one enzyme does in one turnover reaches five, so the
threshold is $E \ge \numCompositeThreshold{}$.

\paragraph{What the two find together.} The second instrument flags \numCompositeEdits{}
templates, \numCompositeEditsharePct{}. The two agree on only \numCompositeBoth{}, which is the
point of having both: each sees most of what the other misses. Their union is
\numCompositeUnion{} templates, \numCompositeUnionsharePct{} of the mined templates. The registered bar
was \numCompositeBarPct{} and the prediction is confirmed. The composite share of the mined bank is
therefore about a fifth, not the \numCompositeLocisharePct{} one instrument reports, and both counts
are reported here so a reader can see how much of the answer is the instrument.

Inspection of the flagged pairs finds both kinds. Among the composites are a pair whose product
carries two new carbonyls on one piperazine ring, a pair in which a ketone is reduced and a
pyridine nitrogen oxidised, and a pair gaining sixteen heavy atoms as a glutathione conjugate
after a substitution. None of these is one enzyme acting once.

The consequence for the paper is confined and stated where it bites: the coverage ceiling is a
one-template ceiling. A rule that performs two edits can reach a metabolite two steps away in one
application, so the ceiling is not the reach of one enzymatic step, and the depth-two experiment
in the Limitations measures a second template application rather than a second enzymatic step. The
corrected share also explains that experiment's small effect. If a fifth of the mined bank
already performs two edits in one application, as P10 finds, a second application has little left
to discover that the first did not already reach, and the observed lift of about $0.012$ is what
that predicts rather than a surprise.

An earlier attempt read the templates themselves rather than the pairs and was abandoned: a
cleavage whose bridging atom carries no map number is invisible to a comparison of mapped bonds,
and a cyclisation joins two distant loci in a single event. Parsing partially mapped SMIRKS is the
wrong instrument for this question and its answer disagreed with what the molecules show.

\section{The registered predictions}

The register is released in full at \texttt{github.com/doctawho42/GRAIL}, with every prediction as
it was written, the threshold and falsification condition fixed before the check, and the outcome
as it was measured. Sixteen predictions are registered. Ten are reported in the paper as P1 to
P10; the register's own identifiers are given beside them in the main text, since its numbering
records when each was written rather than where it appears.

P10 is the newest and its order is checkable rather than asserted. Its threshold, its population
and its falsification condition were committed to the register in a commit of their own, and the
instrument that measures it was written afterwards in a later commit. The two commits are in the
released history, in that order, so a reader need not take the sequence on trust.

One weakness of the register is ours to state rather than a reader's to find. Only P10 fixes its
adjudicating population as part of the pre-specification. The nine before it name a threshold and
a falsification condition and leave the population implicit, and three of them --- P5, P6 and P7
--- were in the event adjudicated on the comparison set, which is derived from the test split,
while the rest were adjudicated on validation. Table~\ref{MS-tab:hyp} records the population each was checked on
so the reader can see which is which, but recording it after the fact is not the same as fixing
it before, and for those three it was not fixed before. The register's later entries do fix it.

The remaining six concern work this paper does not report, and they are named here by their
register identifiers so that a reader can check none has quietly left: H1, a type-indexed rather
than rule-indexed label space; H2, reactivity and accessibility node features; H3, learned
abstention; H4, a ranked prediction of intervention sizes; H5, the same null out of sample; and
H6, a negative control on graph automorphism. Each is stated with its threshold so that a later
result on any of them cannot be a threshold chosen after the fact. None is reported in this paper
because none was run for it, which is a different thing from a prediction that was run and did

\subsection{Ties in the deployed ranking}

The deployed configuration caps the pool by rule score before the fusion sees it. On a pool
smaller than the cap that truncates nothing, but it fixes the order the fusion reads, and that
matters because the fusion has exact ties: for 14 of the \numPopulationN{} substrates the two
best candidates receive identical fused scores, so which of them is returned first is decided by
the order the pool arrives in rather than by the ranking rule. Fusing the pool as stored instead
of after the cap moves micro recall at $k=1$ from \numSelLearnedOne{} to $0.1068$, a
difference of $0.0015$. Every arm of every ablation here uses the deployed convention, so the
differences reported are differences of method and not of tie-breaking.

\section{When a ranking comparison can measure anything}

Two experiments in this work asked whether a ranking beats an alternative ordering of the same
pool, and the first of them could not have answered. It compared the product ordering against
Tanimoto similarity on a pool whose median size is \numSimOldMedianPool{} candidates, at a budget
of 15. For \numSimOldPoolUnderBudget{} of \numSimOldN{} substrates the entire pool fits inside
the budget, so every ordering returns the same set and no arm can separate from another. The
measured difference, $+\numSimOldGap$ with the interval covering zero, is what that design is
able to produce and not evidence that the two orderings are equivalent.  % no-claim: derivation

The same limit governs a null model of the form used elsewhere in this literature, a uniform draw
of $\min(k, N)$ from a method's own pool of $N$.  % no-claim: derivation Its lift tends to one as the budget approaches
the pool, so at $k=15$ a method emitting about eleven candidates has a lift of $1.055$ whatever
its ranking is, while a method emitting eighty still has room. Comparing methods by lift at a
fixed budget therefore ranks them partly by how much they emit, which is the same confound the
budget sweep in the main text is designed to expose.  % no-claim: derivation

The rule both cases give is simple and we state it because neither experiment stated it in
advance: a ranking comparison carries information only over the substrates whose pool exceeds the
budget, and the number of such substrates should be reported with the result.  % no-claim: derivation The comparison in
the main text is run on the deployed pool, capped at \numCaseExhCandidates{}, where the budget is
narrower than the pool for every substrate.

\section{The choice of evaluation population}

The comparison set is a quarter of the evaluated test split and not a random quarter, since it is
fixed by which substrates every method returns predictions for. A separate analysis re-ran three
methods, four metrics and five criteria on two populations of one split, the 150 substrates shared
with a further comparator and the full \numPopulationTestsubs{}, and asked whether the ordering
of methods changes with the population. Of 60 pairwise comparisons, 10 reverse in point estimate,
no population-by-method interaction separates from zero, and none survives a Holm correction. The
design detects an interaction of $0.0661$ at 80\% power, so it excludes large population effects
and not moderate ones. Two populations is a weak design and we do not offer it as a general
claim; it is reported because the alternative is to say nothing about a population the reader can
see is not random.

\section{Runtime methodology}

Table~\ref{MS-tab:modes} prints ``did not finish'' for the exhaustive mode's slowest substrate and
never gives the rate, in a paper that says a service must publish the tail as well as the median.
The rate is \numCostUnfinished{} of \numCostSampled{} sampled validation substrates,
\numCostUnfinishedsharePct{}, at a deadline of \numCostDeadline{} seconds. It is a
validation-timing figure at that deadline and not a property of the comparison: on the test split
the same arm records \numScoredFailed{} failures over \numScoredSubstrates{} substrates, so the
comparison population is unaffected.

Every timing in the paper is a cold measurement. A cache makes a repeated substrate run 16 to 47
times faster, so any timing not taken cold measures a mixture of two regimes.

Standardisation is the binding constraint on the generator, at 94 to 99\% of cold generator time.
Three other candidates were tested against it and rejected by measurement: the pairwise filter's
maximum-common-substructure step, which does not bind because the generator outruns the filter a
hundred to one; rule application, which applies all \numBankParses{} parseable templates to a
109-atom molecule in $5.2$~s, yielding 20{,}655 products at no more than $0.58$~s per rule; and
substrate size as an axis, which fails as a predictor because two 43-atom substrates take
$64.7$~s and no completion respectively.

Two of the registered predictions address the constraint. Moving standardisation to surviving
candidates alone (P8) reduces enumeration by a factor of $13.9$, but standardising the hundred
survivors then becomes 72\% of the new arm, so the whole arm gains only $2.95\times$ against a
registered $10\times$: removing 94 to 99\% of the work does not remove 94 to 99\% of the time.
Adding a tautomer enumeration budget of \numHOneFiveBudget{} passes both halves of its
prediction.

That budget lives in a module-level singleton shared with the tautomer matching key, so setting
it globally would silently move what counts as a hit. It is applied through a private enumerator
instead: the global setting is untouched, and matching keys on 96 molecules are identical with and
without it.

\section{Comparators}

SyGMa 1.1.0 is taken from the installed module and run with the scenario
\texttt{[[phase1,\,1],}\allowbreak\texttt{[phase2,\,1]]}, one cycle of each ruleset, which is
the worked example in that version's own documentation. It is stated because it is the one comparator knob that sets how many candidates a
system may emit, and a paper arguing that such a knob must be declared should declare it.
MetaTox and MetaPredictor predictions are frozen per substrate and released with the code.

GLORYx is not a column here, and the reason is the rule this paper wrote for itself rather than
an oversight. Its reference set is one of the four sources this corpus is assembled from, so its
development data sits inside our corpus; Section~\ref{MS-sec:populations} states that such
overlap flatters the comparator, and we decline to take a comparison that our own criterion says

tool class \citep{Gao_2026}, and its absence would otherwise be conspicuous.

BioTransformer is pinned by digest rather than by version string, because ``3.0'' names several

2023-05-25, its \numBtTemplates{} templates loaded at freeze time rather than taken from a
constant. It carries no column in the comparison, and the rule is the one that defines the
comparison set: an arm is included where it supplies ranked per-substrate predictions on the

template set rather than as a ranked predictor, and on the \numBtN{}-substrate subset shared with
a further comparator: its templates reach \numBtReach{} of the references there,
95\% CI $[\numBtLo, \numBtHi]$, against \numBankRules{} templates of ours reaching
\numBtOurreach{} on the same substrates. A reach figure and a ranked recall are not the same quantity and
we do not place them in one table.

Four systems published in 2025--26 could not be run on our split as released. LAGOM
\citep{Larsson_2025} releases training code under Apache-2.0 but no fine-tuned checkpoint, so
comparing would mean comparing against our own reimplementation. DeepMetab \citep{Zhou_2025}
releases weights and site prediction but its reaction module was withdrawn by the author for
copyright reasons, leaving only Python 3.7 bytecode. DeepCYP \citep{Zhou_2026} offers a web form
and no models or API, so a comparison would mean submitting \numPopulationTestsubs{} substrates to
a third-party server. Metabolite-Gen \citep{Chavan_2026} is a preprint with no repository we could
find. The list is closed by a rule: a comparator that could not be obtained is reported as
unavailable with its reason, is not dropped silently, and the list is not extended after the
first run.

\section{Provenance and reproduction}

One pinned artifact needs a label rather than a correction.
\texttt{results/four\_method\_291.json} defines the comparison population and gates the MetaTox
column, and its comparator columns reproduce Table~\ref{MS-tab:sweep} exactly. Its GRAIL column
is a superseded arm --- \numFourmethodGrailFiveZero{} at $k=50$ against the deployed arms'
\numSweepBankFiveZero{} and \numSweepTrainedFiveZero{} --- and no number in this paper derives
from it; Table~\ref{MS-tab:sweep} is supplied by \texttt{deployment\_table.json}, which carries a
gate asserting the two agree on MetaTox. The consequence worth stating is that the file's own
family-wise block is computed over a family in which this system trails at every budget, so it
cannot speak to the leads the paper claims. The file now says so itself.

\numProvPinned{} artifacts are pinned. Most record the script that wrote them, the SHA-256 of
that script's source, and the commit it ran at; a sweep recomputes the hash of each producer and
refuses the artifact if that producer has changed. All \numProvPinned{} are current.

The guarantee that matters is narrower than the pinned set and is checked by a gate rather than
asserted. The numbers in this paper come from \numProvSources{} of those artifacts, established
by running the number generator with its artifact reader instrumented, so the count is of files
actually opened and not of matches in the source. Of the \numProvSources{},
\numProvSourcesunpinnedWord{} are unpinned and \numProvSourcesunverifiableWord{} carry a producer the
sweep cannot identify. \numProvSourcesinferredWord{} of them predate stamping and are verified by
recovering their producer's source from the history rather than from a recorded digest, which the
sweep labels as inferred; that is a weaker check and is counted separately for that reason.
\numProvSourcesexemptWord{} are exempt with a stated reason, each being an output of the checking
apparatus itself, which cannot verify its own inputs without circularity. The gate fails the build
if any of the first two counts is not zero, and unpinning one artifact makes it fail, which is how
we know it can.

Bringing those artifacts inside the guarantee meant re-running their producers, which is a rerun
and not a relabel, so what moved is reported. One number moved. The train split's clean triple
count is \numSplitTrainTriples{} where the committed artifact recorded three more: three records
of \texttt{train.sdf} that the RDKit version used here no longer parses, which the loader reports
and which affect no substrate, no annotated pair and no figure in this paper. Every other field of
every re-run artifact is byte-identical to what was committed, including the leakage audit's
overlap counts, the contamination audit and the population-defining comparison. That is the
evidence that the unpinned state was harmless, and it is worth having in a form stronger than an
assurance.

Two limits of the apparatus are ours to state. First, and materially: the stamp digests the
producer script's own bytes and nothing else, so a change anywhere in the package --- the code
that computes the tautomer keys, the matcher, the recall --- invalidates no artifact and trips no
gate. The split manifest pins the data and the sweep pins the producer; between them sits the
library, and it is pinned by neither. Second, and narrowly: most stamped artifacts record a dirty
working tree at write time. Verification resolves by the recorded source digest, which is
independent of the commit, so the producer is identified regardless; what the flag says is that
the surrounding tree was not clean, not that the producer is unknown.

The guarantee also covers the pinned set and not the directory: of \numProvFiles{} files under
\texttt{results/}, \numProvUnstamped{} carry no stamp and \numProvChanged{} were written by a
producer that has since changed. None of those is among the \numProvSources{} the numbers come
from, which the gate is what establishes.

A split manifest pins by digest the triple files, the substrate sets, the evaluated test set, the
rule bank and every third-party artifact, and a verify mode recomputes and names any leaf that has
moved.

Numbers reach the page through macros generated from pinned artifacts: \numProvMacrosbody{}
distinct macros in the manuscript and \numProvMacrossi{} here. The claim is not that every
number does. \numProvHandtyped{} measurements are typed by hand, most of them in this document,
and they are enumerated in the checker itself rather than folded into a wildcard --- so a number
typed by hand that is not already on that list still fails the check, and the list is the exact
statement of what remains to be wired. A checker refuses any other numeric literal in either
document, refuses any hand-typed pointer between the two documents in either direction, and
writes the three counts this paragraph quotes. Every such pointer resolves through
\texttt{xr} against the other document's own labels, so a wrong one prints \texttt{??} and
fails the build rather than compiling silently. Nine were typed by hand in an earlier version
and all nine were wrong, one of them badly enough to send a referee to the wrong table.

The train and validation candidate pools are deposited at \ZenodoDoi{}. Every substrate and
every annotated metabolite in them is replaced by its tautomer-canonical InChIKey before the
archive is built, so the deposit holds no source structure; the candidate structures beside the
keys are this work's own output and are released as they are. The transform is part of the
deposit tool and its counts are recorded in the manifest --- how many substrates went in, how
many came out, how many could not be keyed and how many keys collided --- because a silent drop
there would be a silent loss of population. The manifest also pins the SHA-256 of both the
archive and the file inside it together with the record counts, so a download can be verified
against the version used here. The tool refuses to build an archive from a pool whose substrates
were not keyed, and refuses to publish one at all without a licence: a Zenodo record cannot be
withdrawn once published, so the last gate before one exists is that it carries nothing that
cannot be redistributed.
