\section{The corpus and its splits}

\input{si_table_splits}

The annotated substrate--metabolite pairs are assembled from four sources: ChEMBL
\citep{Zdrazil_2024}, DrugBank \citep{Wishart_2018}, MetXBioDB \citep{Djoumbou_Feunang_2019} and
the GLORYx reference set \citep{de_Bruyn_Kops_2020}. The versions used are \CorpusVersions{}.

Their licences do not combine. ChEMBL~34 is released under CC BY-SA 3.0, which requires a
derivative to carry the same or a compatible ShareAlike licence and forbids adding restrictions.
DrugBank~5.1.10 is released under CC BY-NC 4.0, which forbids commercial use. A single derived
work drawing on both cannot satisfy each: adding the non-commercial clause is a restriction
ShareAlike does not permit, and dropping it breaches DrugBank's terms. MetXBioDB~1.0 is
distributed with BioTransformer, whose terms require explicit permission for commercial use or
redistribution.

What the deposited pools actually contain bears on this and is stated rather than assumed. The
substrate structures are corpus records. The candidate structures are produced by applying this
work's rule bank and are not corpus records. The annotated metabolites appear only as
tautomer-canonical InChIKeys, which are hashes and do not carry the structures. The deposit is
therefore a list of corpus substrates, hashes of their annotations, and our own predictions.
Whether that may be redistributed, and under which licence, is \CorpusLicence{}.

How the four sources were queried, filtered, standardised, de-duplicated against one another and
split is \CorpusAssembly{}. Without it a reader can obtain the sources but cannot rebuild the
triple files this work evaluates on, and the split manifest can only prove that a rebuilt corpus
differs, not how.

A second limit on rebuilding: the annotated metabolites are released as tautomer-canonical
InChIKeys rather than as structures. That key supports the default matching criterion and not the
other four, which need the structures, so a third party can reproduce the tautomer column of
Table~S4 and not the rest of it.

The corpus is divided by substrate, so no substrate of one split appears in another. Molecular overlap between splits is non-zero and expected, because a molecule
that is a substrate in one split may appear as a product in another: \numLeakMolOverlap{}
molecules occur in both train and test, and \numLeakSubAsMol{} test substrates appear as molecules
in train. Both counts are recorded so that neither is later discovered and mistaken for leakage.

Three evaluation populations are drawn from these splits, and every figure in the paper names
one. The evaluated test set is the \numPopulationTestsubs{} test substrates that carry at least
one reference, holding all \numPopulationTestrefs{} references of the split; the
\numSplitTestSubstrates{} minus \numPopulationTestsubs{} excluded substrates carry none. The
validation draw is a sample of \numValdrawCap{} substrates at seed \numValdrawSeed{}, of which
\numValdrawDeclared{} produced a candidate pool and \numValdrawPaired{} are held by both arms of a
paired comparison. The comparison set is the \numPopulationN{} substrates on which every method
predicts.

No external evaluation set is reported. GLORYx is one of the four sources this corpus is
assembled from, so its \numGloryxN{} parent substrates are not external to it, and the
\numGloryxOverlap{} that fall inside train or validation are there by construction rather than by
leakage. Holding out one source would not produce an external set either, because the sources
overlap one another: MetXBioDB and the GLORYx reference set both draw on the same published
metabolism, and DrugBank overlaps both.

The same fact bounds the comparison. Rule-based comparators have no training set that could be
held out, and their rules may encode the metabolism reported in these sources: BioTransformer is
built on MetXBioDB, and GLORYx was developed against the reference set of that name. Their
knowledge overlaps our test substrates by construction. No prior benchmark in this area controls
this asymmetry, and neither do we; it is reported because a reader comparing these numbers to
published ones should know that the rule-based figures are, in this respect, optimistic.

\section{Sensitivity of the comparison to the matching criterion}
\label{sec:si-sensitivity}

\input{si_table_criterion}

The paper argues that a recall figure depends on evaluation choices that this literature leaves
unstated. The argument is made about published numbers, and Table~\ref{tab:si-criterion} applies
it to our own: the same comparison, the same population, the same ranking, the same parent-drop
convention, with only the criterion moving.

Ranking is settled before any key is taken, so the criterion changes what counts as a hit and
never what is ranked. The parent-drop convention is re-derived under each criterion so that it
means the same thing in every column.

The two criteria under which GRAIL never leads sit at opposite ends of strictness. Canonical
SMILES equality is the strictest of the five, and under it a hit requires that two pipelines
produce the same string for the same molecule, which is a property of their standardisation
conventions rather than of their chemistry. Tanimoto similarity of one on Morgan fingerprints is
the loosest, and it merges structures a fingerprint cannot separate, which credits every method
with hits it did not earn and credits the comparators with more of them. Neither is a defensible
default, and the paper's argument is not that one criterion is right but that the choice has to be
declared and its consequences shown.

The reference column of Table~\ref{tab:si-criterion} reproduces the verdicts of the main text's
comparison exactly, though it is computed by a different script from the same frozen predictions.
The number of references differs by one between criteria, at \numPopulationReferences{} for three
of them and one fewer for two, and the two cases have different causes. Under the first block of
the InChIKey, the thiol and thiolate annotated for butyrylthiocholine merge, because that block is
the skeleton hash and carries neither the protonation layer nor stereochemistry; the full InChIKey
separates them in its final character. Under Tanimoto similarity of one, two of the carboxychromanol
metabolites of $\alpha$-tocopherol merge, because they differ by a single isoprenoid unit in the
side chain and a Morgan fingerprint of fixed radius counts the substructures a repeating unit
contains without counting how many times it repeats. Both
merges are the criterion acting as declared rather than a defect, and both are the reason the
count is reported per criterion rather than once.

\section{The rule bank}

The bank holds \numBankRules{} templates spanning \numBankTypes{} distinct reaction types, of
which \numBankParses{} parse under RDKit 2022.09.5. It is the deduplicated union of four curated
collections together with templates mined from the training split alone, which is disjoint by
substrate and not by molecule.

The two halves are counted rather than described. \numCuratedTotal{} templates are curated and
\numMinedRules{} are mined. Three of the curated collections ship with the code:
\texttt{data/smirks.txt} (\numCuratedHydroxylation{}), \texttt{data/merged\_smirks.txt}
(\numCuratedMerged{}) and \texttt{resources/notebooks\_rules.txt}
(\numCuratedNotebooks{}), contributing \numCuratedNamed{} distinct rules to the bank after
deduplication. The remaining \numCuratedUnnamed{} come from a fourth collection whose file is not
in the repository. The published source of each collection, and the licence each carries, is
\CuratedProvenance{}; a reader should treat the curated half as unattributed until it is.

Mining locates a ring-aware maximum common substructure for each annotated substrate--product
pair. Atoms whose element, formal charge, hydrogen count, aromaticity or degree changed, atom
pairs whose bond order changed, and leaving or entering atoms are flagged as the reaction centre.
The centre is expanded by one bond and emitted as a mapped SMIRKS template. A template survives
only if applying it to its own source substrate regenerates the annotated product after
canonicalisation, and a selectivity filter rejects templates producing more than 200 distinct
products on average across a 50-substrate sample.

Support across mined templates is heavily skewed: \numMinedSingleton{} of \numMinedRules{} rest on
a single training pair. Re-running the miner on the same split re-derives every template it
derived before and none new.

\section{Model architectures and training}

\subsection{Generator}

The generator estimates $P(r \mid s)$, the probability that rule $r$ applies to substrate $s$,
for the whole bank in one forward pass. Substrate graphs carry 16-dimensional node and
18-dimensional edge features. A three-layer GATv2 encoder ($16 \to 192 \to 256 \to 128$, batch
normalisation, ReLU, dropout $0.1$, mean-pooled readout) embeds the substrate, and a parallel
multilayer perceptron embeds a 1024-bit Morgan fingerprint, the two combined by a gated fusion.
Each rule is embedded independently by a rule-graph encoder of the same family
($128 \to 192 \to 128$) alongside a six-dimensional branch of global rule features.

The logit sums four learned terms, each with its own learned scalar: a cross-attention term in
which the rule embedding queries the substrate's atom states through single-head attention; a
global term, the cosine similarity between substrate and rule embeddings; a local term, the cosine
between the attention context and the rule; and a term reflecting whether the rule's SMARTS
matches. To these are added a per-rule bias and a frequency-prior term at weight $0.4$, the
log-odds that the rule yields a true metabolite estimated from training statistics. A penalty of
$7.5$ is subtracted for rules whose SMARTS does not match, which masks inapplicable rules before
the sigmoid.

Training is multi-label rule classification with a margin-ranking auxiliary at weight $0.25$ and
margin $0.45$. Optimisation uses Adam at $10^{-4}$ with early stopping on validation.

\subsection{Filter}

The filter scores a (substrate, candidate) pair. Substrate and candidate are embedded
independently, each through its own three-layer GATv2 encoder of the same dimensions with a
mean-pooled readout. The two 128-dimensional embeddings are concatenated with both Morgan
fingerprints, and the resulting 2304-dimensional vector is mapped through a three-layer perceptron
($\to 128 \to 64 \to 1$) to a single logit. The estimator is selectable between a non-negative
positive-unlabelled risk on logits and binary cross-entropy on probabilities; the released
configuration uses the second.

A variant encoding one merged substrate--product graph, with cross-edges placed by an
element-aware maximum-common-substructure correspondence, was implemented and measured. It loses
on accuracy (recall@15 $0.357$ against $0.366$, paired difference $-0.0090$, 95\% CI
$[-0.017, -0.001]$, $n = 1{,}170$) and on cost ($34.0$~ms against $9.1$~ms per pair), and is not
deployed.

\subsection{The objective is positive-unlabelled}

Rules that match the substrate but carry no annotation are down-weighted by half rather than
treated as negatives. An annotated metabolite is a positive and a rule-applicable product without
an annotation is unlabelled, because a missing annotation does not establish that a transformation
does not occur \citep{Jain_2016}. The consequence for the reported metrics is that precision is
pessimistic by a bounded but unknown amount, which is why the paper reports it without using it to
order systems.

\section{The registered predictions}

The register is released in full at \texttt{github.com/doctawho42/GRAIL}, with every prediction as
it was written, the threshold and falsification condition fixed before the check, and the outcome
as it was measured. Fifteen predictions are registered. Nine are reported in the paper as P1 to
P9; the register's own identifiers are given beside them in the main text, since its numbering
records when each was written rather than where it appears.

The remaining six concern work this paper does not report: a type-indexed rather than
rule-indexed label space, reactivity and accessibility node features, learned abstention, a
ranked prediction of intervention sizes, the same null out of sample, and a negative control on
graph automorphism. They are stated with their thresholds so that a later result on any of them
cannot be a threshold chosen after the fact.

\subsection{Ties in the deployed ranking}

The deployed configuration caps the pool by rule score before the fusion sees it. On a pool
smaller than the cap that truncates nothing, but it fixes the order the fusion reads, and that
matters because the fusion has exact ties: for 14 of the \numPopulationN{} substrates the two
best candidates receive identical fused scores, so which of them is returned first is decided by
the order the pool arrives in rather than by the ranking rule. Fusing the pool as stored instead
of after the cap moves micro recall at $k=1$ from \numSelLearnedOne{} to $0.1068$, a
difference of $0.0015$. Every arm of every ablation here uses the deployed convention, so the
differences reported are differences of method and not of tie-breaking.

\section{When a ranking comparison can measure anything}

Two experiments in this work asked whether a ranking beats an alternative ordering of the same
pool, and the first of them could not have answered. It compared the product ordering against
Tanimoto similarity on a pool whose median size is \numSimOldMedianPool{} candidates, at a budget
of 15. For \numSimOldPoolUnderBudget{} of \numSimOldN{} substrates the entire pool fits inside
the budget, so every ordering returns the same set and no arm can separate from another. The
measured difference, $+\numSimOldGap$ with the interval covering zero, is what that design is
able to produce and not evidence that the two orderings are equivalent.

The same limit governs a null model of the form used elsewhere in this literature, a uniform draw
of $\min(k, N)$ from a method's own pool of $N$. Its lift tends to one as the budget approaches
the pool, so at $k=15$ a method emitting about eleven candidates has a lift of $1.055$ whatever
its ranking is, while a method emitting eighty still has room. Comparing methods by lift at a
fixed budget therefore ranks them partly by how much they emit, which is the same confound the
budget sweep in the main text is designed to expose.

The rule both cases give is simple and we state it because neither experiment stated it in
advance: a ranking comparison carries information only over the substrates whose pool exceeds the
budget, and the number of such substrates should be reported with the result. The comparison in
the main text is run on the deployed pool, capped at \numCaseExhCandidates{}, where the budget is
narrower than the pool for every substrate.

\section{The choice of evaluation population}

The comparison set is a quarter of the evaluated test split and not a random quarter, since it is
fixed by which substrates every method returns predictions for. A separate analysis re-ran three
methods, four metrics and five criteria on two populations of one split, the 150 substrates shared
with a further comparator and the full \numPopulationTestsubs{}, and asked whether the ordering
of methods changes with the population. Of 60 pairwise comparisons, 10 reverse in point estimate,
no population-by-method interaction separates from zero, and none survives a Holm correction. The
design detects an interaction of $0.0661$ at 80\% power, so it excludes large population effects
and not moderate ones. Two populations is a weak design and we do not offer it as a general
claim; it is reported because the alternative is to say nothing about a population the reader can
see is not random.

\section{Runtime methodology}

Every timing in the paper is a cold measurement. A cache makes a repeated substrate run 16 to 47
times faster, so any timing not taken cold measures a mixture of two regimes.

Standardisation is the binding constraint on the generator, at 94 to 99\% of cold generator time.
Three other candidates were tested against it and rejected by measurement: the pairwise filter's
maximum-common-substructure step, which does not bind because the generator outruns the filter a
hundred to one; rule application, which applies all \numBankParses{} parseable templates to a
109-atom molecule in $5.2$~s, yielding 20{,}655 products at no more than $0.58$~s per rule; and
substrate size as an axis, which fails as a predictor because two 43-atom substrates take
$64.7$~s and no completion respectively.

Two of the registered predictions address the constraint. Moving standardisation to surviving
candidates alone reduces enumeration by a factor of $13.9$, but standardising the hundred
survivors then becomes 72\% of the new arm, so the whole arm gains only $2.95\times$ against a
registered $10\times$: removing 94 to 99\% of the work does not remove 94 to 99\% of the time.
Adding a tautomer enumeration budget of \numHOneFiveBudget{} passes both halves of its
prediction.

That budget lives in a module-level singleton shared with the tautomer matching key, so setting
it globally would silently move what counts as a hit. It is applied through a private enumerator
instead: the global setting is untouched, and matching keys on 96 molecules are identical with and
without it.

\section{Comparators}

SyGMa 1.1.0 is taken from the installed module. MetaTox and MetaPredictor predictions are frozen
per substrate and released with the code.

BioTransformer is pinned by digest rather than by version string, because ``3.0'' names several
builds: jar \texttt{c70cad91}, 115{,}415{,}197 bytes, upstream commit \texttt{6432cf88} of
2023-05-25, its \numBtTemplates{} templates loaded at freeze time rather than taken from a
constant. It carries no column in the comparison, and the rule is the one that defines the
comparison set: an arm is included where it supplies ranked per-substrate predictions on the
\numPopulationN{} substrates. BioTransformer was obtained and measured, but for the reach of its
template set rather than as a ranked predictor, and on the \numBtN{}-substrate subset shared with
a further comparator: its templates reach \numBtReach{} of the references there,
95\% CI $[\numBtLo, \numBtHi]$, against \numBankRules{} templates of ours reaching
\numBtOurreach{} on the same substrates. A reach figure and a ranked recall are not the same quantity and
we do not place them in one table.

Four systems published in 2025--26 could not be run on our split as released. LAGOM
\citep{Larsson_2025} releases training code under Apache-2.0 but no fine-tuned checkpoint, so
comparing would mean comparing against our own reimplementation. DeepMetab \citep{Zhou_2025}
releases weights and site prediction but its reaction module was withdrawn by the author for
copyright reasons, leaving only Python 3.7 bytecode. DeepCYP \citep{Zhou_2026} offers a web form
and no models or API, so a comparison would mean submitting \numPopulationTestsubs{} substrates to
a third-party server. Metabolite-Gen \citep{Chavan_2026} is a preprint with no repository we could
find. The list is closed by a rule: a comparator that could not be obtained is reported as
unavailable with its reason, is not dropped silently, and the list is not extended after the
first run.

\section{Provenance and reproduction}

\numProvPinned{} artifacts are pinned. Each records the script that wrote it, the SHA-256 of that
script's source, and the commit it ran at; a sweep recomputes the hash of each producer and
refuses the artifact if that producer has changed. All \numProvPinned{} are current. The guarantee
covers the pinned set and not the directory: of \numProvFiles{} files under \texttt{results/},
\numProvUnstamped{} carry no stamp and \numProvChanged{} were written by a producer that has since
changed, and those files support no number in the paper.

A split manifest pins by digest the triple files, the substrate sets, the evaluated test set, the
rule bank and every third-party artifact, and a verify mode recomputes and names any leaf that has
moved. Every number in the paper reaches the page through a macro generated from a pinned
artifact, and a checker refuses any numeric literal in the manuscript that is not on a short
allow-list of constants published elsewhere.

The train and validation candidate pools are prepared for deposit at \ZenodoDoi{}. The
deposit's manifest pins the SHA-256 of both the raw file and the uploaded archive together with
the record counts, so a download can be verified against the version used here. The upload is
held until the licence position above is settled, and the tool refuses to run without one: a
Zenodo record cannot be withdrawn once published.
